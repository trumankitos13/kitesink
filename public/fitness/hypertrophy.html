VolumeMore weekly sets build more muscle, with diminishing returnsMuscle growth rises with weekly hard sets per muscle, but each added set gives less than the one before it.BeginnerWell supported

A meta-regression of 67 studies and 2,058 participants (79% men, average age about 25) found that gains in muscle size rose as weekly set volume rose, with diminishing returns. Strength gains levelled off much sooner. The model gave a set that only trains a muscle as a helper half the credit of a direct set.

  • Add sets slowly, and only when progress at your current number has stalled.

Pelland 2026

Volume12 to 20 weekly sets per muscle is a reasonable range for trained liftersA review of trained young men found 12-20 sets per muscle per week a sensible target. Treat it as a guide, not a cap.IntermediateSome support

A systematic review of seven trials in men with at least a year of lifting compared under 12, 12-20 and over 20 weekly sets (six trials were pooled). Moderate and high volumes did not differ for the quadriceps or biceps. High volume did better for the triceps. The newer 67-study analysis found diminishing returns but no clear ceiling, so 20 is not a hard limit.

  • If you train a muscle fewer than 12 sets a week and progress is slow, test a small increase.

Baz-Valle 2022 · Pelland 2026

VolumeAlmost any structured programme beats no training, and multiple sets rank highest for sizeIn a network meta-analysis, every prescription beat no exercise, and multi-set programmes ranked highest for hypertrophy.BeginnerWell supported

The hypertrophy network pooled 119 trials and 3,364 healthy adults. All 12 prescriptions beat non-exercise controls and promoted size comparably. The highest-ranked for hypertrophy was higher-load, multiple-set, twice-weekly training. Rankings are not proof that the top option is better than the rest.

  • Choose a plan you can follow for months, with more than one set per exercise.

Currier 2023

VolumeAt extreme volumes, part of the lean mass gained was fluidWhen trained men ramped up to 32 sets per exercise a week, most of the late lean-mass gain vanished once extra water outside the cells was accounted for.AdvancedThin evidence

Thirty-one trained college-aged men did sets of 10, rising from 10 to 32 sets per exercise a week over 6 weeks. DXA lean mass rose 1.34 kg in weeks 1-3 and 0.85 kg in weeks 4-6; adjusted for extracellular water, the later gain fell to a non-significant 0.25 kg. In a companion paper, forced post hoc tests in the 15 men whose fibres grew suggested actin and myosin concentration fell about 30%. McDonald gives the load as 60% of 1RM and says the men reported about 4 reps in reserve, which he sees as far from failure. His 2018 article predates Pelland 2026, whose pooled trials show size still rising with sets.

  • Expect scale and lean-mass gains in a very high-volume block to include fluid.

Haun 2018 · Haun 2019a · Pelland 2026 · McDonald 2018d

VolumeCounting hard sets is a fair volume measure across about 6 to 20-plus repsA review of trials in trained adults found that sets taken to or near failure seem an adequate measure of training volume when each set has between about 6 and 20+ reps.IntermediateSome support

The review took 14 randomised trials of at least 6 weeks in people aged 18 to 35 with a year or more of training. It judged the number of sets to or near failure an adequate volume measure for growth across that rep span, with other variables held constant. McDonald accepts set counts on those terms, but doubts that very different rep counts are equal, and he treats a set ended about 4 reps short as borderline and anything easier as a warm-up. See the basics table on three ways to count volume.

  • When you total weekly volume, count only sets taken close to failure.

Baz-Valle 2021 · McDonald 2020g

VolumeJumping to very high volume straight awayMore sets add less each time, so a big jump from your current volume mostly adds fatigue.MistakeIntermediateThin evidence

The dose-response analysis shows diminishing returns on weekly sets. A small trial of 16 trained people found a volume built from each person's own logs beat a fixed 22 weekly sets. Coaches therefore add sets in small steps and watch whether performance holds.

  • Add 1-3 sets per muscle per week, then check performance for two weeks.

Pelland 2026 · Scarpelli 2022

VolumeCiting the retracted Barbalho trials as proof of a volume ceilingTwo trials whose titles claimed an upper threshold or ceiling for training volume have been retracted. They are not evidence either way.MistakeIntermediateWell supported

A 2019 trial in trained women and a 2020 trial in trained men, both by Barbalho and colleagues, were presented as evidence of a ceiling on useful weekly sets. Both papers have since been retracted. The men's trial was withdrawn at the authors' request after they found inconsistencies in the data. McDonald took his discussion of them out of his tension series. The current pooled evidence shows diminishing returns from extra sets, not a clear ceiling.

  • Check whether a paper has been retracted before you rely on it.

MSSE 2021 · IJSPP 2020 · Pelland 2026 · McDonald 2019i

EffortSets ended closer to failure tended to build more muscleIn a meta-regression, hypertrophy improved as sets ended nearer to failure, while strength gains were similar across a wide range.IntermediateWell supported

The analysis estimated reps in reserve (RIR, the reps left before failure) for each study from its description, so the exact shape of the curve is unclear, as the authors state. Muscle size had a clear negative slope with RIR. For strength, the intervals included zero, meaning no clear relationship.

  • End most hard sets with only a few reps left in reserve.

Robinson 2024

EffortGoing to absolute failure has not shown a clear edge over stopping just shortTraining to momentary failure was not clearly better for muscle than stopping close to it.IntermediateWell supported

A meta-analysis of 15 studies found no advantage for momentary failure (effect size 0.12, interval -0.13 to 0.37). In a trial of 18 trained people over 8 weeks, legs trained to failure or to about 1-2 reps in reserve grew the same. A 13-study review found no hypertrophy difference when volume was equal.

  • Use 1-2 reps in reserve on most sets, and save true failure for safe exercises such as machines.

Refalo 2023 · Refalo 2024 · Vieira 2021

EffortReps in reserve is a useful guide, but most lifters misjudge itRIR tracks how hard a set was, but people tend to underpredict how many reps they have left, by about one rep on average.IntermediateSome support

In 29 squatters, an RIR-based effort rating moved in step with bar speed. A scoping review and meta-analysis of 12 studies and 414 people found lifters underpredicted reps to failure by 0.95 reps on average, with wide variation. Guesses were better close to the end of a set and on sets of 12 reps or fewer. Training experience did not clearly change accuracy in the pooled data, though one study of 141 people saw a tendency to improve with experience.

  • Rate RIR on sets of about 12 reps or fewer.
  • Occasionally check yourself with one set to failure on a safe exercise.

Zourdos 2016b · Halperin 2022 · Pelland 2022 · Steele 2017

EffortThe effective-reps idea is a useful rule of thumb, not a measured lawThe idea that only the last few reps near failure drive growth fits the direction of the data. No fixed cut-off has been shown.IntermediateThin evidence

Effective (or stimulating) reps is a coaching model. It matches the finding that sets nearer failure tend to build more size. The meta-regression showed a gradual slope with estimated RIR, not a cliff, and its authors note the RIR estimates are rough. The reviews of RIR methods find they differ in accuracy, so a hard rule such as "the last five reps" is not supported.

  • Use the idea to remind yourself to make sets hard, not to count reps.

Robinson 2024 · Pelland 2022 · Halperin 2022

EffortWhere the effective-reps model stops workingThe model assumes a rep near failure is worth the same at any load and that you can tell which reps those are. Both are shaky.AdvancedThin evidence

It also leaves out exercise choice, muscle length and fatigue across sets. A trial that took sets to failure with heavier and lighter loads found similar fibre glycogen depletion in both fibre types, which fits with load mattering less at failure. Sets that stopped about 1-2 reps short still grew muscle as well as failure sets, and RIR is hard to judge exactly.

  • Treat the model as a teaching tool and judge your plan by results over months.

Morton 2019 · Refalo 2024 · Halperin 2022

EffortFailure in every session grew less than percentage training in one small trialWell-trained men who took each exercise to failure three days a week gained less fibre size than men training by percentages. McDonald describes the sets and days as matched.AdvancedThin evidence

Fifteen well-trained men lifted 3 days a week for 10 weeks, one group to failure on each exercise and one by percentages. Only the percentage group increased type I and type II fibre area in the thigh; both increased muscle thickness, with effect sizes favouring the percentage group. McDonald adds that both groups also sprinted twice a week and that the authors themselves raise a lack of recovery. His reading: raising effort without cutting volume or frequency overloads recovery. With 7 and 8 men per group it is weak evidence; see the recovery page on failure slowing recovery.

  • If you take more sets to failure, cut sets or sessions to make room (McDonald's practice).

Carroll 2019 · McDonald 2019h

EffortRep speed drops well before the end of a bench or squat set taken to failureIn bench and squat sets to failure, bar speed fell well before the last rep. McDonald's view is that when every rep moved at the same speed, the set most likely ended short of failure.IntermediateThin evidence

Thirty-six physically active men lifted as fast as they could in bench press and parallel squat sets to failure at 60 to 75% of 1RM. Average rep speed fell significantly after 34 to 40% of the reps on the bench and 48 to 69% on the squat, in the same pattern at every load. In McDonald's timings of filmed sets to true failure, the final rep lasted several times longer than the opening one. He also saw some pulling and shoulder exercises fail with little slowing, so speed is a guide, not a rule. He argues that ending a set once reps slow past a set tempo stops it short of failure.

  • On presses and squats, film your last reps; if they moved as fast as the first, the set probably stopped short of failure (McDonald's view).

Izquierdo 2006 · McDonald 2020k · McDonald 2020i

EffortEnding every set far from failureSets that stop well short of failure leave growth on the table, and self-chosen loads are often light.MistakeBeginnerSome support

The meta-regression found muscle size improved as sets ended closer to failure. People who choose their own weights average about 53% of 1RM. Sets that stop when the work first feels uncomfortable can sit many reps from failure. Many beginners find it hard to judge how many reps remain.

  • Raise the load or reps until the last rep of each working set looks slow and hard.

Robinson 2024 · Steele 2022

Load and repsLight and heavy loads build similar muscle when sets are hardMuscle growth was similar with light and heavy loads when sets were taken close to failure.BeginnerWell supported

A meta-analysis of 45 studies found similar hypertrophy for loads above and at or below 60% of one-rep max (1RM). Another of 28 studies (747 adults), using sets to volitional failure, agreed. In 49 resistance-trained men over 12 weeks, 20-25 reps at about 30-50% 1RM grew muscle as much as 8-12 reps at 75-90%.

  • Pick a load you can control through the full range and take the set close to failure.

Refalo 2021 · Lopez 2021 · Morton 2016

Load and repsHeavier loads give better strength gainsIf one-rep-max strength matters to you, train with heavier loads at least part of the time.BeginnerWell supported

The network meta-analysis found loads above 80% of 1RM maximised strength gains. Pooled results of sets to failure showed high-load training beat low-load for strength. A separate analysis found higher loads improved 1RM strength, with an effect size of 0.34. Muscle size did not follow the same pattern.

  • Include some sets in the 5-8 rep range if you also want a stronger one-rep max.

Currier 2023 · Lopez 2021 · Refalo 2021

Load and repsHalf-load sets to failure took three times as long and felt worse in one trialBack-extension sets at 50% of maximal force took about three times the reps and time of 80% sets and were rated more uncomfortable. A study at 30% found no difference.IntermediateThin evidence

Twenty-six young adults (14 men, 12 women) did one set of machine back extensions to failure each week for 6 weeks at 80% or 50% of maximal force. Strength rose alike, but the lighter sets averaged 26 reps and 159 seconds against 8 reps and 51 seconds, with discomfort rated 7.8 against 4.8. In an acute test with 8 trained men, discomfort did not differ between 30% and 80% loads. McDonald's view is that very light sets to failure earn their place mainly when a joint needs sparing or a machine stack runs out.

  • Keep very light sets to failure for simple machine exercises or for sparing a sore joint (McDonald's practice).

Fisher 2018 · Fisher 2017 · McDonald 2019g · McDonald 2016l

Frequency and splitsWith weekly sets matched, training a muscle more often adds little growthOnce total weekly sets are equal, how often you train a muscle makes little difference to size.BeginnerWell supported

The meta-regression found the effect of frequency on hypertrophy compatible with a negligible effect, and a meta-analysis of 25 studies found no growth difference between frequencies at equal volume. In 21 people given the same weekly volume over 9 weeks, two sessions a week and four sessions a week gave the same muscle and strength gains. In 24 men, training one or three times a week gave similar thigh results. McDonald's view, a practical one, is that a high weekly set count per muscle is easier to train hard when split over two or more sessions.

  • Pick the number of weekly sessions you can keep, then spread your sets across them.
  • If a muscle gets many hard sets a week, give it at least two sessions.

Pelland 2026 · Hamarsland 2022 · Neves 2022 · Schoenfeld 2019 · McDonald 2020b

Frequency and splitsFull-body and split routines give similar resultsA meta-analysis found no meaningful difference in strength or muscle size between split and full-body routines.BeginnerWell supported

Fourteen studies with 392 participants compared the two. Gains in bench press and leg strength, arm and thigh muscle area and lean mass were similar. The review could not say what happens when volume differs, so choose the layout by schedule and preference.

  • Choose the split that fits your week and that you enjoy.

Ramos-Campo 2024

Frequency and splitsMcDonald keeps hard back work off the day before squats or deadliftsHis view is that a tired upper back limits squats, deadlifts and Romanian deadlifts, so he orders a split to keep heavy pulling off the day before them.IntermediateCoaching practice

In his view, upper-back and shoulder-girdle fatigue makes the bar harder to hold in position and lets form slip on hinge and squat patterns, while leg presses and leg curls need little upper-back support and suffer less. On an upper and lower split run on back-to-back days, he puts the lower day first, since general tiredness from legs costs the upper day less. A pull day placed before legs raises the same problem. These are coaching judgments, not trial results.

  • On consecutive training days, put the lower-body day before the upper-body day (McDonald's practice).

McDonald 2015v

Frequency and splitsThe long head of the triceps also works on pulling daysIt crosses the shoulder and helps draw the arm down and back. McDonald's view is that rows and pulldowns therefore work it too, though less directly than presses.AdvancedCoaching practice

McDonald raises this as a fine point, mainly for lifters whose triceps give out first when pressing. He suggests push and pull days placed back to back could each cost the other some performance. For a push, pull and legs plan run once a week, he places the legs day between the two upper-body days. This is coaching reasoning, not a trial result. See the recovery entry on six-day splits for shoulder loading across the week.

McDonald 2015v

Frequency and splitsStarting a rotating split without checking how sessions fall over two weeksThree workouts rotated over four training days land on different weekdays each week, so a poor pairing may only show up every other week.MistakeIntermediateCoaching practice

On that rotation each muscle comes round about every fifth day, but the order of neighbouring sessions keeps changing. McDonald's advice is to draw the calendar for two weeks and look for any session followed the next day by one that needs the muscles it has just tired. He says plans that train each muscle once a week avoid most of these clashes. The recovery entry on six-day splits covers the arm day that spoils a following chest and back day.

  • Before you commit to a split, mark each day's main muscles on a two-week calendar (McDonald's practice).

McDonald 2015v

Exercises and restUse a full range of motion by default; lengthened partials also workFull range of motion is the safe default. Partial reps done in the stretched half held up well in newer trials.BeginnerSome support

A meta-analysis of 16 studies found full range beat partial range for lower-limb muscle growth (effect size 0.88) and strength. No clear difference appeared in muscle thickness overall. A 12-week trial across 15 sites (163 and 134 people in the two arms) found lengthened-partial and full-range training practically equivalent for arm and thigh size, and an 8-week trial in 13 trained people agreed.

  • Move through the full range you can control, and keep partial reps for the stretched end.

Pallarés 2021 · Gschneidner 2025 · Havers 2025

Exercises and restLoading a muscle in its stretched position grew more in several trialsExercises that load a muscle when it is long gave bigger gains than versions that load it short, in trials that compared them.IntermediateSome support

Seated leg curls grew the whole hamstrings more than prone leg curls (+14% against +9%, 20 adults, 12 weeks). Overhead cable triceps extensions beat neutral-arm ones (+19.9% against +13.9%, 21 adults, 12 weeks). In 42 young women, calf raises done in the lower, stretched half grew the medial calf more than full or top-half range. Knee angle matters for the calf too: in 14 untrained adults over 12 weeks, standing calf raises grew the lateral gastrocnemius 12.4% against 1.7% for seated ones, with similar soleus growth. The technique area covers calf raises.

  • Include one exercise per muscle that loads it stretched, such as seated leg curls or overhead triceps extensions.

Maeo 2021 · Maeo 2023 · Kassiano 2023 · Kinoshita 2023

Exercises and restRest long enough to repeat your reps; 2 to 5 minutes gave similar growth in trained menLonger rest keeps reps up across sets. In trained men, resting 2 or 5 minutes gave similar muscle gains.IntermediateSome support

A review of 35 studies found 3-5 minutes rest allowed more reps over several sets. In 13 strength-trained men, 2 and 5 minute rests gave similar muscle and strength gains over two 3-month blocks. A bench press needed 3-5 minutes to hold reps, a single-joint exercise about 2. In 12 untrained men, 2.5 minutes beat 1 minute for arm size.

  • Rest about 3 minutes between heavy compound sets and about 2 minutes between isolation sets.

de Salles 2009 · Ahtiainen 2005 · Senna 2016 · Buresh 2009

Exercises and restLift under control; very slow reps are not neededControlled reps work. Reps of 10 seconds or more are best avoided, and slowing the lowering phase had no clear extra effect on size.IntermediateSome support

An umbrella review of 14 meta-analyses suggested avoiding very slow reps of 10 seconds or more. A meta-analysis of nine studies found uncertain effects of slower lowering on hypertrophy. A review of six studies in 119 untrained people was mixed, and a tempo review notes the evidence is not clear-cut.

  • Lower the weight in about 2 seconds and lift without bouncing.

Bernárdez-Vázquez 2022 · Amdi 2025 · Hackett 2018 · Wilk 2021

Exercises and restIn a compound lift, the muscle that tires first decides when the set endsMcDonald's view: if a helper muscle gives out first, the muscle you want to grow may end the set well short of a hard effort.IntermediateCoaching practice

He argues that a multi-joint set ends when its weakest contributor gives out, and that limb proportions and the relative strength of the helper muscles decide whether the target muscle gets a hard set. He reads this as one reason some people grow well from a lift and others do not. It is coaching reasoning. With work matched, multi-joint and single-joint training changed body composition alike, and pre-exhaustion added nothing; see the basics entry on multi-joint and single-joint lifts and the exercise-order entry here.

  • If a lift never tires the muscle you want to grow, add an isolation exercise for it (McDonald's practice).

McDonald 2019j · McDonald 2019k

Exercises and restCutting the range of motion to lift heavierShortening the rep to add weight tended to give up size in the lower body. Partials in the stretched half are different.MistakeBeginnerSome support

A meta-analysis of 16 studies found full range gave larger lower-limb muscle gains than partial range. Lengthened partials, done on purpose in the stretched half with control, matched full range in a large 12-week trial. Half reps that skip the stretched part, done only to move more weight, are the mistake.

  • Lower under control to the stretched position and drive up without bouncing.

Pallarés 2021 · Gschneidner 2025

Exercises and restCutting rest so short that your reps fallVery short rests lower the reps you can do on later sets, and with it the total work.MistakeBeginnerSome support

In a trial, a 1-minute rest gave fewer total reps over five sets than 3-5 minutes on a bench press. A review of 35 studies found 3-5 minutes allowed more reps over several sets. Short rests are fine for small isolation moves, but for compound lifts they cost volume.

  • Rest long enough to repeat most of your first set's reps.

Senna 2016 · de Salles 2009

Exercises and restAssuming the exercise with more weight on the bar loads the muscle moreMcDonald's view: bar weight tracks muscle tension only within one lift for one person. Between exercises, moment arms and shared work change the picture.MistakeIntermediateCoaching practice

McDonald argues that a multi-joint lift often holds the load closer to the working joint, so the moment arm is shorter, and spreads the turning effect over several muscles. A single-joint lift for the same muscle may use far less weight held farther out, with that muscle doing nearly all the work. On his rough sums, the gap in load on the muscle being trained is much smaller than the gap in bar weight, and can even reverse. It rests on basic mechanics and his estimates; he offers no measurement.

  • Track progress by load and reps on the same exercise, not by comparing exercises.

McDonald 2019j · McDonald 2019k

Special techniquesDrop sets match straight sets for growth and can save timeDrop sets built about the same muscle as normal sets in a pooled analysis, so the case for them is time and variety.IntermediateSome support

A meta-analysis of six studies (142 adults) found both drop sets and normal sets increased muscle size, with no significant difference between them. A systematic review of advanced techniques also lists drop sets as a time-saving tool but says evidence is too thin for firm dosing guidance.

  • Add one drop set at the end of an isolation exercise when you are short on time.

Sødal 2023 · Krzysztofik 2019

Special techniquesSupersets let you do the same work in less timeSupersets gave similar volume and similar strength and size results in shorter sessions.BeginnerSome support

A meta-analysis of 19 studies with 313 people compared supersets with straight sets. Total reps and volume load were similar and sessions were shorter. Supersets felt harder and raised markers of exertion. Pairing opposing muscles kept volume up better than pairing the same muscle.

  • Pair opposing muscles, such as chest and back, when you are short on time.

Zhang 2025

Special techniquesRest-pause and cluster sets are fatigue and time tools, not growth boostersPooled reviews found cluster sets and similar set structures give about the same size and strength as straight sets.IntermediateSome support

A review of 17 studies found no set structure beat another for strength or hypertrophy, and a review of 29 studies found cluster sets matched traditional sets, possibly with less fatigue. One trial in 18 trained people found rest-pause grew thigh thickness more but not other sites.

  • Use cluster or rest-pause sets when you want heavy reps with less fatigue, not as a growth hack.

Prestes 2019 · Jukic 2021b · Davies 2021

Special techniquesBlood-flow restriction can build size with light loads, but needs careLight loads with a cuff that restricts blood flow built muscle similarly to heavy training, but built less strength.AdvancedSome support

A meta-analysis found similar hypertrophy for high-load training and low-load training with blood-flow restriction (BFR), while strength favoured high load. A position stand sets out methods and safety for practitioners. Cuff width and pressure matter, and people with vascular or clotting conditions should involve a clinician first.

  • Learn cuff width and pressure from a qualified practitioner, and ask a clinician if you have a vascular condition.

Lixandrão 2018 · Patterson 2019

ProgrammingAdding reps and adding weight both work as progressionIn new lifters, progressing by reps or by load gave the same strength and muscle gains over 10 weeks.BeginnerThin evidence

Thirty-nine previously untrained people (20 men, 19 women) had one leg progress by load and the other by reps. Leg extension 1RM and thigh muscle area rose by similar amounts in both. This was a short trial in beginners, so it may not transfer to advanced lifters. Combining the two, adding reps within a range and then adding weight, is called double progression.

  • Use double progression: add reps within a range, then raise the load and build the reps back up.

Chaves 2024

ProgrammingPut the exercises that matter most firstReps and volume are higher for an exercise placed early in a session, and pre-exhaustion gave no extra benefit.BeginnerSome support

A review of exercise order found total repetitions were higher when an exercise was done at the start of a session. A 12-week trial in 39 trained people found pre-exhaustion (isolation before compound) gave no greater benefit than putting compound exercises first.

  • Start with the lifts and muscles you most want to improve.

Simão 2012 · Fisher 2014

ProgrammingWarm up with lighter sets of the first lift; save long stretches for laterWarming up helps performance. Long static stretches right before lifting can reduce it slightly.BeginnerSome support

A meta-analysis of 32 studies found warm-ups improved performance in 79% of measured outcomes. A review found static stretches of 60 seconds or more per muscle lowered performance by 4.6%, against 1.1% for shorter holds, when tested right afterwards, and a review of 106 studies found holds under 30 seconds had no detrimental effect. A squat trial found extra heavy or fast warm-up sets gave no clear gain in total reps or volume. The training basics area covers warm-up sets and when to stretch first.

  • Do two or three lighter, increasing sets of your first lift before working sets.
  • Hold long stretches for after training.

Fradkin 2010 · Behm 2016 · Souza 2024 · Kay 2012

ProgrammingDeloads are common practice, but trials do not yet show they are neededCoaches use deloads regularly. The one small trial in beginners found cutting volume in weeks 4 and 8 neither helped nor hurt.IntermediateThin evidence

A deload is a period of reduced training stress, defined by consensus among 21 coaches who finished a Delphi study. In 246 strength and physique athletes, deloads came about every 5.6 weeks and lasted about 6.4 days. In 19 untrained men, deload weeks did not change muscle growth over 8 weeks. Experienced lifters were not tested.

  • Plan a lighter week about every five or six weeks, or sooner when performance drops for several sessions or joints ache.

Bell 2023 · Rogerson 2024 · Pancar 2026

ProgrammingPeriodising your training helps strength more than muscle sizeWhen volume was equal, planned changes in volume and load helped strength but not muscle size.IntermediateWell supported

A meta-analysis of 35 studies found periodised training had a small advantage for 1RM strength over non-periodised training. Hypertrophy did not differ between periodised and non-periodised, or between linear and undulating, plans. Undulating beat linear plans for 1RM only in trained people.

  • Vary rep ranges across the week if it keeps training interesting, but do not expect it to change size.

Moesgaard 2022

ProgrammingMuscle size gains in the first weeks partly reflect swellingMeasured size rises in the first weeks, but some of that early gain is swelling from training stress, not new muscle tissue.BeginnerSome support

In 25 sedentary men, thigh muscle area rose 9.6% over 8 weeks, and the authors judged real hypertrophy likely appeared around weeks 3-4. Other researchers found early increases came with swelling markers, and a review argues true hypertrophy appears after roughly 18 sessions. The timing is debated.

  • Judge progress over months, not the first three weeks.

DeFreitas 2011 · Damas 2016a · Damas 2018

ProgrammingHow to read a training study before you act on itCheck who was studied, for how long, how muscle was measured and how failure was defined before copying a result.IntermediateCoaching practice

Most volume and frequency data come from short trials in young people: in the 67-study set, the average age was about 25 and 79% were men. Studies also define failure differently and often do not report reps in reserve. A single trial is weak evidence next to a meta-analysis, and an interval that includes zero means no clear difference.

  • Ask who was studied and for how many weeks before applying a finding.

Pelland 2026 · Pelland 2022

ProgrammingAdd weight only while form and range hold; drop it when they slipMcDonald's view: a heavier weight raises tension on the target muscle only if the lift still looks the same, and a lighter weight done properly can load it more.BeginnerCoaching practice

McDonald's view is that weight on the bar stands in for muscle tension only while technique stays constant. If a jump brings leaning, heaving, bouncing or a shorter range, the target muscle may get no more tension, or less. Taking weight off until the lift is controlled through the full range can raise tension on the target muscle even though the load is lower. He applies the same check to adding sets or cutting rest. For range of motion, see the entry on cutting the range to lift heavier.

  • Keep a new weight only if reps, range and form match the old one.
  • If form has drifted, lower the load and build it back up (McDonald's practice).

McDonald 2019k

ProgrammingPast the first years, expect to add load every few weeks, not every weekMcDonald's view: beginners can add weight most sessions, largely from learning the lift, while after two or three years a jump every 4 to 6 weeks is realistic.IntermediateCoaching practice

He assumes working sets already end within 2 to 3 reps of failure. On that basis, once gains slow, a hard enough load stays a growth stimulus for weeks, and an advanced lifter need not add weight or sets every week. His check: about every fourth week, take the last set of a lift to failure where that is safe. If you clearly beat the target, such as 12 reps with a weight chosen for 8, add weight next session. Lifters who judge reps in reserve well can instead add weight when a set planned at 1 to 3 in reserve feels like 4 or more. For the basic rule of adding weight at the top of a rep range, see the basics page.

  • Every few weeks, test the last set of a lift for reps where failing is safe (McDonald's practice).

McDonald 2019k

ProgrammingMcDonald pairs heavier sets with lighter, short-rest sets for the same muscleHis hypertrophy plans open each muscle with a few heavier sets, then add higher-rep sets on short rests. It is his practice, not a tested rule.IntermediateCoaching practice

McDonald's view is that the heavy sets supply tension, which he links to contractile protein, and the light work supplies fatigue, which he links to fluid, stored fuel and other non-contractile parts of the fibre. He says older powerbuilding plans had the same shape. Pooled trials of sets taken to failure found no difference in growth between light, moderate and heavy loads (see the light-and-heavy-loads entry). No trial cited here tests the pairing against either alone. Those pooled trials did find more strength with heavier loads, which the heavy sets cover.

  • If you want a stronger 1RM as well as size, start each muscle with a few heavier sets (McDonald's practice).

McDonald 2019c · McDonald 2007h · Lopez 2021

ProgrammingA spell of low-rep strength work may raise the loads you later use for sizeMcDonald's view is that a phase of heavy sets of five or fewer builds strength that lets you use more weight in later higher-rep work.IntermediateCoaching practice

Loads above 80% of 1RM gave the largest strength gains in a network meta-analysis (see the heavier-loads entry). McDonald argues that much of that strength is neural, and that a stronger lifter then puts more tension through the muscle in later hypertrophy blocks. He describes his own scheme of four training zones and how to rotate them in his 2007 series. No trial cited here tests this sequence, and periodised plans helped strength, not size, in pooled trials (see the periodisation entry).

  • Before your first block of sets under five reps, spend at least six weeks on sets of about four to six (McDonald's practice).

McDonald 2007i · McDonald 2007j · Currier 2023

ProgrammingMcDonald: a plan that raises volume, effort or frequency must give ground on anotherMcDonald's view is that well-known hypertrophy plans differ mainly in which of the three they push, and end up in a similar place over a couple of years.IntermediateCoaching practice

One popular plan trains each muscle often with mostly submaximal sets; another trains each muscle every fifth day or so with sets driven past failure; his own sits between them on frequency and effort, with slightly more work per session than either. He judges that any of them, run for two years with steady load progression and enough food, would give similar results. In a network meta-analysis, 12 prescriptions built muscle comparably, but they varied load, sets and frequency, not effort, and multi-set plans ranked highest (see the any-programme entry). He chooses between plans by temperament and recovery.

  • Pick a plan whose effort per set, session length and frequency you will keep up for months (McDonald's view).

McDonald 2008a · Currier 2023

ProgrammingRepeating the same weights and reps with no logWithout a record, you cannot tell whether you are adding reps or load, which is what progression means.MistakeBeginnerCoaching practice

In 39 beginners, adding load and adding reps each raised strength and thigh muscle area over 10 weeks. Both methods need you to know last week's numbers. Writing down weight, reps and effort for each set takes little time and makes stalls easy to spot.

  • Log weight, reps and reps in reserve for every working set.

Chaves 2024

RecoveryShort sleep lowers lifting performanceSleep loss lowered exercise performance in a large meta-analysis, and one small study found lower muscle protein synthesis after a sleepless night.BeginnerSome support

A meta-analysis of 69 publications defined sleep loss as 6 hours or less in 24 and found an average 7.56% drop in performance. A review of 17 studies found restricted sleep reduced force on compound lifts. In 13 adults, one night without sleep cut muscle protein synthesis by 18%. Long-term growth effects were not tested.

  • Protect regular, full nights of sleep, especially before heavy sessions.

Craven 2022 · Knowles 2018 · Lamon 2021

RecoveryCurls tired the elbow flexors more, and for longer, than rowsIn 16 highly trained men, 8 hard sets of preacher curls cut elbow-flexor strength more and for longer than rows did. That fits giving a row only part credit for the arms.IntermediateThin evidence

Sixteen highly trained men did 8 sets of 10RM one-arm seated rows with one arm and preacher curls with the other. Ten minutes after, elbow-flexor strength was down 26.8% after curls and 15.1% after rows. At 24 hours it was back to baseline after rows but still 8.4% down after curls, and soreness lasted longer after curls. McDonald recalls a similar comparison, pulldowns against curls, to argue that a compound set is not a full biceps set. The dose-response analysis on this page reached a half-credit rule from growth data.

  • Allow the biceps more recovery after hard curls than after rows.

Soares 2015 · McDonald 2018e

RecoveryJudging a session by how sore you areSoreness does not show that a session worked, and its absence does not show that it failed.MistakeBeginnerSome support

A review found muscle damage is not what drives hypertrophy, and protocols causing little damage still grow muscle. Soreness is largest early on and fades as you adapt. Chasing it can lead to piling on volume or exercises you cannot recover from.

  • Judge sessions by reps, load and effort recorded in a log.

Damas 2018

Growth signalsIn two small studies, the biceps was driven almost fully and the quadriceps a little lessTwitch tests found near-full activation of the elbow flexors in 11 subjects and 88 to 95% in the quadriceps of 8 men, not the small share of muscle often claimed.AdvancedThin evidence

Voluntary activation is tested by adding an electrical pulse to the nerve or muscle during a maximal effort; any extra force shows drive the person did not supply. In 11 subjects the elbow flexors reached a median 99.4% in fast lifting efforts and 98.0% when held still. In 8 men the quadriceps reached 95.2% held still, 89.7% when shortening and 88.3% when lengthening, slowly. The studies came from different labs and methods, and held still the gap was only 98.0% against 95.2%. McDonald speculates that the gap may be why legs seem to need more volume; he offers no test of it.

Gandevia 1998 · Babault 2001 · McDonald 2009aj · McDonald 2019i

Growth signalsBodybuilders' fibres were the largest but made the least force for their sizeIn single thigh fibres, bodybuilders' were far bigger than power athletes', yet produced less force per unit of area than either power athletes' or control subjects'.AdvancedThin evidence

Thigh fibres from 12 bodybuilders (high volume, low to moderate loads), 6 power athletes and 14 controls were tested one by one. Bodybuilders' fibre area was 67% above the power athletes' and 88% above the controls'. Their fibres made 32% more absolute force than the controls', but force per unit of area was 62% below the power athletes' and 41% below the controls'. The authors' guess is less densely packed contractile protein, altered proteins, or both. Comparing different people at one time cannot show the training caused it. McDonald's article adds that 9 of the 12 said they used anabolic steroids.

Meijer 2015 · McDonald 2019c

Growth signalsTrained men with smaller fast-twitch fibres at the start grew more on one planIn 30 trained college-aged men on six weeks of high-volume training, a higher share of type II fibres and smaller type II fibres at the start went with more growth.AdvancedThin evidence

All 30 men, with about five years of lifting behind them, did the same programme. The 10 lowest responders had begun with type II fibres 18% larger than the 10 highest. Starting type II share and size each accounted for only about 15% and 21% of the variation in growth, and the authors call their sample limited. McDonald reads this as fibres already near their size having less room left. That is his interpretation, not something the study tested.

Haun 2019b · McDonald 2019c

Growth signalsSigns of new muscle fibres were commoner in steroid-using powerlifters than drug-free onesTrapezius biopsies from high-level powerlifters showed more markers of fibre formation in those who reported years of steroid use than in those who had never used.AdvancedThin evidence

The reported users had larger fibres, more nuclei, more centrally placed nuclei and more fibres carrying early developmental forms of protein, which the authors read as new fibres forming. Fibre-type mix did not differ. The groups were compared at one point in time, use was self-reported, and the authors say genetic differences cannot be excluded. McDonald's view is that animal models, such as round-the-clock stretch of a bird's wing, say little about lifting. Anabolic steroids are prescription-only drugs with real health risks; this is a research finding, not guidance on use. See the basics hyperplasia term.

Kadi 1999 · McDonald 2016k

Growth signalsTwo arm trials disagree on whether a hormone-raising leg session adds growthA hard leg session raised blood hormones around arm training. One trial found no extra arm growth; a smaller one, with legs trained first, found some.IntermediateThin evidence

In 12 young men over 15 weeks, one arm trained alone and the other before a high volume of leg work. Growth hormone, IGF-1 and testosterone rose only with the legs, yet arm area grew 12% and 10%, no different. In 9 subjects over 11 weeks, legs trained before one arm; both arms gained strength and volume, but only that arm grew at its widest point, and its curl 1RM rose more. In 56 young men, growth hormone and cortisol rises were weakly linked with fibre growth, and no hormone rise with strength. McDonald's view is that growth is driven within the muscle. See the basics entry on the post-session hormone rise.

West 2010 · Rønnestad 2011 · West 2012 · McDonald 2020e

Growth signalsGrowth hormone raised lean mass but not strength in fit young peopleIn a systematic review of controlled trials, healthy young people given growth hormone gained lean mass, but strength and exercise capacity did not seem to improve.IntermediateSome support

The review pooled 27 study samples of fit people aged 13 to 45, mostly from short trials. Lean mass rose 2.1 kg against no treatment, but recipients more often had soft-tissue swelling and fatigue, few studies measured performance, and the authors say exercise capacity may worsen. An older review found exercising young adults gained similar muscle size and strength on it or placebo. McDonald argues the rise in growth hormone after short-rest sessions matters little for growth. Growth hormone is a prescription-only medicine with real side effects. This is not guidance on use; such treatment belongs with a doctor.

Liu 2008 · Yarasheski 1994 · McDonald 2020e

Growth signalsHeavy low-volume training did not raise muscle energy proteins in one small trialTen weeks of heavy, low-volume lifting grew type II fibres in 15 trained men without the rise in energy-system proteins this lab saw after very high volume.AdvancedThin evidence

The men trained the whole body at 68 to 90% of 1RM, with no comparison group. Type II fibre area and squat strength rose; sarcoplasmic protein concentration and muscle fluid did not change, and in the 10 men given protein profiling, metabolic proteins were unaffected. Actin abundance fell 3.2%, myosin trended down and whole-body fat-free mass fell. McDonald cites the trial as support for heavy loads favouring contractile protein, but its abstract does not show that. The contrast with high volume is between two separate studies; the high-volume one is in the extreme-volume fluid entry.

Vann 2020 · McDonald 2019c

Growth signalsSquatting to make your upper body grow through a hormone boostThe testosterone rise after hard exercise is brief. McDonald's view is that chest and arms grow from the work they do themselves; the trials are mixed.MistakeBeginnerThin evidence

Heavy squatters are often said to be big all over. McDonald's explanation is that people who train legs that hard tend to train everything hard, and that squats and deadlifts also work the back, and the arms that steady the bar. In his view only lifters who compete in the squat must do it; others can pick leg exercises that suit their build. One small trial did find extra arm growth when legs were trained first; see the entry on hormone-raising leg sessions and arm growth, the testosterone time-window figure, and the basics entry on the hormone rise after a session.

  • Train your legs for your legs and your sport, not to raise hormones for the upper body (McDonald's view).
