# Diet: KiteSink fitness field guide

Losing or gaining weight on purpose. Each entry gives the point, the reasoning, what to do, how well supported it is, and its sources.

## Guidance

### The 3,500 kcal per pound rule is a rough guide that varies with body fat

**About 3,500 kcal (7,700 per kg) per pound of loss is only an average, and leaner people need less per unit lost.**

The rule says a cumulative deficit of 3,500 kcal equals one pound (32.2 MJ per kg) of weight loss. A model found it approximately matches obese people with more than 30 kg of body fat, and it overestimates the deficit needed for people with less body fat. Weight also responds slowly: the half time was about one year in a separate model. Use it to size a plan, then correct against the scale.

- Use the rule to pick a starting deficit, then check the trend over three to four weeks.

Deficit and rate of loss · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Hall 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.ijo.0803720), [Hall 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(11)60812-X)

### Aim to lose about 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight a week

**A pace of 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight a week is the usual advice for keeping muscle while getting leaner.**

A contest-prep review recommends 0.5 to 1 percent a week, and a later review prefers 0.5 percent or less for attenuating fat-free mass loss. In 24 athletes, both groups lost about 5.5 percent of body weight, but the 0.7 percent weekly group gained 2.1 percent lean mass and lost 31 percent of fat mass, while the 1.4 percent group kept lean mass unchanged and lost 21 percent of fat mass. At 80 kg (176 lb), 0.5 to 1 percent is 0.4 to 0.8 kg (0.9 to 1.8 lb).

- Pick a weekly percentage, convert it to kg or lb, and check against your four-week trend.

Deficit and rate of loss · Intermediate · Some support · Sources: [Helms 2014b](https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-11-20), [Garthe 2011](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97), [Roberts 2020](https://doi.org/10.2478/hukin-2019-0096)

### Deficits much beyond 500 kcal a day cost lean mass in lifters

**In a meta-regression, a deficit of about 500 kcal a day stopped lean-mass gains from training, while strength held up.**

A systematic review and meta-analysis of resistance training in an energy deficit found lean-mass gains were impaired against training without a deficit (effect size -0.57), but strength gains were comparable. The meta-regression put the point where gains were prevented at roughly 500 kcal a day. For people trying to preserve lean mass rather than add it, the authors advise avoiding deficits above that size.

- Keep deficits at or below about 500 kcal a day if gaining or keeping muscle is the priority.

Deficit and rate of loss · Intermediate · Well supported · Sources: [Murphy 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14075)

### Eat more protein when you diet: about 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of lean mass

**Lean lifters in a deficit kept more muscle with higher protein, around 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of fat-free mass.**

A review of six studies in lean, resistance-trained athletes in a deficit put protein needs at 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of fat-free mass, scaled up with the severity of the deficit and the leanness. The ISSN position gives 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of body weight. In a 4-week trial of 40 young men on a large deficit, 2.4 g/kg gained 1.2 kg of lean mass against 0.1 kg on 1.2 g/kg. A meta-analysis of 24 trials found higher protein held on to 0.43 kg more fat-free mass.

- Set protein by your lean mass, and move up as the diet gets harder and you get leaner.

Keeping muscle · Intermediate · Well supported · Sources: [Helms 2014a](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2013-0054), [Jäger 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8), [Longland 2016](https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.115.119339), [Wycherley 2012](https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.112.044321)

### Keep lifting while dieting, because training tells the body to keep muscle

**Resistance training reduced the lean mass lost in a diet, and strength was largely kept in deficit trials.**

An overview of 12 systematic reviews (149 studies) found resistance training limited lean mass loss during weight loss by about 0.8 kg. In six trials of older adults with obesity, training offset about 93 percent of the lean mass lost to calorie restriction. Strength gains in the deficit trials were comparable with and without a deficit.

- Keep the main lifts heavy, and drop sets before you drop load.

Keeping muscle · Beginner · Well supported · Sources: [Bellicha 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/obr.13256), [Sardeli 2018](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu10040423), [Murphy 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14075)

### Short sleep shifts diet loss toward muscle and away from fat

**In ten adults, five and a half hours of sleep cut the fat share of weight lost by 55 percent.**

Ten overweight adults ate a reduced-calorie diet for 14 days in two conditions, with 8.5 or 5.5 hours in bed. Short sleep cut the fraction of weight lost as fat by 55 percent (1.4 vs 0.6 kg) and increased loss of fat-free mass by 60 percent (1.5 vs 2.4 kg). Hunger rose too. It is a very small and short crossover study, so treat it as a strong hint and not a finding.

- Protect seven to nine hours in bed while dieting.

Keeping muscle · Intermediate · Thin evidence · Sources: [Nedeltcheva 2010](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-153-7-201010050-00006)

### Protein and unprocessed food help control hunger, at least in the short run

**Protein reduced hunger in meal studies, and a ward trial found unprocessed meals led to about 500 kcal less intake.**

A meta-analysis of 49 acute studies found protein lowered hunger and raised fullness, but long-term trials were inconclusive. In a ward study of 20 adults, ultra-processed meals led to 508 kcal a day more intake than unprocessed meals matched on presented calories, sugar, fibre and sodium, with weight up 0.9 kg against down 0.9 kg over two weeks. That is one small, short crossover trial.

- Build meals around protein and whole foods before cutting portion size.

Hunger and fullness · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Kohanmoo 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physbeh.2020.113123), [Hall 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2019.05.008)

### Appetite rises as you lose weight, so hunger is part of the process

**In one 52-week trial, appetite rose enough to add about 100 kcal a day per kg of weight lost.**

In 153 people losing weight without being aware of the deficit, a model found eating drifted above baseline by about 100 kcal a day for each kilogram lost, more than three times the matching fall in energy expenditure. This is one analysis of one drug trial. It points to appetite, not metabolism, as the main pressure against keeping weight off.

- Plan for hunger to rise late in a diet, and use protein, fibre and a fixed end date.

Hunger and fullness · Intermediate · Thin evidence · Sources: [Polidori 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.21653)

### Water-rich fruit and vegetables lower hunger for the same calories

**In one year-long trial, women who ate more water-rich foods lost more weight and reported less hunger.**

In 97 women with obesity told to cut fat, one group was also told to eat more water-rich foods, mainly fruit and vegetables, with no calorie goal. Among completers after a year, that group lost 7.9 kg against 6.4 kg, ate a greater weight of food at a lower energy density, and reported less hunger. It is one trial in women with obesity, not lifters.

- Fill half the plate with vegetables or fruit before adding the rest.

Hunger and fullness · Beginner · Thin evidence · Sources: [Ello-Martin 2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/85.6.1465), [Hall 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2019.05.008)

### Metabolic slowdown with dieting is real but smaller than most people fear

**Adaptation is real but small, tens to low hundreds of kcal a day in most studies, so check your log first.**

A systematic review of 33 studies (2,528 participants) found adaptive thermogenesis in 27, with lower or non-significant values in the better-designed studies. In 171 women, the drop after loss was 54 kcal a day, and in a subset of 46 it was no longer significant at one and two years. In 32 men on a 50 percent deficit for three weeks, true adaptation was 72 kcal a day. After 6 years, 14 Biggest Loser contestants were 499 kcal a day below prediction, an extreme case.

- Check the log and weekends before blaming metabolism.

Scale, plateaus and metabolism · Intermediate · Some support · Sources: [Nunes 2022a](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114521001094), [Martins 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/nqaa086), [Müller 2015](https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.115.109173), [Fothergill 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.21538)

### Day-to-day weight changes are mostly water, so read the trend

**Two-week changes of 1 to 3 kg were largely fat-free mass and water, not fat, in free-living adults.**

In 46 adults, a unit of two-week weight change was 84 percent fat-free mass, and the energy density of 1 to 3 kg changes was about 2,380 kcal per kg, well below that of fat. Glycogen is stored with three to four parts water. Weight runs higher on Sunday and Monday in 80 adults, and fluid retention peaks at the start of menstrual flow in 62 women. None of these is fat gain.

- Weigh at the same time under the same conditions, and judge the weekly average.

Scale, plateaus and metabolism · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Bhutani 2017](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.13336), [Kreitzman 1992](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/56.1.292S), [Orsama 2014](https://doi.org/10.1159/000356147), [White 2011](https://doi.org/10.1155/2011/138451)

### A two-week stall is not proof your diet stopped working

**Check logging and weekend intake before cutting calories, since stalls are usually noise or under-logging.**

Ten people who reported not losing weight on under 1,200 kcal actually under-reported intake by 47 percent and over-reported activity by 51 percent. In a one-year trial of 48 adults, calorie-restricted participants stopped losing weight on weekends. Add water noise from the table above and a two-week stall tells you little. A real stall holds over three to four weeks of honest logging.

- Wait three to four weeks, then cut by about 100 to 200 kcal or add steps.

Scale, plateaus and metabolism · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Lichtman 1992](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199212313272701), [Racette 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/oby.2008.320), [Bhutani 2017](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.13336)

### Weighing yourself regularly goes with more loss inside a programme

**Programmes that included self-weighing beat minimal care by 3.4 kg, and daily weighing did no better than weekly.**

A meta-analysis found 15 trials of multi-component programmes that included self-weighing lost 3.4 kg more than minimal or no intervention, with no significant difference between daily and weekly weighing. Only one study tested self-weighing alone, and it found no evidence of an effect. The scale works as a feedback tool inside a plan, not as a plan.

- Weigh on a fixed schedule, then act on the weekly average.

Scale, plateaus and metabolism · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Madigan 2015](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-015-0267-4)

### Diet breaks and refeeds give mixed fat-loss results and may ease hunger

**Planned weeks at maintenance did not improve fat loss in lifters, and they lowered hunger.**

In 61 resistance-trained adults over 12 weeks, alternating three weeks of restriction with one week at maintenance gave the same fat and lean mass as continuous dieting, with lower hunger. A meta-analysis of 12 trials (881 participants) found the same body composition and a smaller drop in resting rate. A small 7-week trial found a weekly two-day carb refeed kept more lean mass (-0.4 vs -1.3 kg). Results in men with obesity favoured breaks.

- Try a one-week break at maintenance if hunger or training quality is slipping.

Diet type and meal pattern · Intermediate · Some support · Sources: [PEOS 2021](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000002636), [Poon 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/nutrit/nuad168), [Campbell 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/jfmk5010019), [Byrne 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/ijo.2017.206)

### The best diet is the one you can keep to, since types differ little

**Low-carb and low-fat diets lost about the same weight, so pick the one you can keep.**

A network meta-analysis of 48 randomized trials (7,286 people) found weight loss with any low-carbohydrate or low-fat diet, with small differences among named diets. A larger analysis of 121 trials (21,942 patients) found about 4.6 kg at 6 months with low carb and 4.4 kg with low fat. In a 12-month trial of 609 adults, the difference was 0.7 kg, not significant. Behavioural support added about 3.2 kg at six months in one analysis.

- Choose the pattern that fits your food preferences and schedule.

Diet type and meal pattern · Beginner · Well supported · Sources: [Johnston 2014](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2014.10397), [Ge 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m696), [Gardner 2018](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.0245)

### Intermittent fasting works by eating less, with no clear extra effect

**Time-restricted eating did not beat plain calorie control in long trials, though one pooled analysis disagrees.**

In 139 adults over 12 months, eating 8 am to 4 pm with calorie restriction lost 8.0 kg against 6.3 kg for calorie restriction alone, not a significant difference. A 12-week trial of 116 people found no advantage, and a 41-person feeding trial matched calories and found no effect on weight. A pooled analysis of 30 studies did report 1.46 kg more loss in matched comparisons. With lifting, an analysis of eight studies (221 participants) kept fat-free mass.

- Use a fasting window only if it makes eating less easier for you.

Diet type and meal pattern · Intermediate · Some support · Sources: [Liu 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2114833), [Lowe 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2020.4153), [Maruthur 2024](https://doi.org/10.7326/M23-3132), [Fernandes-Alves 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nutrit/nuaf053), [Ashtary-Larky 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physbeh.2021.113453), [Keenan 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu12082349)

### Meal frequency and late eating matter little for fat loss at equal calories

**Differences between meal patterns are small at equal calories; fewer meals showed about 1 kg more loss in one analysis.**

A network meta-analysis of 22 trials (647 people) found two meals a day slightly lowered weight against three meals or six, by about 1 kg, under equal calories. In 16 obese adults on the same deficit, three meals and three snacks did no better than three meals. A meta-analysis of 9 trials found earlier eating gave 1.2 kg more loss, and a controlled crossover trial found late eating raised hunger. Pick the pattern you can follow.

- Choose a meal pattern around your day, and keep protein in each meal.

Diet type and meal pattern · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Schwingshackl 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/advances/nmaa056), [Cameron 2010](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114509992984), [Young 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/obr.13537), [Vujović 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2022.09.007)

### Weight lost tracked sticking to the diet, not which diet it was

**In one year-long trial, weight lost followed how closely people kept to their diet, with no link to the diet they chose.**

In 160 overweight or obese adults with heart-risk factors, assigned to Atkins, Zone, Weight Watchers or Ornish, mean one-year loss was 2.1 to 3.3 kg and only 50 to 65 percent finished. Loss correlated with self-reported adherence (r = 0.60) but not with diet type (r = 0.07). Adherence was self-reported and chosen by the participants, so this shows who did well, not that pushing adherence causes loss.

- Pick the eating pattern you can see yourself keeping for a year.

Diet type and meal pattern · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Dansinger 2005](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.293.1.43), [Johnston 2014](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2014.10397)

### Exercise adds to fat loss, and daily movement drops on diets if you let it

**Exercise adds 1.5 to 3.5 kg of loss in reviews, and people on diets may move less outside the gym.**

An overview of 12 meta-analyses found exercise led to 1.5 to 3.5 kg of weight loss and similar results from aerobic and interval training when energy expenditure matched. The ACSM suggests 150 minutes a week as a floor and 200 to 300 for long-term loss. A systematic review of 36 articles found lower non-exercise activity in 63 percent of diet-only arms. In 16 overfed adults, NEAT explained the large differences in fat gain.

- Set a daily step or activity floor and keep it during the diet.

Cardio and daily movement · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Bellicha 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/obr.13256), [DONNELLY 2009](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181949333), [Silva 2018](https://doi.org/10.1017/S000711451800096X), [Levine 1999](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.283.5399.212)

### Getting very lean costs hormones, mood and training quality, so go carefully

**Prolonged energy availability under about 25 kcal per kg of lean mass led to muscle loss and hormonal and mood problems.**

Reviews of lean athletes link a long period at low energy availability to muscle loss, hormone disruption and psychological problems. A case study of one natural bodybuilder saw body fat fall from 14.8 to 4.5 percent and testosterone from 9.22 to 2.27 ng/mL, recovering after the contest. In 27 women dieting for a fitness show, sex hormones fell and menstrual irregularities rose, and most recovered within three to four months. Involve a coach and clinician.

- Treat contest-level leanness as a short, supervised project, not a lifestyle.
- See a clinician if periods stop or mood drops.

Very lean, regain and maintenance · Advanced · Some support · Sources: [Fagerberg 2018](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2016-0332), [Rossow 2013](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.8.5.582), [Hulmi 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2016.00689), [Loucks 2003](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2002-020369), [Mountjoy 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994), [Helms 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports7070172)

### Weight regain is common, and maintenance is possible with sustained habits

**Most people regain some weight, and a minority keep most of it off by staying active and weighing themselves.**

In 171 women, participants regained 52 percent of the weight lost at one year and 89 percent at two. A meta-analysis of 29 US studies found that after five years the average person kept off more than 3 kg. Among 2,886 National Weight Control Registry members, mean loss was 31.3 kg at entry and 23.1 kg at ten years. They report about an hour of activity a day, regular weighing and consistent eating on weekends. The registry is self-selected.

- After the diet, raise calories in steps and keep weighing weekly.
- Keep activity and protein high.

Very lean, regain and maintenance · Intermediate · Some support · Sources: [Martins 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/nqaa086), [Anderson 2001](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/74.5.579), [Thomas 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amepre.2013.08.019), [Wing 2005](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/82.1.222S), [Hall 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mcna.2017.08.012), [Rosenbaum 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/ijo.2010.184)

### A small surplus adds muscle, and a large surplus mostly adds fat

**A surplus of about 10 to 20 percent over maintenance is a common starting range for lifters, and nobody has proven the best size.**

A narrative review for physique athletes suggests 10 to 20 percent above maintenance, with advanced lifters more conservative. In 17 lifters over 8 weeks, a 15 percent surplus mainly increased fat gain rather than strength or muscle thickness. Another review finds the ideal surplus is unknown. If maintenance is 2,500 kcal, 10 to 20 percent is 250 to 500 kcal. Evidence is thin and short.

- Start near the low end of the surplus and adjust to the scale.

Gaining phases · Intermediate · Thin evidence · Sources: [Iraki 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports7070154), [Helms 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-023-00651-y), [Slater 2019](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2019.00131)

### Recomposition works best for beginners, returners and people carrying more fat

**Gaining muscle while losing fat is realistic with high protein and hard training, and likely harder for lean, advanced lifters.**

In 40 young men on a roughly 40 percent deficit with heavy training and 2.4 g/kg of protein, lean mass rose 1.2 kg while fat mass fell 4.8 kg over four weeks. A review of six studies found that the groups that held on to or gained lean mass were among those with the highest body fat, the smallest deficit or a new training stimulus. Advanced lifters with low body fat have less room.

- Use a small deficit or maintenance with high protein and progressive training.

Gaining phases · Intermediate · Thin evidence · Sources: [Longland 2016](https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.115.119339), [Helms 2014a](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2013-0054), [Murphy 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14075)

## Common mistakes

### Dieting faster than 1 percent of body weight a week

**Faster loss retained less lean mass in athletes, and the speed is hard to keep up.**

In 24 athletes, a 1.4 percent weekly loss kept lean mass unchanged and cut fat mass 21 percent, while a 0.7 percent loss gained 2.1 percent lean mass and cut fat mass 31 percent. Reviews recommend 0.5 to 1 percent a week.

- Slow the deficit to 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight a week.

Deficit and rate of loss · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Garthe 2011](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97), [Helms 2014b](https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-11-20)

### Cutting protein and stopping lifting while dieting

**The two habits that protect muscle in a deficit are protein and heavy training, and dropping both costs lean mass.**

Higher protein held on to lean mass in trials of lean lifters, and training reduced lean loss by about 0.8 kg across an overview of reviews.

- Keep protein high and lifts heavy until the diet ends.

Keeping muscle · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Helms 2014a](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2013-0054), [Bellicha 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/obr.13256)

### Cutting calories after a short stall

**Short stalls are mostly water and weekend noise, and cutting on them stacks deficit on top of a working plan.**

A two-week plateau can come from water, a missed weekend or logging drift, as 48 adults who stopped losing on weekends showed. Cutting calories on a stall moves you toward the 500 kcal limit sooner. Wait three to four weeks, check the log, and only then change calories or steps.

- Check the four-week trend and the log before changing calories.

Scale, plateaus and metabolism · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Racette 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/oby.2008.320), [Bhutani 2017](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.13336), [Murphy 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14075)

### Dieting with no end date and no plan for after

**Regain follows the diet for most people, and a plan for the exit matters as much as the deficit.**

In 171 women, 52 percent of lost weight was regained after one year and 89 percent after two. Registry members who kept it off kept activity, regular weighing and steady eating. Set an end date for the diet.

- Plan the exit: a target date, a maintenance range and a weekly weigh-in.
- Step calories up toward maintenance over several weeks.

Very lean, regain and maintenance · Beginner · Some support · Sources: [Martins 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/nqaa086), [Wing 2005](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/82.1.222S)

### Treating a gaining phase as a licence to eat anything

**A large surplus mainly added fat in a trial, and extra fat has to be dieted off later.**

In 17 lifters, a 15 percent surplus mostly increased fat gain rather than strength or muscle thickness. A review suggests 10 to 20 percent for physique athletes, less for advanced lifters. The extra fat then has to be dieted off.

- Use a measured surplus and watch the rate of weight gain.

Gaining phases · Intermediate · Thin evidence · Sources: [Helms 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-023-00651-y), [Iraki 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports7070154)

## Rate of gain by training age

## Terms

- **Adaptive thermogenesis (metabolic adaptation)**: A fall in energy use beyond what the loss of body mass explains, seen during and after weight loss. Studies find it ranges from small to modest.
- **Diet break and refeed**: A diet break is a planned stretch, often one to two weeks, at maintenance calories. A refeed is shorter, usually one or two days of higher carbohydrate.
- **Energy deficit**: Eating less energy than you use. It is the one requirement for losing body fat. A daily deficit of 500 kcal is about 3,500 kcal a week.
- **NEAT (non-exercise activity)**: Non-exercise activity thermogenesis: energy spent on walking, standing, fidgeting and chores. It can fall during a diet without you noticing.
- **Recomposition**: Gaining muscle and losing fat at the same time, so body weight changes little while the body changes shape.
- **Set point and settling point**: Two models of body weight: a defended set point, or a settling point where weight drifts with food and activity. A review finds both incomplete.
- **Time-restricted eating**: Eating all meals within a set daily window, such as eight hours, and fasting for the rest. It is one form of intermittent fasting.
