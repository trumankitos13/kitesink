171 terms, in plain words.

Every term the field guide uses, defined once. Search, or narrow by area.

Working notes from someone who trains, not a coach or a dietitian. General information, not medical advice.

20-rep squat (breathing squat)
An old rest-pause method: one set of 20 squats with a weight you could normally lift about 10 times, made possible by standing with the bar and taking several deep breaths between reps. Training basics
McDonald 2016d
Absolute and relative gains
An absolute gain is the raw change, such as 2 kg of muscle or 20 kg on a lift. A relative gain is that change as a percentage of the starting value. Sex comparisons often flip depending on which one a study reports. Women
Jones 2021
Accessory exercise
An exercise outside the three competition lifts, chosen to build a muscle or fix a weak point. Powerlifting
Active recovery (active rest)
A short, easy session placed between harder days, with less volume and much lower intensity than normal training. McDonald's test is that you leave feeling fresher than you arrived. Recovery
McDonald 2010a
Adaptive thermogenesis (metabolic adaptation)
A fall in energy use beyond what the loss of body mass explains, seen during and after weight loss. Studies find it ranges from small to modest. Diet
Adipose tissue and adipocytes
Adipose tissue is body fat. Its cells, adipocytes, store energy as triglyceride and release hormones such as leptin, so fat acts as a gland as well as a fuel store. Body composition
Considine 1996 · McDonald 2008ad
Aerobic intervals
Easy exercise broken into short bouts with rests, for people who cannot yet keep going for 20 minutes. For example, 5 easy minutes, a short break, then repeat. Over the weeks the bouts get longer and the rests shorter. Conditioning
McDonald 2008y
Allostatic load
The cumulative cost to the body of adapting to every stressor it meets. Training, work, short sleep, dieting and emotional strain draw on the same stress response, so they add together. Recovery
McEwen 2000 · McDonald 2010n
Alpha-2 and beta adrenoceptors
Receptors on fat cells for adrenaline and noradrenaline. Beta receptors switch fat release on and alpha-2 receptors switch it off. A depot with relatively more alpha-2 receptors lets go of fat more slowly. Body composition
Karpe 2015 · McDonald 2008af
Amenorrhea (primary and secondary)
Absence of menstrual periods. Primary: periods have not begun by about age 15. Secondary: three or more periods in a row missed by a woman who has menstruated before and is not pregnant. Both need a doctor's assessment. Women
Elliott-Sale 2021
Anabolic-androgenic steroids (AAS)
Testosterone and synthetic drugs derived from it. They build muscle (anabolic) and produce male sex characteristics (androgenic). They are prescription medicines with medical uses, carry health risks and are banned in drug-tested sport. Body composition
Android and gynoid fat patterns
Android means fat carried on the trunk and abdomen, the usual male pattern. Gynoid means fat on hips and thighs, more common in women before menopause. Upper-body fat is linked to more heart disease and type 2 diabetes. Body composition
Karpe 2015
ApoB (apolipoprotein B)
A protein carried on each cholesterol-bearing particle that can clog arteries. The blood level reflects the number of these particles and can differ from LDL cholesterol. Health
Soffer 2024
Atwater factors
Standard energy values per gram that food labels use: 4 kcal for protein and carbohydrate, 9 for fat, 7 for alcohol. They are averages, not measurements of your food. Nutrition
Back-cycle (reset)
A planned step back after a stall: cut the load by 10 to 20%, then build up again over three to four weeks and aim to pass the old best. Training basics
McDonald 2015b
Beginner (novice)
A lifter who still progresses on a simple, repeated routine. The 2009 ACSM position stand uses novice for people with no lifting experience, or none for several years. Training basics
ACSM 2009 · McDonald 2015e
BMR and RMR
Basal metabolic rate is the lowest energy use the body can run on, measured under strict conditions. Resting metabolic rate is measured awake, fasted and lying quietly, and reads a little higher. Most labs and equations give RMR. Body composition
McDonald 2007g
Body composition
What your body weight is made of. The simplest model has two parts. One is fat. The other, fat-free mass, is everything else: muscle, bone, organs, blood, water and stored carbohydrate. Body composition
McDonald 2008ac
Bomb out
To miss all three attempts on a lift; in a full-power meet that usually means no total. Meet day
Calorie partitioning
Where energy goes or comes from when intake changes: how much of a surplus builds muscle and how much is stored as fat, and how much of a deficit is met from fat and how much from lean tissue. Body composition
McDonald 2008i
Chief referee
The referee who gives the commands on the platform; two side referees also judge each lift. Meet day
Classic (raw)
A division with a non-supportive singlet and no supportive suits, shirts or knee wraps; belt, knee sleeves and wrist wraps are allowed within limits. Meet day
Cognitive dietary restraint
The ongoing, deliberate effort to limit eating in order to control body weight, measured by questionnaire. It describes a mindset and does not always mean eating less. Recovery
McLean 2001 · Rideout 2006
Compound and isolation exercises
A compound (multi-joint) exercise moves two or more joints and works several muscles, as in a squat or row. An isolation (single-joint) exercise moves one joint, as in a leg extension or curl. Both exist as free-weight and machine versions. Training basics
Concentric, isometric and eccentric failure
Being unable to lift the weight, to hold it still, or to lower it under control. A muscle can lower more than it holds and hold more than it lifts, so they come in that order. Nearly all sets stop at concentric failure. Hypertrophy
McDonald 2020h
Concurrent periodisation
Training several qualities, such as speed, maximal strength and size, every week with a changing emphasis. Popularly called conjugate training; McDonald says the original Russian term meant something else. Hypertrophy
McDonald 2007h
Daily undulating periodisation (DUP)
A plan that varies rep ranges and loads between sessions within the week, such as heavy, medium and light days. Powerlifting
De novo lipogenesis (DNL)
The making of new fat from carbohydrate. In people it is a minor route on ordinary diets and becomes large only when glycogen stores are full during heavy, sustained carbohydrate overfeeding. Body composition
Acheson 1988
Delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS)
Muscle tenderness and stiffness that builds in the day or two after unaccustomed or eccentric-heavy exercise and fades over several days. It is most common early in a programme and after a layoff. Recovery
Cheung 2003 · McDonald 2015k
Deload
A period of reduced training stress meant to lower fatigue and prepare you for later training, as defined by a coach consensus. Hypertrophy
Bell 2023
Deload
A planned lighter week or block, with less volume or load, to clear fatigue between harder blocks. Powerlifting
Detraining
The partial or complete loss of training adaptations when training stops or falls too low. Strength, power and muscle size fall at different rates, and the losses grow with the length of the break. Recovery
Bosquet 2013
Diet break and refeed
A diet break is a planned stretch, often one to two weeks, at maintenance calories. A refeed is shorter, usually one or two days of higher carbohydrate. Diet
Double progression
A way of progressing inside a rep range such as 6 to 12: add reps from session to session until you reach the top of the range, then add weight, which drops you back toward the bottom, and build up again. Training basics
McDonald 2019n
Dynapenia
The age-related loss of muscle strength. In later life it runs faster than the loss of muscle size, so strength and mass are tracked separately. Health
Mitchell 2012
Effect size and confidence interval
Effect size is how big a difference is, in standard deviations. The interval shows a plausible range. If it spans zero, there is no clear difference. Hypertrophy
Energy availability (EA)
Energy eaten minus the energy spent in exercise, divided by fat-free mass. It is the kcal per kg of fat-free mass left each day for every other body function, including the reproductive system. Women
Burke 2018
Energy deficit
Eating less energy than you use. It is the one requirement for losing body fat. A daily deficit of 500 kcal is about 3,500 kcal a week. Diet
EPOC (the afterburn)
Excess post-exercise oxygen consumption: the extra oxygen, and so energy, your body uses after a session while it recovers. It rises with intensity and duration, yet stays a small fraction of what the session itself cost. Conditioning
LaForgia 2006
Ergogenic aid
Something taken to improve exercise performance or training results. Creatine and caffeine are the best-supported examples. Nutrition
Essential fat
The fat the body cannot give up and stay healthy, held in nerves, bone marrow, organs and cell membranes. In healthy men the measured floor is about 4 to 6 percent of body weight. Women's is higher. Body composition
Friedl 1994 · McDonald 2008f
Eumenorrhea
Regular menstrual cycles. A research guide defines it as cycle lengths of 21 to 35 days with nine or more periods in a row over a year, and asks for hormone checks before a study calls a participant eumenorrheic. Women
Elliott-Sale 2021
Exercise efficiency
The share of the energy you burn that becomes external work, with the rest lost as heat. McDonald puts it at about 20 to 25 percent in cycling. Higher efficiency means more speed or power for the same oxygen cost. Conditioning
Joyner 2008 · McDonald 2008m
External and internal focus
External focus is attention on the effect of a movement, such as the bar's path. Internal focus is attention on body parts, such as squeezing a muscle. Motor-learning research favours external focus for skill. Technique
Chua 2021
False hope syndrome
Polivy and Herman's name for a cycle of repeated self-change attempts driven by unrealistic hopes about how fast, how far and how easily change will come, and what it will bring. Failure is followed by a fresh attempt. Diet
Polivy 2002 · McDonald 2016p
Fartlek
Swedish for speed play: an easy session with short, unplanned faster stretches. McDonald suggests a timed version indoors, such as a brief pick-up every few minutes that is quicker than cruising pace without being a sprint. Conditioning
McDonald 2014r
Fat-free mass index (FFMI)
Fat-free mass (kg) over the square of height (m). It is the lean-mass version of BMI and lets you compare muscularity between people of different heights. Body composition
Kouri 1995
Fat-free mass or lean body mass (FFM, LBM)
All of the body that is not fat. In everyday use the two terms mean the same thing. Muscle is only part of it, and its water and glycogen content can shift by a kilo or more within days. Body composition
McDonald 2008ac · McDonald 2019a
Female athlete triad
Three linked components in active women: energy status, reproductive function and bone health. Each runs on a spectrum from healthy to clinical, and energy deficiency starts the chain. Women
De Souza 2026
Fitness-fatigue model
A two-factor description of training: each session adds both fitness and fatigue, performance reflects fitness minus fatigue, and fatigue fades faster. It is a curve fitted to data, not a biological mechanism. Recovery
Hellard 2006 · McDonald 2019m
Five sets of five (5x5)
A strength routine of five sets of five reps on a few compound lifts, popularised by Bill Starr in a book for football players and revived by later barbell programmes. The label covers several quite different set plans. Training basics
McDonald 2009b
Flexion-relaxation
The spinal muscles going electrically quiet near a full forward bend, so that ligaments and discs carry more of the load. A review links it to load sharing by passive tissues. Technique
Colloca 2005
Flight (group)
A group of lifters who take their attempts together; larger sessions split into flights, with lower previous totals lifting first in IPF rules. Meet day
Follicular phase
The first half of the menstrual cycle, from the first day of bleeding to ovulation. Estrogen and progesterone are both low at the start. Estrogen then climbs to its peak just before ovulation while progesterone stays low. Women
Elliott-Sale 2021
Force-velocity curve
The trade-off between the force a muscle can produce and its shortening speed: the faster the movement, the lower the force. Power, the product of the two, peaks in the middle of the curve. Training basics
McDonald 2014d
Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea (FHA)
Loss of ovulation and periods with no identifiable disease behind it, often linked to stress, weight loss, heavy exercise or a mix of these. Doctors diagnose it only after excluding other causes. Women
Gordon 2017
Functional overreaching
A short dip in performance after a block of harder training that clears within days to about two weeks of easier work and is followed by performance at or above the earlier level. Recovery
Meeusen 2013 · Kreher 2012
Functional threshold
The hardest pace or power you can hold steadily for about an hour. Lab names for nearby points include maximal lactate steady state and the second lactate threshold. Above it, fatigue builds within minutes. Conditioning
Billat 2003 · McDonald 2009y
Gastrocnemius and soleus
The two main calf muscles. The gastrocnemius is the visible one and crosses both knee and ankle. The soleus lies under it and crosses only the ankle, so it does most of the work when the knee is bent. Technique
General adaptation syndrome (GAS)
Hans Selye's three-stage account of the response to severe stress: alarm, resistance, exhaustion. Coaches borrowed it to justify periodised training, and how well it fits training is disputed. Recovery
Buckner 2020 · Cunanan 2018
General and specific warm-up
A general warm-up is light whole-body activity that raises body temperature. A specific warm-up is progressively heavier sets of the exercise you are about to train, which also rehearse technique and ready the nervous system. Training basics
McDonald 2009ai
Glycaemic index (GI)
How far a food's carbohydrate raises blood glucose against the same amount from glucose or white bread. Fat, protein and fibre in a meal lower it, and in studies GI did not change how much fat people burned. Diet
Díaz 2006 · McDonald 2008q
Hard set
A working set taken close to failure, usually within a few reps. Warm-up sets and easy sets do not count towards weekly volume. Hypertrophy
Hazard ratio (HR)
A figure comparing risk between groups over time. 1.0 means no difference, 0.80 means 20% lower risk and 1.20 means 20% higher. It shows association, not cause. Health
HbA1c (glycated haemoglobin)
A blood test that shows average blood sugar over about the past 2 to 3 months. It is given as a percentage, and published ranges separate normal, prediabetes and diabetes. Health
ADAPPCD 2025
Heart rate variability (HRV)
The beat-to-beat variation in the time between heartbeats, usually measured on waking and reported as RMSSD. Higher values reflect more parasympathetic (vagal) activity. Read it against your own rolling baseline. Recovery
Bellenger 2016 · Manresa-Rocamora 2021
Heavy-light-medium training
A weekly pattern for training the same lifts three times: one heavy day, one light day and one in between. Bill Starr popularised it for lifters who had grown too strong to go heavy at every session. Recovery
McDonald 2015o
Horizontal adduction
Moving the upper arm across the front of the body toward the midline, as in a dumbbell fly. It is the main action of the chest muscle in every press. Technique
Hypertrophy and hyperplasia
Hypertrophy is the enlargement of existing muscle fibres. Hyperplasia would be a rise in the number of fibres. Growth from training in humans is explained by hypertrophy; hyperplasia is unproven and, in McDonald's view, minor at most. Training basics
McDonald 2014i · Jorgenson 2020
Intra-abdominal pressure (IAP)
Pressure inside the abdominal cavity. It rises when you hold a breath and tighten the trunk, and a lifting belt raised it further in a deadlift study. It is thought to help support the spine. Technique
Hagins 2004 · Harman 1989
Kilojoule (kJ) and kilocalorie (kcal)
Two units of food energy. One kcal, the calorie on most labels, equals 4.184 kJ. Many countries print kJ. Nutrition
Lactate threshold
The effort above which lactate starts to build up in the blood faster than it clears. The first threshold marks the end of easy effort and varies between people. Health
MacIntosh 2021
Lengthened-position training
Loading a muscle while it is stretched, as at the bottom of a curl or squat. Several trials found it grew more than loading it short. Hypertrophy
Maeo 2021
Leptin
A hormone made mainly by fat cells. Blood levels rise with body fat and fall quickly when you eat less, so it tells the brain both how much energy is stored and whether food is coming in. Body composition
Considine 1996 · McDonald 2008h
Leucine
An essential amino acid that acts as a trigger for muscle protein synthesis. Animal proteins contain more than most plant proteins, per gram of protein. Nutrition
Load intensity and effort intensity
Two different meanings of intensity. Intensity of load is the weight as a share of your one-rep max. Intensity of effort is how close the set comes to failure, often given as RPE or reps in reserve. Training basics
McDonald 2008ae
Lot number
A number drawn at weigh-in; if two lifters choose the same weight, the lower lot number lifts first. Meet day
Luteal phase
The part of the cycle that runs from ovulation to the next bleed. Progesterone reaches its highest level and estrogen stays fairly high, then both fall before the period. Body temperature runs slightly higher in this phase. Women
Elliott-Sale 2021 · McDonald 2015v
Macronutrients (macros)
The three nutrients that provide most energy: protein, carbohydrate and fat. Alcohol is sometimes counted as a fourth source of energy. Nutrition
Maintenance calories
The daily energy intake at which your body weight stays steady over weeks. Also called total daily energy expenditure. Nutrition
Maximal strength
The greatest force you can produce in one effort with no time limit. In the gym it is measured as a one-rep max. Coaches outside the lifting sports often track a 3- or 5-rep max to avoid true maximum attempts. Training basics
McDonald 2014m
Mechanotransduction
How a muscle fibre senses physical tension and converts it into chemical signals for growth. A 2011 review puts mTOR signalling at the centre of this response to load. Hypertrophy
Hornberger 2011 · McDonald 2019i
Melatonin
A hormone released by the pineal gland in the brain at night. It helps set the timing of sleep, and light reaching the eyes in the evening suppresses it. It also interacts with the hormones that run the reproductive cycle. Women
McDonald 2020a
MET (metabolic equivalent)
A unit of effort. 1 MET is resting energy use, and brisk walking is about 3 to 5 METs. Fitness is often reported as the peak METs reached on a treadmill test. Health
Metabolic (depletion) training
Weight training with light loads, sets of 15 to 20 or more and rests of a minute or less. It empties muscle glycogen and builds tolerance for fatigue more than it builds size or strength. Training basics
McDonald 2014b
Metabolic flexibility
The ability to switch between burning carbohydrate and burning fat as supply and demand change. McDonald notes it is often reduced with obesity and inactivity, and that regular training helps restore it. Conditioning
McDonald 2014s · McDonald 2014u
Moment arm and torque
A moment arm is the perpendicular distance from a joint to the line along which a force acts. Torque, the turning effect at the joint, is force times moment arm, so a weight held farther from the joint is harder to move. Hypertrophy
McDonald 2019j
Momentary muscular failure
The point where you cannot finish another rep despite maximal effort. Volitional failure means you stop by choice. Hypertrophy
Pelland 2022
mTOR (mTORC1)
A protein kinase complex in muscle cells that helps switch on protein synthesis in response to loading. In a human trial, blocking it with the drug rapamycin stopped the early rise in synthesis after hard contractions. Hypertrophy
Drummond 2009 · Hornberger 2011
Muscle memory
The observation that lost strength and muscle return faster than they were first built. Proposed explanations are retained nuclei in muscle fibres, lasting epigenetic changes and retained skill. None is settled in humans. Recovery
Cumming 2024 · Rahmati 2022 · McDonald 2009aa
Muscular exhaustion
The state in which a muscle can produce no useful force at all. Failure on a set is narrower: the muscle can no longer meet that load but could still move a lighter one. Lifting sets almost never reach exhaustion. Hypertrophy
McDonald 2020f
Myofibrillar and sarcoplasmic hypertrophy
Myofibrillar growth adds contractile protein, the part of a fibre that produces force. Sarcoplasmic growth is a disproportionate expansion of the fluid and stored material around it. Training basics
Roberts 2020b
Myonuclear domain
The share of a fibre served by one nucleus. In 15 young men it grew over 90 days of heavy training with no rise in nuclei and returned to baseline after stopping, so moderate growth needed no new nuclei. Hypertrophy
Kadi 2004
NEAT (non-exercise activity)
Non-exercise activity thermogenesis: energy spent on walking, standing, fidgeting and chores. It can fall during a diet without you noticing. Diet
Negative calorie illusion (dieter's paradox)
The mistaken belief that adding a healthy food to an unhealthy meal lowers its calories. In Chernev's studies the error was larger in people most concerned with their weight. Diet
Chernev 2011 · McDonald 2012b
Non-functional overreaching
A performance drop from more training than recovery allowed that takes weeks to months to clear, often with mood, sleep or hormonal symptoms. Rest restores the old level, but nothing is gained. Recovery
Meeusen 2013 · Kreher 2012
Nutrient balance (protein, carbohydrate and fat balance)
Intake minus oxidation for one nutrient. In 54 adults in a chamber, the body matched carbohydrate and protein burning to intake, so day-to-day surpluses and gaps landed almost entirely on fat balance. Diet
Abbott 1988 · McDonald 2017h
Oligomenorrhea
Infrequent periods, defined in research as cycles longer than 35 days. Causes range from energy deficiency to polycystic ovary syndrome, so the pattern needs a doctor's assessment, not a guess. Women
Elliott-Sale 2021 · Hagmar 2009
One-rep max (1RM)
The heaviest load you can lift for one full rep. Training loads are often given as a percent of it, such as 70% of 1RM. Hypertrophy
One-repetition maximum (1RM)
The heaviest weight you can lift once with legal technique; the base for percentage programming. Powerlifting
Opener (first attempt)
The first of your three attempts on a lift; chosen to be easy enough that you lift it with room to spare. Meet day
Outcome, performance and process goals
An outcome goal is a result against others, such as winning. A performance goal is a standard of your own, such as a 100 kg (220 lb) squat. A process goal is an action you carry out, such as hitting depth on every rep. Training basics
Williamson 2022
Overtraining syndrome (OTS)
A performance drop lasting months, usually with fatigue and mood change, after a long stretch in which training and life stress outran recovery. No test confirms it; doctors reach it by excluding other causes. Recovery
Meeusen 2013 · Carrard 2022
P-ratio
The share of a change in body energy that comes from protein, which stands for lean tissue. A low P-ratio during a diet means the loss was mostly fat. A high one means much of it was lean. Body composition
Hall 2007 · McDonald 2007e
Passive rest
A day with no training at all. Ordinary walking is allowed. It is the safer choice for anyone who cannot keep an easy session easy. Recovery
McDonald 2010a
Patellofemoral joint
The joint between the kneecap (patella) and the groove at the end of the thigh bone. How hard it is loaded changes with knee angle and differs between squats and leg extensions. Technique
Powers 2014
Periodisation
Planned changes in volume, load or exercises over weeks and months, such as linear steps or weekly undulation. Hypertrophy
Moesgaard 2022
Polarized and pyramidal training
Polarized training puts most sessions at easy effort and a small share at hard interval effort, with little in between. Pyramidal training also starts from a large easy base but keeps more work in the middle zone than at the top. Conditioning
Rosenblat 2025
Post-activation potentiation and complex training
A short rise in explosive performance after a few heavy efforts. Complex training alternates heavy sets with jumps to use it. A meta-analysis of 47 studies found small to moderate effects, larger in stronger, more experienced lifters. Training basics
Seitz 2016 · McDonald 2014h
Power (muscular power)
Work divided by the time taken to do it, or force multiplied by speed. Lifting the same weight through the same distance in half the time doubles the power. Training basics
McDonald 2014c
Prilepin's chart
A table from Soviet weightlifting, attributed to A. S. Prilepin, that gives reps per set and total reps in a session for each band of intensity. It records what elite weightlifters did and was never tested in an experiment. Training basics
McDonald 2014o
Progressive overload
Gradually asking more of the muscle over time, by adding reps, load or sets, so it keeps having a reason to adapt. Adding reps up to the top of a range and then adding weight is called double progression. Hypertrophy
Rack height sheet
The form where you record squat and bench rack and foot-block settings at weigh-in, so loaders set them before your attempt. Meet day
Ramped sets and sets across
Ramped sets rise in weight from set to set, so only the last one or two are hard. Sets across use the same working weight for every set, after separate warm-up sets. Training basics
McDonald 2009b
Rate of force development (RFD)
How quickly force rises at the start of an effort. Sprint steps, jumps and throws allow only about 0.1 to 0.2 seconds, so the force you can reach in that window matters more than your slow maximum. Training basics
McDonald 2014c
Rate of perceived exertion (RPE)
A 1-10 effort scale. On the RIR-based version, RPE 10 means 0 RIR, RPE 9 means 1 RIR, and so on. Hypertrophy
Zourdos 2016
Rating of perceived exertion (RPE)
A 1 to 10 rating of how hard a set was; in strength training it is tied to how many repetitions were left. Powerlifting
Recomposition
Gaining muscle and losing fat over the same period. Body weight may hold steady or fall while the body changes shape. Diet
Recruitment and rate coding
The two ways a muscle grades its force. Recruitment adds motor units; rate coding fires active ones faster. In one study the biceps kept recruiting to high forces, while a small thumb muscle finished early and relied more on firing rate. Hypertrophy
Kukulka 1981 · McDonald 2019i
Relative energy deficiency in sport (REDs)
The International Olympic Committee's name for the health and performance problems that follow exposure to low energy availability. It applies to women and men, and diagnosis is clinical. Women
Mountjoy 2023
Relative strength
Strength divided by body weight. It matters most where you move your own body or compete in a weight class. Gaining weight faster than strength lowers it. Training basics
McDonald 2014m
Repeated bout effect
The protection that one session of unaccustomed eccentric exercise gives against damage and soreness from later sessions. It is why soreness fades within a few sessions even when the work stays the same. Recovery
McHugh 2003
Repetition continuum
The idea that low, moderate and high reps lean toward strength, size and endurance in turn. The zones overlap: the heavy end of size training is the light end of strength training. Training basics
McDonald 2014b
Repetition maximum (RM) load
The top weight a lifter can manage for a stated number of reps in good form. A 10RM is a load you can lift ten times and not eleven. A zone such as 8 to 12RM sets the load without a percentage. Training basics
McDonald 2015s
Repetitions in reserve (RIR)
The number of reps you could still have done when the set ended. 0 RIR means failure. 2 RIR means two more reps were possible. Hypertrophy
Zourdos 2016
Repetitions in reserve (RIR)
How many more clean repetitions you could have done when the set ended; 2 RIR means two more were possible. Powerlifting
Resting metabolic rate (RMR)
The energy your body uses at rest to keep you alive, usually 60 to 70 percent of a day for inactive people, estimated by equations or measured. Nutrition
Rigid and flexible restraint
Two styles of holding back on food. Rigid control runs on fixed rules and forbidden foods, all or nothing. Flexible control allows any food in limited amounts and evens out over days. Diet
Westenhoefer 1999
Romanian deadlift (RDL)
A hip hinge started from standing: knees slightly bent, hips pushed back, back flat, bar lowered along the thighs. The name is credited to Romanian weightlifter Nicu Vlad. Technique
McDonald 2009ab
Sarcopenia
A condition of low muscle strength with low muscle quantity or quality, found mostly in older adults. A clinician diagnoses it using strength tests and a body measure. Health
Cruz-Jentoft 2018
Satellite cells
Stem cells that sit against the outside of each muscle fibre. Once activated they can divide and donate nuclei. In 15 young men their count rose 31% over 90 days of heavy training and was still raised 60 days after stopping. Hypertrophy
Kadi 2004
Scapular retraction and depression
Retraction is drawing the shoulder blades back toward the spine. Depression is drawing them down, away from the ears. Rows finish with the first and pulldowns with the second. Technique
Set point and settling point
Two models of body weight: a defended set point, or a settling point where weight drifts with food and activity. A review finds both incomplete. McDonald's view is that the body resists loss far more than gain. Diet
Speakman 2011 · McDonald 2008x
Size principle (orderly recruitment)
Motor units join a contraction in order of size: small, low-force units first, large, high-force units last, as the force needed rises or earlier units tire. So the largest units work mainly under heavy loads or late in hard sets. Hypertrophy
McDonald 2019i
Sports continuum
Lyle McDonald's way of placing sports on a line from pure strength and power, through mixed sports such as team and combat sports, to pure endurance. Where a sport sits sets its main training, the adaptations it seeks and its nutrition. Conditioning
McDonald 2011e
Spotter/loaders
Helpers who load the bar and spot squat and bench; two to five are on the platform at a time in IPF rules. Meet day
Static, dynamic and ballistic stretching
Static stretching holds a muscle at length without moving. Dynamic stretching moves a joint under control through a range that grows with each rep. Ballistic stretching flings the limb through the range and has fallen out of favour. Training basics
McDonald 2009ai
Sticking point
The position in a lift where the bar slows most or where failures happen. Powerlifting
Strategic deconditioning
A planned week or so off, used in one popular plan to undo the repeated bout effect so muscle responds afresh. McDonald's view is that it fails. In an indirect test, three-week breaks in 14 young men matched continuous training, no better. Hypertrophy
McDonald 2008a · Ogasawara 2013
Strides
Short, relaxed accelerations of about 50 to 100 m, or around 20 seconds, at faster than race pace, each followed by a minute or more of rest. Runners do 5 to 10 to practise speed and good form without building fatigue. Conditioning
McDonald 2009v
Subacromial impingement
Pinching of tendons and other soft tissue between the top of the upper arm bone and the bony roof of the shoulder as the arm is raised. A clinician makes the diagnosis. Technique
Kolber 2014
Subcutaneous, visceral and ectopic fat
Subcutaneous fat lies under the skin and can be pinched. Visceral fat surrounds the abdominal organs. Ectopic fat is fat stored inside organs such as the liver. Visceral and ectopic fat carry the most cardiometabolic risk. Body composition
Neeland 2019 · McDonald 2010g
Supercompensation
The idea that training first lowers a capacity, which then rebounds above its starting level before fading again. It fits muscle glycogen well and describes adaptation in general poorly. Recovery
McDonald 2019m
Supraphysiological dose
A dose that lifts a hormone above the range the body makes naturally. Testosterone trials use the term for doses several times those given to treat men with low testosterone. Body composition
Bhasin 1996
Surface EMG (electromyography)
A recording of a muscle's electrical activity through electrodes on the skin. It shows that a muscle is active during a lift; the size of the signal does not reliably predict strength gains or growth. Technique
Vigotsky 2018
Sweet spot training
Andrew Coggan's name for steady work between tempo and threshold effort, usually done in blocks of 10 to 20 minutes with rests half as long. It gives a large aerobic stimulus for the time and is too demanding for beginners. Conditioning
McDonald 2009q
Taper (peaking)
A planned drop in training stress, mostly volume, in the last days or weeks before a meet so fatigue clears and strength shows. Powerlifting
Targeted and cyclical ketogenic diets
Ketogenic diets adapted for hard training. A targeted version adds a small dose of carbohydrate around sessions; a cyclical one alternates several low-carbohydrate days with one or two days of high carbohydrate. Diet
McDonald 2007b
Technical failure
The point in a set where good form can no longer be kept, even if a rougher rep is still possible. On skilled lifts it comes before momentary muscular failure and is the safer place to stop. Training basics
McDonald 2014l
Technique fault
A departure from a lift's general model that build or style does not explain and that limits performance or progress, or raises injury risk. McDonald's working definition; faults come in degrees. Technique
McDonald 2016b
Tempo (intensive endurance)
Steady work in the upper aerobic range: clearly harder than easy pace, yet still a steady state that you could hold for an hour or two. Cyclists call it tempo. Sprint coaches use the same word for sub-maximal repeat runs. Conditioning
McDonald 2009q
Thermic effect of food (TEF)
The energy used to digest, absorb and store food, about 5 to 15 percent of daily energy expenditure on a mixed diet, and higher with protein and alcohol. Nutrition
Thermogenic
A compound or product that raises the body's energy expenditure, often felt as warmth. Most are stimulants. McDonald puts the effect at 5 to 10 percent of energy expenditure at most, which matters little without a diet. Women
McDonald 2015m
Thrifty and spendthrift metabolism
Labels for how energy expenditure responds to intake. In 14 men, 24-hour expenditure rose about 9 percent with two days of double feeding and fell about 9 percent with fasting, and the two responses were correlated between individuals. Body composition
Weyer 2001 · McDonald 2010i
Time-restricted eating
Eating all meals within a set daily window, such as eight hours, and fasting for the rest. It is one form of intermittent fasting. Diet
Training age
How many years a person has been training, as distinct from how old they are. A long training age means a more automatic pattern and tissues adapted to it. Technique
McDonald 2016c
Training variables (loading parameters)
The settings that define a programme: intensity (how heavy), volume (how many sets and reps), frequency (how often) and exercise choice. Rest between sets and speed of movement are often added. Training basics
Triple extension
Straightening the hips, knees and ankles together, as at take-off in a jump or at the top of a weightlifting pull. Technique
Two-a-day training
Two separate sessions in one day with a real break between them. In this guide it means working the same lifts or muscles in both, as distinct from a split that puts different body parts in the morning and the evening. Recovery
McDonald 2014y
Under-recovery
A name some prefer to overtraining. It places the fault in too little sleep, food or rest to absorb the training, and not in the training itself, which may be what the goal requires. Recovery
Kreher 2012 · McDonald 2010n
Valsalva manoeuvre
Trying to breathe out against a closed throat. It raises pressure in the chest and abdomen, which stiffens the trunk and also raises blood pressure. Lifters use a brief, partial version on heavy reps. Technique
Hackett 2013
Velocity-based training (VBT)
Setting load or stopping sets from measured bar speed, using a device that reports mean concentric velocity in metres per second. Powerlifting
VO2 max
The highest rate at which your body can use oxygen during hard exercise, in mL of oxygen per kg of body weight per minute. It is the standard lab measure of aerobic fitness. Health
Volume
The amount of work. In this page, usually the number of hard sets per muscle per week. Volume load is sets times reps times weight. Hypertrophy
Pelland 2026
Volume load (tonnage)
Sets multiplied by reps multiplied by the weight lifted, added up over a session or a week. Three sets of 5 with 100 kg (220 lb) is 1,500 kg (3,300 lb). Training basics
McDonald 2014a
Waist-to-height ratio
Waist circumference divided by height, both in the same units. A ratio of 0.5 means the waist is half your height. Health
NIHCE 2025
Weider principles
Names such as the superset, split routine and drop set principles, attached by the publisher Joe Weider to methods lifters already used. They are labels from his magazines, not discoveries; the hypertrophy area covers what the methods do. Training basics
McDonald 2016k
Whoosh
Informal name for a sudden drop in scale weight, often a kilo or more overnight, after days or weeks with no change on a diet. It is thought to be retained water leaving. The mechanism has not been shown in studies. Body composition
McDonald 2009x
Work capacity
The amount of training you can do and recover from. Beginners build it slowly. Leaving every session exhausted means the workload is ahead of it. Training basics
McDonald 2015f
Working max (training max)
A weight you can repeat on a normal day, often set below a true max, and used as the base for training percentages. Powerlifting
Zone 2
Easy aerobic effort just below the first lactate or ventilatory threshold. You breathe harder but can still speak in sentences. Zone numbers vary between systems. Health
Sitko 2025
