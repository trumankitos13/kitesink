Do you have to train to failure to grow muscle?Overstated

Getting close to failure helps muscle growth, but reaching failure itself showed no clear extra benefit over stopping a little short.

Across training trials, muscle growth rose as sets ended closer to failure, while strength gains were similar across a wide range of effort. A separate meta-analysis found no advantage for sets taken to momentary failure over sets stopped short (effect size 0.12, interval from -0.13 to 0.37). How close people stopped was often estimated, so the exact curve is unclear.

Who was studied. Refalo 2023: 15 studies in healthy adults of any age and experience. Robinson 2024: meta-regressions of training trials, with proximity to failure estimated.

  • Finish most hard sets with a few reps left in reserve, meaning reps you could still have done.
  • If you do go to failure, pick exercises where a missed rep is safe, such as machines or cables.

Do only the last five reps of a set count?Overstated

Research on closeness to failure shows a gradual trend, not a cut-off at five reps.

The 'effective reps' idea says only the final reps before failure build muscle. It draws on muscle-activation work: in 15 untrained women doing lateral raises, activity in a set of about 15 reps climbed and then levelled off over the last 3 to 5 reps before failure. That was activation in one session, not growth. The meta-analyses cited here did not test a five-rep cut-off. They found growth tended to rise as sets ended closer to failure, and one concluded the relationship may not be a straight line. Both rely on rough estimates of reps in reserve.

Who was studied. Refalo 2023: 15 studies in healthy adults. Robinson 2024: exploratory meta-regressions of training trials. Neither tested a five-rep rule. Sundstrup 2012: 15 untrained women, one exercise, EMG only.

  • Take working sets close to failure instead of counting a fixed number of final reps.

Do light weights build as much muscle as heavy ones?Mostly holds

For muscle size, yes when sets are hard; heavier loads still build more one-rep-max strength.

A network meta-analysis of sets taken to failure found no differences in muscle growth between low, moderate and high loads, but strength gains were larger with high and moderate loads than with low. A second meta-analysis of 45 studies found the same split. A third pooled 119 studies on growth and found all prescriptions promoted it comparably. Light sets only work when they are taken close to failure.

Who was studied. Lopez 2021: 28 studies, 747 healthy adults. Refalo 2021: 45 studies. Currier 2023: 119 studies on growth (3,364 people) and 178 on strength (5,097 people).

  • For size, pick any load from heavy to light, as long as the set ends near failure.
  • If you want a bigger one-rep max, include sets above 80% of your max, the range that ranked best for strength.

Does every muscle need training twice a week?Depends

For size, weekly sets matter more than how they are split; for strength, training more often may help, though findings differ.

A meta-regression of 67 studies found growth rose with weekly set volume, while frequency's effect was compatible with negligible effects; a meta-analysis of 25 studies found no growth difference at equal volume. Strength gains rose with frequency, with diminishing returns, though another meta-analysis found no significant strength effect at equal volume. A network analysis ranked twice weekly top for growth. McDonald's view, a practical one: once weekly sets per muscle are high, two or more sessions keep each one manageable.

Who was studied. Pelland 2026: 67 studies, 2,058 participants (about 79% male, average age about 25). Schoenfeld 2019: 25 studies. Ralston 2018: 12 studies on strength. Currier 2023: network analysis of 12 plans.

  • Set your weekly sets first, then choose the number of days that lets you train each session hard.
  • If a muscle gets many hard sets a week, split them over at least two sessions.
  • Add a day if sessions get so long that your effort drops.

Is muscle soreness a sign of a good workout?Overstated

Soreness is a poor guide to muscle damage, and muscle growth does not need it.

In 110 male students doing maximal eccentric arm exercise, soreness correlated weakly or not at all with other markers of damage, and the authors called it a poor reflector of damage. In an eight-week trial, people who ramped up gradually and showed little damage gained the same muscle size and strength as people who started with a damaging bout. A review concluded damage does not mediate growth.

Who was studied. Nosaka 2002: 110 male students, 12 to 60 maximal eccentric arm actions, tracked for 4 days. Flann 2011: pre-trained versus naive adults, 8 weeks. Damas 2018: review.

  • Judge progress by the reps, loads and measurements you log, not by how sore you feel.
  • Ease into a new exercise or a jump in volume so soreness does not cost you sessions.

Does cardio kill your muscle gains?Overstated

Adding cardio did not reduce overall muscle growth in a 43-study review, though a small effect showed at fibre level, mainly with running.

A meta-analysis of 43 studies found adding aerobic work to the same lifting program did not compromise muscle hypertrophy (effect -0.01) or maximal strength, with no significant influence of cardio type (cycling or running) or weekly frequency. Explosive strength gains were reduced (effect -0.28), more so when cardio and lifting shared a session than when they were 3 hours or more apart. A second meta-analysis of 15 studies measuring muscle fibre size found a small negative effect overall (-0.23); for type I fibres it was seen with running but not cycling. The fibre data are preliminary.

Who was studied. Schumann 2022: 43 studies in healthy adults, at least 4 weeks of supervised concurrent training. Lundberg 2022: 15 studies on muscle fibre size, same design.

  • Keep your lifting volume the same when you add cardio and, if you can, leave 3 hours or more between the sessions.

Do you need to change your exercises often to keep growing?Depends

Two small trials gave mixed results: varied exercises may help strength a little, and muscle growth was similar with or without variety.

In 49 active people training twice a week for 12 weeks, groups with constant or varied exercises gained similar thigh muscle size, while the varied-exercise, constant-intensity group gained the most squat strength. In 17 strength-trained men over 9 weeks, choosing exercises session by session produced a small advantage in lean mass and bench press strength over a fixed list. Both trials are small and short.

Who was studied. Fonseca 2014: 49 physically active people, twice a week for 12 weeks. Rauch 2020: 17 strength-trained men, 3 sessions a week for 9 weeks.

  • Keep the main lifts you want to improve, and rotate accessory movements when one stalls or irritates a joint.

Do you need a deload every fourth week?Depends

Deloads are common coaching practice, but outcome trials are few; one found reduced-volume weeks did not cost muscle gain in beginners.

A Delphi panel of expert coaches (34 in round one, 21 in round three) agreed a definition: a period of reduced training stress meant to lower fatigue and prepare for later training. In a survey of 246 competitive strength and physique athletes, all deloaded, on average every 5.6 weeks for about 6 days, mostly planned in advance. Both are opinion and practice, not outcome trials. In one trial, 19 untrained young men trained with or without reduced weeks at weeks 4 and 8; muscle thickness and strength-endurance rose similarly. No study here tests a fixed fourth-week rule in trained lifters.

Who was studied. Bell 2023: 34 expert coaches in round one, 21 in round three. Rogerson 2024: survey of 246 competitive athletes. Pancar 2026: 19 untrained young men, 8 weeks, reduced weeks at 4 and 8.

  • Plan a lighter week about every five or six weeks, and bring it forward if fatigue, sleep or motivation drop.
  • In a deload, cut sets and sessions but keep the same exercises.

Is a calculated one-rep max as good as a tested one?Mostly holds

A set of about 5 reps predicted a max well on two exercises; accuracy fell as reps rose, and the tested lifts were not squat or deadlift.

In 70 adults tested at 1, 5, 10 and 20 reps, a 5-rep test gave the best prediction of a one-rep max on the chest press and the leg press (R2 of 0.993 and 0.974). Typical error was about 3 kg (6.6 lb) on the chest press and 16 kg (36 lb) on the leg press. The authors advise using no more than 10 reps in these equations. Squat and deadlift were not tested.

Who was studied. Reynolds 2006: 70 adults (34 men, 36 women) aged 18 to 69, tested at 1, 5, 10 and 20 reps on the chest press and leg press.

  • Use a set of about 5 hard reps to estimate a max; the more reps, the rougher the estimate.
  • Use the number to choose training loads, and test a heavy single when the exact figure matters.

Do lifting belts weaken your core?Overstated

No cited study shows a lifting belt weakening the core; short trials found little change in muscle activity and more reps with a belt.

In 28 men squatting at 75% of predicted max, a belt alone did not change muscle activity, while abdominal bracing raised abdominal activity with or without a belt. In 49 lifters, a belt raised reps with a 6-rep-max load from 6.5 to 8.1. In 9 adults deadlifting 90% of max, a belt raised intra-abdominal pressure, the usual reason given for wearing one. A review of 35 studies of lumbar supports, which are medical braces and not lifting belts, found inconsistent effects on trunk muscles. The belt studies lasted one or two sessions, so months of belt use are not covered.

Who was studied. Lynch 2025: 28 men, one session. Veltzke 2026: 49 men and women, two sessions a week apart. Harman 1989: 9 adults, one session. Azadinia 2017: review of 35 studies of lumbar supports.

  • Learn to brace your abdomen without a belt, then add a belt for heavy sets if you like.

Do you need to max out in training to get stronger?Overstated

Strength gains were similar across a wide range of effort, so regular true maxes are not required, though practising heavy lifts matters.

A meta-regression found strength gains were similar across a wide range of reps in reserve. A systematic review of six studies found one set of 6 to 12 reps at about 70 to 85% of 1RM, 2 to 3 times a week and near failure, raised squat and bench maxes in resistance-trained men over 8 to 12 weeks. In 38 untrained adults, practising the max test alone matched the strength gain of high-volume training, so skill with the lift counts.

Who was studied. Robinson 2024: meta-regressions of trials. Androulakis-Korakakis 2020: 6 studies in resistance-trained men. Mattocks 2017: 38 untrained adults, 8 weeks.

  • Train most sets with reps left in reserve, and test a max only now and then.
  • Practise heavy singles on your competition lifts before a meet so the feel is familiar.

Are deep squats bad for your knees?Overstated

One literature review found no good evidence that deep squats harm healthy knees and backs when technique is sound and load is built up gradually.

A review of over 164 articles found the compressive force behind the kneecap peaks around 90 degrees of knee bend and falls with deeper bending as the load spreads across more joint surface. It argued that half and quarter squats with very heavy loads may favour joint wear more than deep squats. It stressed learning the lift accurately under expert supervision and adding load progressively. This was biomechanics and review evidence, not a long trial comparing depths.

Who was studied. Hartmann 2013: literature review of over 164 articles on squat biomechanics, including calculations and cadaver joint studies.

  • Squat to a depth you control with good form, and add load gradually.
  • See a clinician or experienced coach if a squat causes knee pain.

Is powerlifting dangerous?Overstated

Injury rates in powerlifting were similar to other non-contact strength sports and low compared with contact sports, though the studies are weak.

A systematic review of 9 studies found 1.0 to 4.4 injuries per 1,000 hours of powerlifting training, and named the spine, shoulder and knee as the most common sites. A review of 20 studies across weight-training sports also found relatively low injury rates versus common team sports. Most studies were low quality or retrospective, and little is known about risk factors.

Who was studied. Aasa 2017: 9 studies in weightlifting and powerlifting. Keogh 2017: 20 studies of injuries in competitive weight-training sports.

  • Build loads gradually and keep technique consistent as the weight gets heavy.
  • Have pain that lasts checked by a clinician rather than lifting through it.

Do you need a periodised program to get stronger?Mostly holds

Planned variation in volume and intensity gave a moderate edge for maximal strength in meta-analyses, and no clear edge for muscle size.

A meta-analysis of 18 studies found 1RM improved more after periodised than non-periodised plans (effect size 0.43), with undulating plans looking more favourable. A second meta-analysis of 35 studies with volume matched found periodisation favoured 1RM strength (0.31) but not muscle size (0.13, interval crossing zero). The edge for undulating over linear plans appeared in trained lifters only.

Who was studied. Williams 2017: 18 studies, 81 effects. Moesgaard 2022: 35 studies with volume equated between plans, strength and size outcomes.

  • Plan changes in load and reps across the weeks instead of repeating the same session.

Is a sumo deadlift allowed in competition?Holds up

Under the IPF rules the deadlift sets no stance, so sumo and conventional are both legal.

The IPF Technical Rules Book (section 4.3) describes the deadlift as the bar laid in front of the feet, gripped with an optional grip and lifted until the lifter stands erect with knees locked. It does not name or restrict stance. Lifts fail for causes such as downward movement of the bar, supporting the bar on the thighs, or stepping or moving the feet laterally. Other federations publish their own books, so check yours.

Who was studied. Not a study. IPF Technical Rules Book effective 1 March 2026, sections 4.3 and 4.3.1.

  • Pick the stance that suits your build and train it, since the same lockout rules apply to both.
  • Read your own federation's rulebook if your meet is not run under IPF rules.

Do you need a singlet at your first meet?Holds up

Under IPF rules, yes: a non-supportive singlet from the approved list is required in Classic (raw) competition.

Section 3.1 requires a one-piece, form-fitting singlet in Classic competitions, with the straps over the shoulders in every lift and legs 3 to 25 cm (1.2 to 9.8 in) long. It must come from a manufacturer on the approved list. A t-shirt is worn under it, and the same type of singlet is worn for all three lifts. At weigh-in, clothing is limited to the singlet, one approved t-shirt (if the division uses one) and standard underwear. Local meets may set their own entry rules.

Who was studied. Not a study. IPF Technical Rules Book effective 1 March 2026, sections 3.1, 3.2 and 5.

  • Buy a singlet from your federation's approved list well before the meet and train in it once or twice.
  • Check your meet's entry details for gear rules.

Can you wear any lifting belt you like at an IPF meet?Not supported

No: the belt must come from the approved list and meet size and construction limits, including a maximum width of 10 cm (3.9 in).

Section 3.6 allows a belt, worn outside the suit, only from manufacturers on the approved list. The main body must be non-stretch leather, vinyl or similar, with no added padding, bracing or supports. Maximum width is 10 cm (3.9 in) and maximum thickness 13 mm (0.5 in) along the main length. The buckle may have one or two prongs or a quick-release lever.

Who was studied. Not a study. IPF Technical Rules Book effective 1 March 2026, section 3.6.

  • Check the approved list before buying a belt for a meet.
  • Measure the width and thickness of any belt you already own.

Can you change an attempt after you hand it in?Depends

It depends on the attempt: the first can be changed once, second attempts cannot be changed, and the third deadlift can be changed twice.

Under section 6.1, you declare starting weights at weigh-in. One change of weight is allowed on the first attempt of each lift, higher or lower, up to three minutes before the first round of that lift begins for the first group. Second-round weights cannot be changed. Third attempts on squat and bench cannot be changed. On the third deadlift two changes are allowed if the bar is not yet loaded and called. Hand in each next attempt within one minute.

Who was studied. Not a study. IPF Technical Rules Book effective 1 March 2026, section 6.1 (the round system).

  • Agree your three attempts with your coach before the meet, and decide in advance what would change them.
  • Hand in your second and third attempts within one minute of the lights, or the rules set the weight for you.

Is a water cut a good idea for a first meet?Not supported

Dehydration cut strength in a meta-analysis, and an IPF weigh-in about two hours before lifting leaves little time to recover, so a first meet is a poor place to try one.

A meta-analysis of 28 papers found dehydration reduced muscle endurance by 8.3%, strength by 5.5% and anaerobic power by 5.8% on average, with smaller drops in trained people. IPF weigh-in takes place no earlier than two hours before the session, over a 90-minute window. Large fluid losses carry health risks, so talk to an experienced coach and a clinician before attempting any cut.

Who was studied. Savoie 2015: 28 papers on dehydration and muscle performance. IPF Technical Rules Book effective 1 March 2026, section 5 (weighing in).

  • Enter the class you already weigh near instead of cutting.
  • If you want a different class, plan a gradual change over months with a coach.

Will one bad night's sleep before a meet ruin your lifts?Depends

One short night often has little effect on strength, but sleep restricted over several nights, and afternoon sessions after a bad night, showed bigger drops.

A meta-analysis of 69 publications found sleep loss lowered performance across exercise types (average drop 7.56%), with consistent effects only after deprivation or late restriction. Tasks done in the afternoon were consistently affected, and tasks done in the morning were largely unaffected. A systematic review of 17 studies found total sleep deprivation had little effect on strength, while consecutive nights of restricted sleep could reduce force in multi-joint lifts.

Who was studied. Craven 2022: 69 publications, 227 outcome measures, mostly male participants. Knowles 2018: 17 studies of sleep loss and resistance exercise.

  • Protect your sleep across the whole week before the meet, not just the last night.
  • If you sleep badly the night before, keep to your usual routine and warm-up.

Do you have to eat protein within an hour of training?Overstated

No beneficial effect of a specific protein timing showed up on lean mass or strength, so total daily protein matters more.

A systematic review of 65 trials (2,907 participants) found protein supplementation raised lean mass by 0.62 kg (1.4 lb) in adults, independent of when it was taken, with no beneficial effect of a specific timing on lean mass, handgrip or leg-press strength. The ISSN position stand says the best time is likely a matter of individual tolerance, and that the anabolic effect of exercise lasts at least 24 hours but likely diminishes as time passes after training.

Who was studied. Wirth 2020: 65 trials with 2,907 healthy adults. Jager 2017: ISSN position stand reviewing the protein and exercise literature.

  • Hit your daily protein target first.
  • Eat a protein-rich meal before or after training whenever it suits you.

Can your body only use 30 grams of protein per meal?Not supported

In one tracer study, 100 g of protein gave a bigger and longer muscle response than 25 g, so a fixed per-meal cap is not supported.

In 36 young men after whole-body exercise, 100 g of milk protein, which is mostly slow-digesting casein, produced a greater and more prolonged (over 12 hours) anabolic response than 25 g, with a negligible effect on amino acid oxidation. This measured short-term protein synthesis, not muscle gained over months. The ISSN suggests 20 to 40 g per serving, spread every 3 to 4 hours, as a practical guide.

Who was studied. Trommelen 2023: 36 healthy, recreationally active young men, one feeding of 0, 25 or 100 g protein after exercise, followed for 12 hours.

  • Use 20 to 40 g per meal as a practical guide for spreading protein through the day.
  • A larger protein meal now and then is not wasted.

Does a high-protein diet harm healthy kidneys?Not supported

In 28 trials of adults without kidney disease, higher protein did not change the drop or rise in kidney filtration rate compared with lower protein.

A meta-analysis defined high protein as at least 1.5 g per kg of body weight, at least 20% of energy, or at least 100 g a day. The change in glomerular filtration rate, a measure of kidney filtering, did not differ between higher and lower protein diets, and the authors concluded high intakes did not adversely affect kidney function in healthy adults. It included trials as short as 5 days and noted an unclear risk of selection bias. It says nothing about people with kidney disease.

Who was studied. Devries 2018: 28 randomised trials, 1,358 adults without kidney disease, high versus normal or lower protein for more than 4 days.

  • If you have kidney disease or a family history of it, ask your doctor before changing your protein intake.

Does a bigger calorie surplus build more muscle?Overstated

In one small trial, eating much more mostly added body fat, not muscle, and the best surplus size is still unknown.

In an eight-week trial of 21 trained lifters (17 finished) at maintenance, a 5% surplus or a 15% surplus, there was no evidence of group differences in muscle thickness or squat max. Faster body-mass gain mainly predicted skinfold (fat) gain, though the 15% group did raise bench press more. A review says the surplus needed to support muscle gain is unknown and has not been validated in lifters.

Who was studied. Helms 2023: 21 trained lifters randomised to maintenance, 5% or 15% surplus for 8 weeks, 17 completers. Slater 2019: narrative review.

  • Start with a small surplus and adjust to your progress instead of eating as much as you can.

Is creatine bad for your kidneys?Not supported

In studies of healthy people, creatine did not damage the kidneys at the amounts and durations tested.

A systematic review and meta-analysis (15 studies in the review, 6 in the pooled analysis) concluded creatine supplementation does not induce renal damage in the studied amounts and durations. The ISSN position stand states that short and long-term supplementation, up to 30 g a day for 5 years, is safe and well tolerated in healthy people. The pooled analysis had only 6 studies, and none of this speaks for people with kidney disease.

Who was studied. De Souza 2019: 15 studies, 6 pooled. Kreider 2017: ISSN review covering healthy people taking up to 30 g a day for 5 years.

  • If you have kidney disease or take medicines that affect the kidneys, ask your doctor before using creatine.

Does creatine cause hair loss?Overstated

The worry comes from one small study showing higher DHT; a 12-week trial that measured hair found no difference from placebo.

The concern is that creatine raises dihydrotestosterone (DHT), a hormone linked to hair loss. In 20 rugby players, creatine raised DHT by 56% after a loading week and 40% after maintenance, but hair was not measured. In a 12-week trial of 45 resistance-trained men, 5 g a day made no difference to DHT, hair density, follicle count or thickness. Both studies were in young men, and only one trial measured hair.

Who was studied. Lak 2025: 45 resistance-trained men (38 finished), 5 g a day or placebo for 12 weeks. Van der Merwe 2009: 20 college rugby players, 3 weeks, crossover.

  • See a doctor or dermatologist for new hair loss, since creatine is one of many possible causes.

Do you need supplements to make progress?Not supported

The IOC says supplements make a minor contribution and that only a few have good evidence of benefit.

The IOC consensus statement on supplements for high-performance athletes says nutrition usually makes a small but valuable contribution to performance, and supplements a minor part of that. Only a few, including caffeine, creatine, specific buffering agents and nitrate, have good evidence. Some products can harm health, and inadvertently taking banned substances is a known risk. It advises a full nutritional assessment before any decision.

Who was studied. Maughan 2018: IOC consensus statement by 25 authors on supplements for high-performance athletes. It is an expert review, not a trial.

  • Fix training, protein, total food and sleep first, and add a supplement only if a gap remains.
  • If you compete in a tested federation, check any product against its anti-doping rules.

Does caffeine help you lift more?Mostly holds

Caffeine gave a small boost to strength and power in a meta-analysis, clearer for upper-body strength than lower-body.

Can a calorie calculator tell you exactly how much to eat?Not supported

No. Even the best equation misses by more than 10 percent for a share of people, so treat it as a starting point.

A systematic review compared four equations for resting metabolic rate, the energy you burn at rest. Mifflin-St Jeor predicted within 10 percent of the measured value for more people than the others, and it still missed for a share of individuals. Daily need is that figure times an activity factor, and FAO/WHO/UNU gives about 1.40 to 2.40 as the range sustained by free-living adults. On a 2,400 kcal day, 10 percent is 240 kcal.

Who was studied. Frankenfield: systematic review of four equations in healthy adults with and without obesity. FAO/WHO/UNU: expert report on human energy requirements.

  • Eat your estimate for two to three weeks while weighing yourself daily.
  • Adjust by 100 to 200 kcal if the weekly average moves the wrong way.

How much protein do you need to build muscle?Mostly holds

About 1.6 g per kg a day covers most lifters, returns shrink above that, and needs may be higher while dieting.

The ISSN position stand calls 1.4 to 2.0 g per kg a day sufficient for most exercising people, and says 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg may be needed to keep lean mass when resistance-trained people cut calories. A meta-analysis of 74 trials found added protein helped lean mass in adults under 65 who ate 1.6 g per kg or more. A dose-response review of 105 articles (5,402 participants) found each extra 0.1 g per kg added 0.39 kg of lean mass below 1.3 g per kg a day and 0.12 kg above it.

Who was studied. Nunes: 74 trials of added protein with resistance exercise. Tagawa: 105 articles, 5,402 participants. Jager: the ISSN position stand.

  • Hit the daily total before you worry about timing or splits.
  • During a calorie deficit, aim for the top of that range or above it.

Will drinking alcohol ruin your gains?Depends

A very heavy dose after training blunted muscle repair in one small study, and light drinking has not been tested.

Eight active men trained, then drank alcohol at 1.5 g per kg (about 12 standard drinks) with protein or carbohydrate. Muscle protein synthesis was 24 percent lower with alcohol and protein, and 37 percent lower with alcohol and carbohydrate, than with protein alone. A meta-analysis of 22 studies (701 participants) found people did not eat less to offset alcohol: total energy intake rose by 1,072 kJ (about 256 kcal).

Can plant protein build as much muscle as animal protein?Mostly holds

When total protein is enough, strength gains match, and soy matched milk protein for muscle in pooled trials. Some other plant proteins fell behind.

A meta-analysis of 18 studies found protein source did not change absolute lean mass or strength, though younger adults gained 0.41 kg more lean mass on animal protein. A 2025 meta-analysis of 30 trials found a small animal advantage for muscle mass, with no difference between soy and milk across 17 trials, and no difference in strength. In a 12-week trial, 19 vegans and 19 omnivores on about 1.6 g per kg gained alike. One trial differed: in 56 novice men training for 12 weeks, milk after sessions built more lean mass than a matched soy drink, with equal strength gains.

Who was studied. Lim: 18 studies reviewed. Reid-McCann: 30 RCTs pooled, split by age and protein source. Hevia: 19 vegans and 19 omnivores for 12 weeks. Hartman: 56 young novice men, 12 weeks.

  • Lean on soy and mix legumes and grains across the day.

Does intermittent fasting burn more fat than ordinary dieting?Overstated

With calories matched, trials of time-restricted eating mostly found no significant extra weight loss, though one meta-analysis found a small edge.

In a 12-month trial of 139 adults, time-restricted eating lost 8.0 kg (17.6 lb) versus 6.3 kg (13.9 lb) with calorie restriction, not a significant gap. In a 12-week feeding trial with matched calories, weight fell 2.3 versus 2.6 kg. A 12-week trial of 16:8 eating found no difference between groups. Do six small meals a day speed up your metabolism?Not supported

Eating more often did not raise energy expenditure, and fewer meals did not hurt; meal count mainly affects hunger.

A network meta-analysis of 22 trials (647 participants) found that, on equal calories, 2 meals a day probably reduced body weight slightly versus 3 meals (-1.02 kg) or 6 meals (-1.29 kg), and energy intake was not affected. Is fasted cardio better for fat loss?Overstated

Fasted exercise burns slightly more fat during the session, but body-mass and body-fat changes were similar in the small review that tracked them.

A meta-analysis of 27 studies (273 participants) found fat oxidation during exercise was higher fasted, by about 3 g. A systematic review of 5 studies (96 participants) found trivial differences in weight and body composition, while warning that the data were limited; its body-fat and lean-mass results came from women only. In a trial that controlled the diet, 20 young women did an hour of cardio 3 times a week for 4 weeks, and fasted and fed groups lost similar fat. McDonald's view is that these women were not lean, so a small benefit for very lean dieters is untested rather than disproven.

Who was studied. Hackett 2017: 5 studies, 96 healthy adults, body-fat and lean-mass results in women only. Vieira 2016: 27 studies, 273 adults. Schoenfeld 2014: 20 young women, 4 weeks, diet controlled.

  • Train at the time of day you will stick to, since the evidence shows no clear fat-loss edge from fasting.

Do diet breaks speed up fat loss?Depends

Results are mixed: one trial found more fat loss with breaks, two syntheses found no difference in fat lost, with less hunger or less metabolic slowing.

In 51 men with obesity, 2-week diet blocks alternating with 2-week breaks gave more fat loss among the 36 who completed the protocol (12.3 versus 8.0 kg). In 61 resistance-trained adults over 12 weeks, 3-week diet blocks with 1-week breaks gave no difference in fat or lean mass but lower hunger. A meta-analysis of 12 trials found no group difference in body measures, and a smaller drop in resting metabolic rate with breaks.

Who was studied. Byrne 2018: 51 men with obesity randomised, 16 weeks of dieting. Peos 2021: 61 resistance-trained adults, 12 weeks. Poon 2025: 12 trials, 881 participants.

If you track everything and cannot lose weight, is your metabolism broken?Overstated

In one careful study, people who said they could not lose weight had normal energy expenditure and were eating more than they reported.

In 10 adults with a history of diet resistance, measured energy expenditure was within 5 percent of predicted. They under-reported food intake by 47% on average and over-reported activity by 51%. Does starvation mode stop weight loss?Overstated

Energy expenditure does fall during a diet, at rest and in non-resting activity. That slows weight loss, but no study here shows it stopping loss.

In 171 overweight women who lost an average of 12 kg, metabolic adaptation was about 54 kcal a day after weight loss and was not present at 1 or 2 years in a subset. In 32 men on a half-calorie diet for 3 weeks, resting energy expenditure fell 266 kcal a day, of which 108 kcal was adaptive thermogenesis (72 kcal after accounting for lean mass). Does spot reduction work?Not supported

Training one body part did not reduce fat there in most studies, though one small trial of overweight men found otherwise.

A meta-analysis of 13 controlled studies (1,158 participants) comparing trained and untrained limbs found no local effect (pooled effect size -0.03). Six weeks of abdominal exercise in 24 sedentary adults did not reduce abdominal fat, and 12 weeks of single-leg training reduced arm and trunk fat but not leg fat. A 2023 trial of 16 overweight men found more trunk fat loss when abdominal work replaced part of the running, with the two programmes designed to use the same energy from pilot measurements. One small trial does not outweigh the meta-analysis, so the question is not fully closed.

Who was studied. Ramirez-Campillo 2022: 13 studies, 1,158 participants. Vispute 2011: 24 adults, 6 weeks. Ramirez-Campillo 2013: 11 adults, 12 weeks. Brobakken 2023: 16 men, 10 weeks.

Can you out-train a bad diet?Overstated

In pooled trials, exercise alone produced modest weight loss, so it is a weak tool for offsetting a poor diet.

Do nuts make you put on weight?Not supported

In trials, adding nuts did not raise body weight, and people who ate more nuts were slightly less likely to become overweight.

A 2021 meta-analysis of 86 randomized trials (5,873 people) found no effect of nuts on body weight (0.09 kg, interval -0.09 to 0.27). Across six cohorts of about 570,000 people, nut eaters had a 7% lower risk of overweight or obesity. Part of the energy is never absorbed: in 18 adults, almonds yielded 4.6 kcal per gram against 6.0 to 6.1 predicted, so the standard factors overestimated them by 32%. Lyle McDonald’s view is that much of the effect relies on people eating less later in the day, so large amounts eaten on top of a full diet can still add weight.

Who was studied. Nishi 2021: 86 RCTs (5,873 people) and 6 prospective cohorts (569,910 people). Novotny 2012: 18 adults, crossover with 0, 42 or 84 g of almonds a day, 18 days each.

  • Weigh a portion now and then; a 28 g (1 oz) serving is a small handful.
  • Eat nuts in place of another snack rather than in addition to it.

Do diet drinks with artificial sweeteners make you gain weight?Not supported

Can a fitness watch tell you how many calories you burned?Overstated

Wrist devices track heart rate fairly well, but in a lab test of seven wrist devices, none estimated calories within 20%.

In 60 adults measured against lab equipment while sitting, walking, running and cycling, six of seven wrist devices kept median heart-rate error under 5% on the bike, yet none estimated energy use within 20%. A meta-analysis of 60 validation studies found accuracy varied widely with the activity, and adding heart rate to motion sensing helped. Who was studied. Shcherbina 2017: 60 adults, 7 wrist devices, lab tests against indirect calorimetry. O'Driscoll 2020: 60 validation studies of wrist and arm monitors in healthy adults.

Is it safe to work out when you have a cold?Depends

In young adults, moderate exercise during a mild head cold did not make it worse or longer in a controlled trial. Fever and chest symptoms were not tested.

Are muscle cramps during exercise caused by dehydration or low salt?Overstated

In a large race, cramping went with faster pacing and a history of cramps, not with fluid loss or blood salt levels.

In 210 Ironman triathletes, the 43 who cramped did not differ significantly in body-weight or electrolyte changes from the 166 who did not. Can you build muscle on a ketogenic diet?Depends

People doing resistance training on ketogenic diets lost more fat and more fat-free mass; in trained lifters without planned restriction, no clear difference.

Do you gain less muscle and strength if you lift in the morning?Not supported

Morning and evening training built similar strength and muscle, though strength is on average higher in the evening at the start.

Does keeping your home cold help you burn off fat?Overstated

Mild cold raises energy use by a few percent, but studies of people who got used to the cold found little change in body weight.

In 25 adults, 12 hours at 19 °C (66 °F) instead of 24 °C (75 °F) raised energy expenditure by about 6% with no significant change in spontaneous movement. In healthy adults with low brown fat activity, two hours a day at 17 °C (63 °F) for six weeks raised brown fat activity and lowered body fat. A 2020 review found the rise in human energy use consistently small, and cold-acclimation studies produced minimal changes in body weight; only two studies measured how much people ate in the cold. Lyle McDonald doubts most people would stay that cold every day for a small burn, and notes that cold can raise hunger.

Does skipping meals or fasting for a day slow your metabolism?Not supported

Resting energy use did not fall after a day of very low intake, and it rose over the first three days of a total fast.

In 11 lean, healthy adults on an 84-hour fast, resting energy expenditure rose from 3.97 to 4.53 kJ a minute by day 3; noradrenaline more than doubled by day 4. In 8 men with overweight or obesity, a day at 25% of energy needs did not change energy use over 4 hours after breakfast the next morning, and appetite and the amount eaten in the following two days matched a normal day. The slower metabolism of a long diet builds over weeks (see the starvation mode claim). Lyle McDonald's view is that the body's adjustments lag by days in both directions, so one high-calorie day does not reverse them either.

Is eating extra fat the way to burn body fat?Not supported

Cutting carbohydrate raises the fat you burn, yet on equal calories cutting fat removed more body fat in a ward study.

The study cut fat or carbohydrate; it did not add fat. Nineteen adults with obesity on a metabolic ward ate two reduced-calorie diets with equal calories, each for six days, in random order. Cutting carbohydrate raised fat burning and lost 53 g of body fat a day by fat balance; cutting fat lost 89 g, because less fat was coming in, and the authors’ model predicts the gap narrows over time. Lyle McDonald’s view is that the shift to fat burning on low-carb diets comes from the missing carbohydrate, not the added fat. Over months the two diet styles lose similar weight (see the low-carb claim).

Who was studied. Hall 2015: 19 adults with obesity on a metabolic ward, crossover, two 6-day reduced-calorie diets with equal calories, body fat loss from fat balance.

  • Pick the carbohydrate and fat split you can keep to, and set calories first.

Is a food labelled zero calories truly calorie-free?Not supported

US labels may show 0 calories for anything under 5 a serving and 0 g fat for under 0.5 g, so large or repeated servings still add energy.

Under the US labelling rule, calories are rounded to the nearest 5 up to 50 and to the nearest 10 above that, and a serving with fewer than 5 calories may be shown as zero. Total fat under 0.5 g a serving is shown as 0 g. So adding up a label's protein, carbohydrate and fat seldom matches its calorie line exactly. Calories count as misbranded when a lab composite runs more than 20% above the label. Lyle McDonald's point is that rounding matters little unless 'zero' foods are used freely and left out of the log. Rules differ in other countries.

Who was studied. US Code of Federal Regulations, 21 CFR 101.9 (nutrition labeling of food), as published, retrieved November 2026.

  • Weigh and log foods you use in quantity even when the label says zero.
  • Expect small gaps between a label's macros and its calories; they come from rounding.

Can you use your running heart rate zones on the bike?Not supported

Heart rate at threshold on a treadmill did not predict heart rate at threshold on a bike, so each sport needs its own test.

In 371 people who did graded tests on both a treadmill and a bike within three weeks, heart rate at the individual anaerobic threshold, and at a blood lactate of 4 mmol/L, did not correlate between the two. Lyle McDonald could not confirm the gym rule that threshold sits 10 beats lower on the bike; his view is that people test best in the activity they train most, and that running uses more muscle.

Is plain water the best drink after a sweaty session?Depends

Training the free limb reduced strength loss in a limb held still, with a smaller effect on muscle size, in healthy volunteers.
