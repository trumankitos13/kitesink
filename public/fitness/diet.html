Deficit and rate of lossThe 3,500 kcal per pound rule is a rough guide that varies with body fatAbout 3,500 kcal (7,700 per kg) per pound of loss is only an average, and leaner people need less per unit lost.BeginnerSome support

The rule says a cumulative deficit of 3,500 kcal equals one pound (32.2 MJ per kg) of weight loss. A model found it approximately matches obese people with more than 30 kg of body fat, and it overestimates the deficit needed for people with less body fat. Weight also responds slowly: the half time was about one year in a separate model. Use it to size a plan, then correct against the scale.

  • Use the rule to pick a starting deficit, then check the trend over three to four weeks.

Deficit and rate of lossAim to lose about 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight a weekA pace of 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight a week is the usual advice for keeping muscle while getting leaner.IntermediateSome support

A contest-prep review recommends 0.5 to 1 percent a week, and a later review prefers 0.5 percent or less for attenuating fat-free mass loss. In 24 athletes, both groups lost about 5.5 percent of body weight, but the 0.7 percent weekly group gained 2.1 percent lean mass and lost 31 percent of fat mass, while the group set 1.4 percent (1.0 percent achieved) kept lean mass unchanged and lost 21 percent of fat mass. McDonald's view is that faster loss mainly costs muscle when protein is low and lifting is light. At 80 kg (176 lb), 0.5 to 1 percent is 0.4 to 0.8 kg (0.9 to 1.8 lb).

  • Pick a weekly percentage, convert it to kg or lb, and check against your four-week trend.

Deficit and rate of lossDeficits much beyond 500 kcal a day cost lean mass in liftersIn a meta-regression, a deficit of about 500 kcal a day stopped lean-mass gains from training, while strength held up.IntermediateWell supported

A systematic review and meta-analysis of resistance training in an energy deficit found lean-mass gains were impaired against training without a deficit (effect size -0.57), but strength gains were comparable. The meta-regression put the point where gains were prevented at roughly 500 kcal a day. For people trying to preserve lean mass rather than add it, the authors advise avoiding deficits above that size. Short trials can differ: 40 young men on a 40 percent deficit with intense training gained 1.2 kg of lean mass on 2.4 g/kg of protein in 4 weeks.

  • Keep deficits at or below about 500 kcal a day if gaining or keeping muscle is the priority.

Murphy 2022 · Longland 2016

Deficit and rate of lossSize the deficit as a share of maintenance, not a fixed calorie numberA percentage cut scales with the person: 20 percent is 360 kcal a day at 1,800 kcal maintenance and 600 kcal at 3,000.BeginnerCoaching practice

A fixed 500 kcal cut is more than a quarter of a small woman's intake and a sixth of a big man's. McDonald sets deficits as a share of maintenance and calls 10 to 15 percent small, 20 to 25 percent moderate and anything beyond 25 percent large. The same percentage gives a larger person a bigger daily gap, so they lose faster on paper. At high maintenance levels a 25 percent cut can pass the roughly 500 kcal point where lean-mass gains stopped in a meta-regression.

  • Estimate maintenance, take 20 to 25 percent off, and compare the result with the 500 kcal guide.

Deficit and rate of lossLosing over 1 percent of body weight a week without high protein and heavy liftingBigger deficits take a larger share of the loss from lean mass, and athletes who lost more slowly gained lean mass. Going faster needs high protein and heavy lifting.MistakeBeginnerSome support

In 24 athletes lifting four times a week, a group set to lose 1.4 percent a week (1.0 percent achieved) kept lean mass unchanged and cut fat mass 21 percent, while a 0.7 percent loss gained 2.1 percent lean mass and cut fat mass 31 percent. A review found that lean tissue makes up a growing share of the loss as the deficit deepens, and a bigger share in leaner people. McDonald's view is that the old warnings about speed came from low-protein diets without lifting, and that lean dieters with high protein and heavy loads can go faster for short spells. Reviews recommend 0.5 to 1 percent a week.

  • Use 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight a week as the default pace.
  • If you go faster, keep protein high, keep lifting heavy and keep the fast spell short.

Deficit and rate of lossRunning a 10 percent deficit while logging food looselyA deficit of 200 to 300 kcal a day is about the size of ordinary label and logging error, so it can vanish without you noticing.MistakeIntermediateSome support

US rules let a label run up to 20 percent over the declared calories, and 19 percent of 269 restaurant items held at least 100 kcal more per portion than stated. A review of 59 studies against doubly labelled water found most report eating less than they do. On 2,000 kcal a day, a 10 percent error cancels a 10 percent deficit. McDonald's view is that small deficits suit lean, experienced dieters who weigh their food; for others a larger cut leaves room for error.

  • Weigh food and log everything if you choose a small deficit.
  • If your logging is rough, use a 20 to 25 percent cut instead.

Keeping muscleEat more protein when you diet: about 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of lean massLean lifters in a deficit kept more muscle with higher protein, around 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of fat-free mass.IntermediateWell supported

A review of six studies in lean, resistance-trained athletes in a deficit put protein needs at 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of fat-free mass, scaled up with the severity of the deficit and the leanness. The ISSN position gives 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of body weight. In a 4-week trial of 40 young men on a large deficit, 2.4 g/kg gained 1.2 kg of lean mass against 0.1 kg on 1.2 g/kg. A meta-analysis of 24 trials found higher protein held on to 0.43 kg more fat-free mass.

  • Set protein by your lean mass, and move up as the diet gets harder and you get leaner.

Helms 2014a · Jäger 2017 · Longland 2016 · Wycherley 2012

Keeping muscleKeep lifting while dieting, because training tells the body to keep muscleResistance training reduced the lean mass lost in a diet, and strength was largely kept in deficit trials.BeginnerWell supported

An overview of 12 systematic reviews (149 studies) found resistance training limited lean mass loss during weight loss by about 0.8 kg. In six trials of older adults with obesity, training offset about 93 percent of the lean mass lost to calorie restriction. Strength gains in the deficit trials were comparable with and without a deficit.

  • Keep the main lifts heavy, and drop sets before you drop load.

Bellicha 2021 · Sardeli 2018 · Murphy 2022

Keeping muscleShort sleep shifts diet loss toward muscle and away from fatIn ten adults, five and a half hours of sleep cut the fat share of weight lost by 55 percent.IntermediateThin evidence

Ten overweight adults ate a reduced-calorie diet for 14 days in two conditions, with 8.5 or 5.5 hours in bed. Short sleep cut the fraction of weight lost as fat by 55 percent (1.4 vs 0.6 kg) and increased loss of fat-free mass by 60 percent (1.5 vs 2.4 kg). Hunger rose too. It is a very small and short crossover study, so treat it as a strong hint and not a finding.

  • Protect seven to nine hours in bed while dieting.

Keeping muscleCutting protein and stopping lifting while dietingThe two habits that protect muscle in a deficit are protein and heavy training, and dropping both costs lean mass.MistakeBeginnerSome support

Higher protein held on to lean mass in trials of lean lifters, and training reduced lean loss by about 0.8 kg across an overview of reviews.

  • Keep protein high and lifts heavy until the diet ends.

Helms 2014a · Bellicha 2021

Keeping muscleSetting protein as a percentage of calories during a dietA fixed share of calories gives less protein as calories fall, just when a deficit raises the need. Set protein in grams per kg instead.MistakeIntermediateSome support

At 25 percent of calories, protein is 175 g on 2,800 kcal but 125 g on 2,000 kcal, a drop of 50 g. A review of lean, resistance-trained athletes in a deficit found the need rises with the size of the deficit and with leanness, and set it per kg of fat-free mass. McDonald makes the same point for every nutrient: needs follow body size, so a percentage means little until you know the calories behind it. Fix protein in grams first and share out the rest.

  • Fix protein in grams first, for example 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of lean mass for a lean lifter in a deficit.

Helms 2014a · McDonald 2008j · McDonald 2007a

Hunger and fullnessProtein and unprocessed food help control hunger, at least in the short runProtein reduced hunger in meal studies, and a ward trial found unprocessed meals led to about 500 kcal less intake.BeginnerSome support

A meta-analysis of 49 acute studies found protein lowered hunger and raised fullness, but long-term trials were inconclusive. In a ward study of 20 adults, ultra-processed meals led to 508 kcal a day more intake than unprocessed meals matched on presented calories, sugar, fibre and sodium, with weight up 0.9 kg against down 0.9 kg over two weeks. That is one small, short crossover trial.

  • Build meals around protein and whole foods before cutting portion size.

Kohanmoo 2020 · Hall 2019

Hunger and fullnessAppetite rises as you lose weight, so hunger is part of the processIn one 52-week trial, appetite rose enough to add about 100 kcal a day per kg of weight lost.IntermediateThin evidence

In 153 people losing weight without being aware of the deficit, a model found eating drifted above baseline by about 100 kcal a day for each kilogram lost, more than three times the matching fall in energy expenditure. This is one analysis of one drug trial. It points to appetite, not metabolism, as the main pressure against keeping weight off.

  • Plan for hunger to rise late in a diet, and use protein, fibre and a fixed end date.

Polidori 2016

Hunger and fullnessWater-rich fruit and vegetables lower hunger for the same caloriesIn one year-long trial, women who ate more water-rich foods lost more weight and reported less hunger.BeginnerThin evidence

In 97 women with obesity told to cut fat, one group was also told to eat more water-rich foods, mainly fruit and vegetables, with no calorie goal. Among completers after a year, that group lost 7.9 kg against 6.4 kg, ate a greater weight of food at a lower energy density, and reported less hunger. It is one trial in women with obesity, not lifters.

  • Fill half the plate with vegetables or fruit before adding the rest.

Ello-Martin 2007 · Hall 2019

Hunger and fullnessFlexible control of eating goes with lower weight and fewer binges than rigid rulesIn large surveys, people who allowed limited amounts of any food weighed less and binged less than people who ran on strict food rules.BeginnerSome support

Among 54,517 people in a weight-loss programme and a population sample of 1,838 aged 14 and over, rigid control was linked with higher BMI and more frequent, more severe binges. Flexible control was linked with lower BMI, fewer binges and a better chance of losing weight over a year. In 188 non-obese women, rigid dieting went with eating disorder symptoms and low mood, and flexible dieting did not. These are associations, not trials, so they show who does well rather than proving the cause.

  • Plan some favourite foods into the week instead of banning them.
  • If food rules or binges feel out of your control, see a doctor or an eating disorder specialist.

Westenhoefer 1999 · Stewart 2002 · McDonald 2008v

Hunger and fullnessTreating one off-plan snack as a ruined dietA 200 kcal treat is under a tenth of a 3,500 kcal weekly deficit. Giving up on the day because of it is what costs you.MistakeBeginnerCoaching practice

All-or-nothing rules make a small lapse feel like failure, and the usual response is to eat freely until the day or the week is over. Rigid control was linked with more frequent and more severe binges in a large survey. McDonald's view is that the snack itself does little harm, especially if the next meals are a little smaller, and that calling planned meals off the diet 'cheats' keeps the good-or-bad framing that drives the spiral. He uses planned free meals and diet breaks instead.

  • After an unplanned treat, eat the next meal as planned.
  • Call planned off-plan meals free meals, and plan them in advance.

Westenhoefer 1999 · McDonald 2008v · McDonald 2009a

Hunger and fullnessDropping whole fruit from a fat-loss dietIn a six-week trial in adults with obesity, a calorie-restricted diet with fruit lost more weight than one that kept fructose very low.MistakeBeginnerThin evidence

In 131 adults, mostly women, on restricted diets of 1,500 to 2,000 kcal, a group eating under 20 g of fructose a day lost 2.83 kg, while a group getting 50 to 70 g from natural fruit lost 4.19 kg. It was one short trial, and the authors still advised less added fructose. Water-rich foods such as fruit also lowered hunger in a year-long trial (see the energy density entry). McDonald's view is that whole fruit helps many dieters feel fuller, while juice gives the sugar without the bulk.

  • Keep a few portions of whole fruit in the day and count them in your calories.
  • Choose whole fruit over juice.

Madero 2011 · Ello-Martin 2007 · McDonald 2009a

Scale, plateaus and metabolismMetabolic slowdown with dieting is real but smaller than most people fearAdaptation is real but small, tens to low hundreds of kcal a day in most studies, so check your log first.IntermediateSome support

A systematic review of 33 studies (2,528 participants) found adaptive thermogenesis in 27, with lower or non-significant values in the better-designed studies. In 171 women, the drop after loss was 54 kcal a day, and in a subset of 46 it was no longer significant at one and two years. In 32 men on a 50 percent deficit for three weeks, true adaptation was 72 kcal a day. After 6 years, 14 Biggest Loser contestants were 499 kcal a day below prediction, an extreme case.

  • Check the log and weekends before blaming metabolism.

Nunes 2022a · Martins 2020 · Müller 2015 · Fothergill 2016

Scale, plateaus and metabolismDay-to-day weight changes are mostly water, so read the trendTwo-week changes of 1 to 3 kg were largely fat-free mass and water, not fat, in free-living adults.BeginnerSome support

In 46 adults, a unit of two-week weight change was 84 percent fat-free mass, and the energy density of 1 to 3 kg changes was about 2,380 kcal per kg, well below that of fat. Glycogen is stored with three to four parts water. Weight runs higher on Sunday and Monday in 80 adults, and fluid retention peaks at the start of menstrual flow in 62 women. None of these is fat gain.

  • Weigh at the same time under the same conditions, and judge the weekly average.
  • If you menstruate, compare the same week of one cycle with the same week of the next.

Bhutani 2017 · Kreitzman 1992 · Orsama 2014 · White 2011

Scale, plateaus and metabolismA two-week stall is not proof your diet stopped workingCheck logging and weekend intake before cutting calories, since stalls are usually noise or under-logging.BeginnerSome support

Ten people who reported not losing weight on under 1,200 kcal actually under-reported intake by 47 percent and over-reported activity by 51 percent. In a one-year trial of 48 adults, calorie-restricted participants stopped losing weight on weekends. Add water noise from the table above and a two-week stall tells you little. A real stall holds over three to four weeks of honest logging.

  • Wait three to four weeks, then cut by about 100 to 200 kcal or add steps.

Lichtman 1992 · Racette 2008 · Bhutani 2017

Scale, plateaus and metabolismWeighing yourself regularly goes with more loss inside a programmeProgrammes that included self-weighing beat minimal care by 3.4 kg, and daily weighing did no better than weekly.BeginnerSome support

A meta-analysis found 15 trials of multi-component programmes that included self-weighing lost 3.4 kg more than minimal or no intervention, with no significant difference between daily and weekly weighing. Only one study tested self-weighing alone, and it found no evidence of an effect. In two studies of female first-year students, daily weighing with feedback on the trend of the last 7 days kept weight steady while controls gained 3.1 and 2.0 kg. McDonald favours daily weigh-ins read as a running average. The scale is feedback inside a plan.

  • Weigh daily or on a fixed schedule, and act on the 7-day average, not on single days.

Madigan 2015 · Levitsky 2006 · McDonald 2014q

Scale, plateaus and metabolismA fixed calorie cut slows to a plateau because the gap closes as you shrinkOn a fixed cut a shrinking body burns less, so loss slows and stops. Supervised dieters fell about a quarter short of the 3,500 rule within two months.IntermediateSome support

Each kg lost early holds less energy than fat, because water and lean tissue go too (see the entry on energy per kg lost at week four). Even so, 103 adults with supervised or measured intake lost 20.1 lb (9.1 kg) in about 65 days, against 27.6 lb (12.5 kg) predicted by the rule. A validated model puts each lasting change of 100 kJ (24 kcal) a day at about 1 kg of eventual weight, half within a year. Eating less also trims the energy spent digesting food. To keep the pace, reopen the gap with less food or more movement.

  • Use a dynamic planner, such as the NIH Body Weight Planner, for forecasts beyond a few weeks.

Thomas 2013 · Heymsfield 2012 · Hall 2011 · McDonald 2015s

Scale, plateaus and metabolismWhen the trend says change, move calories about 10 percent and wait againAdjust intake or activity in steps of about a tenth, then give each change three to four weeks before you judge it.IntermediateCoaching practice

Loss faster than planned, or lifts falling with sound training, means ease off: add about 10 percent to calories or drop some cardio, as falling strength can signal muscle loss. A stall over three to four weeks of honest logging means cut intake or add activity by about a tenth. A rate on target needs nothing. Stop cutting and take a break at maintenance if intake is already low or sleep, mood or training are slipping (see the very-lean entry). McDonald's rule: replace a change that shows nothing in about four weeks and keep one that works.

  • Write down the weekly loss you expect before you start.
  • Change one thing at a time.

McDonald 2009c · McDonald 2008o · McDonald 2007d

Scale, plateaus and metabolismCutting calories after a short stallShort stalls are mostly water and weekend noise, and cutting on them stacks deficit on top of a working plan.MistakeBeginnerSome support

A two-week plateau can come from water, a missed weekend or logging drift, as 48 adults who stopped losing on weekends showed. Cutting calories on a stall moves you toward the 500 kcal limit sooner. McDonald's view is that cutting harder can also add stress-related water retention, which hides fat loss for longer. Wait three to four weeks, check the log, and only then change calories or steps.

  • Check the four-week trend and the log before changing calories.

Racette 2008 · Bhutani 2017 · Murphy 2022 · McDonald 2009c

Scale, plateaus and metabolismReading a big first-week drop on low carb as fast fat lossAn early drop on low carb is mostly stored glycogen and the water held with it, and fat loss did not speed up on a ketogenic diet in a ward study.MistakeBeginnerSome support

Glycogen is stored with three to four parts water, so emptying it moves the scale while releasing little stored energy. In 17 men on a metabolic ward, swapping a high-carbohydrate diet for a ketogenic one with the same calories raised chamber energy expenditure slightly, by about 57 kcal a day, yet body fat loss slowed and fat-free mass loss rose. McDonald notes that the water returns as soon as carbohydrate does. Judge any diet on the trend from week three or four onward.

  • Leave out the first week when you compare diets or judge your rate of loss.

Kreitzman 1992 · Hall 2016 · McDonald 2015s · McDonald 2007f

Diet type and meal patternDiet breaks and refeeds give mixed fat-loss results and may ease hungerPlanned weeks at maintenance did not improve fat loss in lifters, and they lowered hunger.IntermediateSome support

In 61 resistance-trained adults over 12 weeks, alternating three weeks of restriction with one week at maintenance gave the same fat and lean mass as continuous dieting, with lower hunger. A meta-analysis of 12 trials (881 participants) found the same body composition and a smaller drop in resting rate. A small 7-week trial found a weekly two-day carb refeed kept more lean mass (-0.4 vs -1.3 kg). Results in men with obesity favoured breaks.

  • Try a one- to two-week break at maintenance if hunger or training quality is slipping.

PEOS 2021 · Poon 2025 · Campbell 2020 · Byrne 2018

Diet type and meal patternThe best diet is the one you can keep to, since types differ littleLow-carb and low-fat diets lost about the same weight, so pick the one you can keep.BeginnerWell supported

A network meta-analysis of 48 randomized trials (7,286 people) found weight loss with any low-carbohydrate or low-fat diet, with small differences among named diets. A larger analysis of 121 trials (21,942 patients) found about 4.6 kg at 6 months with low carb and 4.4 kg with low fat. In a 12-month trial of 609 adults, the difference was 0.7 kg, not significant. Behavioural support added about 3.2 kg at six months in one analysis.

  • Choose the pattern that fits your food preferences and schedule.

Johnston 2014 · Ge 2020 · Gardner 2018

Diet type and meal patternIntermittent fasting works by eating less, with no clear extra effectTime-restricted eating did not beat plain calorie control in long trials, though one pooled analysis disagrees.IntermediateSome support

In 139 adults over 12 months, eating 8 am to 4 pm with calorie restriction lost 8.0 kg against 6.3 kg for calorie restriction alone, not a significant difference. A 12-week trial of 116 people found no advantage, and a 41-person feeding trial matched calories and found no effect on weight. A pooled analysis of 30 studies did report 1.46 kg more loss in matched comparisons. With lifting, an analysis of eight studies (221 participants) kept fat-free mass.

  • Use a fasting window only if it makes eating less easier for you.

Liu 2022 · Lowe 2020 · Maruthur 2024 · Fernandes-Alves 2026 · Ashtary-Larky 2021 · Keenan 2020

Diet type and meal patternMeal frequency and late eating matter little for fat loss at equal caloriesDifferences between meal patterns are small at equal calories; fewer meals showed about 1 kg more loss in one analysis.BeginnerSome support

A network meta-analysis of 22 trials (647 people) found two meals a day slightly lowered weight against three meals or six, by about 1 kg, under equal calories. In 16 obese adults on the same deficit, three meals and three snacks did no better than three meals. A meta-analysis of 9 trials found earlier eating gave 1.2 kg more loss, and a controlled crossover trial found late eating raised hunger. Pick the pattern you can follow.

  • Choose a meal pattern around your day, and keep protein in each meal.

Schwingshackl 2020 · Cameron 2010 · Young 2023 · Vujović 2022

Diet type and meal patternWeight lost tracked sticking to the diet, not which diet it wasIn one year-long trial, weight lost followed how closely people kept to their diet, with no link to the diet they chose.BeginnerSome support

In 160 overweight or obese adults with heart-risk factors, assigned to Atkins, Zone, Weight Watchers or Ornish, mean one-year loss was 2.1 to 3.3 kg and only 50 to 65 percent finished. Loss correlated with self-reported adherence (r = 0.60) but not with diet type (r = 0.07). Adherence was self-reported and chosen by the participants, so this shows who did well, not that pushing adherence causes loss.

  • Pick the eating pattern you can see yourself keeping for a year.

Dansinger 2005 · Johnston 2014

Diet type and meal patternSet up a fat-loss diet in order: calories, protein, fat, then carbohydrateFix the calorie target first, then protein, then a moderate fat intake, and let carbohydrate fill whatever is left.BeginnerCoaching practice

A contest-prep review sets calories for a loss of 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight a week, protein at 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of lean mass, fat at 15 to 30 percent of calories and carbohydrate as the remainder. McDonald uses the same order and keeps fat near a quarter of calories because very low-fat diets are hard to stick to. Example: 75 kg (165 lb) on 2,200 kcal, with 165 g protein (660 kcal) and 61 g fat (550 kcal), leaves about 248 g of carbohydrate. When calories must drop, carbohydrate usually gives way first.

  • Write down the four numbers before you choose foods.

Helms 2014b · McDonald 2009ac · McDonald 2008m · McDonald 2010e

Diet type and meal patternAthletes in a deficit should keep food around hard sessions and cut elsewhereWhen an athlete trims carbohydrate to diet, the cut should come from meals away from hard training, not from the meals that fuel it.IntermediateCoaching practice

A review of weight management in athletes lists eating around exercise among its core strategies, next to avoiding severe restriction and choosing foods of low energy density. It warns that severe restriction during hard training lowers strength, glycogen stores, concentration and the response to training. McDonald's view is that skipping food before or after hard sessions to save calories only hurts training, because the deficit sets fat loss either way. Easy, low-intensity sessions are the exception.

  • Put most of the day's carbohydrate in the meals before and after hard training.

Manore 2015 · McDonald 2008m

Diet type and meal patternSkipping breakfast does not slow fat lossIn a meta-analysis of short trials, people assigned to breakfast ate about 260 kcal a day more and weighed slightly more than people assigned to skip it.BeginnerSome support

In randomised trials in adults, breakfast eaters weighed 0.44 kg more (7 trials) and took in about 260 kcal a day more (10 trials), though trials were short and mostly of low quality. The energy spent digesting food depends on how much you eat, not how often. McDonald's view is that smaller dieters on low intakes often do better with three or four filling meals than with six tiny ones. Skipping breakfast differs from eating late, and newer trials favour eating earlier in the day (see the meal timing entry).

  • Eat breakfast if it keeps you full to lunch, and skip it if it does not.

Sievert 2019 · McDonald 2008r · McDonald 2015o

Diet type and meal patternCutting out dairy for a diet or contest prepDairy did not change weight overall in a meta-analysis, but in calorie-restricted trials it gave slightly more weight and fat loss.MistakeIntermediateSome support

Across 29 randomised trials (2,101 people), dairy had no overall effect on weight. In short or energy-restricted trials it lowered weight, and pooled over 22 trials body fat fell a modest 0.45 kg, with the benefit confined to short or restricted trials. So dairy fits a diet and may help a little. McDonald traces the habit of dropping it before shows to old milk-heavy bulking diets, and notes dairy is a main source of calcium. Avoid it with a diagnosed milk allergy; lactose intolerance is separate and often managed with lactose-free products.

  • Count dairy in your calories and protein like any other food.

Chen 2012 · McDonald 2007c

Cardio and daily movementExercise adds to fat loss, and daily movement drops on diets if you let itExercise adds 1.5 to 3.5 kg of loss in reviews, and people on diets may move less outside the gym.BeginnerSome support

An overview of 12 meta-analyses found exercise led to 1.5 to 3.5 kg of weight loss and similar results from aerobic and interval training when energy expenditure matched. The ACSM suggests 150 minutes a week as a floor and 200 to 300 for long-term loss. A systematic review of 36 articles found lower non-exercise activity in 63 percent of diet-only arms. In 16 overfed adults, NEAT explained the large differences in fat gain.

  • Set a daily step or activity floor and keep it during the diet.

Bellicha 2021 · DONNELLY 2009 · Silva 2018 · Levine 1999

Very lean, regain and maintenanceGetting very lean costs hormones, mood and training quality, so go carefullyLong spells under about 25 kcal per kg of lean mass led to muscle loss and hormone and mood problems in men. In women, 30 marks rising risk, not a cut-off.AdvancedSome support

Reviews of lean athletes link a long period at low energy availability to muscle loss, hormone disruption and psychological problems. In one natural bodybuilder, body fat fell from 14.8 to 4.5 percent and testosterone from 9.22 to 2.27 ng/mL, recovering after the contest. In 27 women dieting for a fitness show, sex hormones fell and most recovered within three to four months. The 30 figure comes from a five-day lab study; in 35 women over three cycles, risk rose steadily with no cut-off, and the 2025 Triad update moves away from a threshold. See the women area. Involve a coach and clinician.

  • Treat contest-level leanness as a short, supervised project, not a lifestyle.
  • See a clinician if periods stop or mood drops.

Fagerberg 2018 · Rossow 2013 · Hulmi 2017 · Loucks 2003 · Mountjoy 2023 · Helms 2019 · Lieberman 2018 · Williams 2026 · De Souza 2026

Very lean, regain and maintenanceWeight regain is common, and maintenance is possible with sustained habitsMost people regain some weight. A minority keep most of it off, and they report steady activity and regular weighing.IntermediateSome support

In 171 women, 52 percent of the weight lost was regained at one year and 89 percent at two. A meta-analysis of 29 US studies found the average person kept off more than 3 kg at five years. Among 2,886 National Weight Control Registry members, mean loss was 31.3 kg at entry and 23.1 kg at ten years; they report about an hour of activity a day and regular weighing. That link to activity is observational and self-selected: an overview of controlled trials found no significant effect of exercise training on maintenance. McDonald's rule is to return to maintenance promptly, not to climb in small steps for months.

  • After the diet, bring calories back to maintenance within about two weeks, then hold there.
  • Keep weighing, activity and protein up.

Martins 2020 · Anderson 2001 · Thomas 2014 · Wing 2005 · Hall 2018 · Rosenbaum 2010 · Bellicha 2021 · McDonald 2016l

Very lean, regain and maintenanceLosing weight quickly did not mean regaining it faster in a long trialIn 200 adults with obesity, a 12-week rapid diet and a 36-week gradual one ended with the same share regained nearly three years later.IntermediateSome support

Both groups aimed for 15 percent loss. Of the rapid group 81 percent reached 12.5 percent, against 50 percent of the gradual group, and completers had regained 70.5 and 71.2 percent by week 144. The rapid programme ran through a hospital, and one person in it needed gallbladder surgery. In 262 women, the fastest losers in month one had lost most at 18 months. McDonald argued in 2009 that fast loss can last if it builds habits; the trial came later and found no regain penalty, not an advantage.

  • Do a rapid diet in a supervised programme, with a clinician involved.
  • Lean lifters should still use the slower pace in the rate-of-loss entry.

Purcell 2014 · Nackers 2010 · McDonald 2009k · McDonald 2009aa

Very lean, regain and maintenanceAthletes should diet in a low-intensity training block, not close to competitionPlan fat loss for the off-season or a general preparation block, and aim for a weight you can hold for most of the year.IntermediateCoaching practice

A review on athletes' weight advises finding a healthy weight that can be kept for most of the year, so little has to come off before competition, and allowing enough time to get there safely.   • Choose a target weight you can hold without constant dieting.

Gaining phasesA small surplus adds muscle, and a large surplus mostly adds fatA surplus of about 10 to 20 percent over maintenance is a common starting range for lifters, and nobody has proven the best size.IntermediateThin evidence

A narrative review for physique athletes suggests 10 to 20 percent above maintenance, with advanced lifters more conservative. In 17 lifters over 8 weeks, a 15 percent surplus mainly increased fat gain rather than strength or muscle thickness. Another review finds the ideal surplus is unknown. If maintenance is 2,500 kcal, 10 to 20 percent is 250 to 500 kcal. Evidence is thin and short.

  • Start near the low end of the surplus and adjust to the scale.

Gaining phasesRecomposition works best for beginners, returners and people carrying more fatGaining muscle while losing fat is realistic with high protein and hard training, and likely harder for lean, advanced lifters.IntermediateThin evidence

  • Use a small deficit or maintenance with high protein and progressive training.
