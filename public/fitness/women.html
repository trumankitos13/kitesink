How women and men differAdult men carry at least 15 times the testosterone of adult womenAfter puberty men's blood testosterone runs more than 15-fold above women's, and the two ranges do not overlap in healthy adults.BeginnerWell supported

Before puberty there is no sex difference in testosterone or in athletic performance. At puberty the testes raise output about 30-fold. A 2018 endocrine review ties the gap to men's larger muscle mass, greater strength and higher haemoglobin, and to a performance edge of at least 8 to 12 percent. An American College of Sports Medicine consensus puts the edge at 10 to 30 percent depending on the event. Lyle McDonald's summary is that women have a tenth to a thirtieth of men's level. The gap explains differences in absolute numbers. It does not predict how well a woman responds to training.

  • Compare your lifts with women's standards or with your own past numbers.
  • Do not read a smaller absolute number as a poorer response to training.

Handelsman 2018 · Hunter 2023 · McDonald 2015p

How women and men differThe strength gap between the sexes is widest in the upper bodyWomen's upper-body strength sits further below men's than their lower-body strength, mainly because less of their muscle is carried there.BeginnerSome support

In a lab comparison of eight men and eight women, the women were about 52 percent as strong in the upper body and 66 percent as strong in the lower body. Their biceps were 45 percent smaller in cross-section and their thigh muscles 25 to 30 percent smaller. A 2023 review of the wider literature reaches the same conclusion: the gap is larger in upper-body muscles, and it comes from muscle mass and fast-fibre size, not from a weaker ability to switch muscle on. Pressing and pulling lifts therefore start further behind than squats and deadlifts do.

  • Expect presses and pull-ups to start further behind than your leg lifts.

Miller 1993 · Nuzzo 2023

How women and men differA given amount of muscle produces the same force in women and menStrength per unit of muscle cross-section did not differ by sex, so the gap in strength is a gap in muscle size.IntermediateSome support

The 1993 comparison of eight men and eight women found no significant sex difference in strength relative to muscle cross-sectional area at the elbow or the knee, in the number of biceps fibres, or in any motor unit measure. The men's fibres were larger. A 2023 review also found that men's greater strength does not come from higher voluntary activation. McDonald makes the same point from the tissue side: male and female muscle is close to identical, with small differences such as slightly more fat within women's muscle. Added muscle should therefore carry the same strength value for either sex.

Miller 1993 · Nuzzo 2023 · McDonald 2015p

How women and men differWomen's muscle has a larger share of slow-twitch fibre areaPooled biopsies from over 5,000 people show men with bigger fibres of every type and women with more of their muscle area as type I fibre.AdvancedWell supported

A 2024 meta-analysis pooled 110 biopsy studies covering 2,875 men and 2,452 women, most from the outer thigh muscle of healthy adults aged 18 to 59. Men had larger cross-sectional areas for all fibre types and a greater share of type II (fast-twitch) fibre. Women had a greater share of type I (slow-twitch) fibre, by count and by area. The author suggests this helps explain sex differences in strength and in muscle endurance. It is a difference of degree. Both sexes have both fibre types, and a separate meta-analysis found type II fibres grew similarly in women and men after training.

Nuzzo 2024b · Refalo 2025

How women and men differWomen burn relatively more fat and less carbohydrate in steady exerciseAt the same relative effort women draw more on fat and less on carbohydrate and amino acids than men, and estrogen appears to drive it.AdvancedSome support

Compared with women, active men oxidise more carbohydrate and more of the amino acid leucine, and less fat, at rest and during moderate endurance exercise. In a double-blind crossover trial, 12 men took estradiol or placebo for 8 days and then cycled for 90 minutes at 65 percent of maximum. On estradiol, carbohydrate oxidation fell 5 to 16 percent, leucine oxidation fell 16 percent and fat oxidation rose 22 to 44 percent. This was a laboratory experiment to isolate the hormone's effect, not a treatment. Fuel mix during a session is also not the same as fat lost over weeks, which depends on energy balance.

  • Do not expect a different fuel mix to change how fast you lose fat.

Hamadeh 2005 · McDonald 2015p

How women and men differThe monthly cycle, not only lower testosterone, sets female physiology apartA man's hormones stay nearly flat from day to day. A cycling woman's estrogen and progesterone swing widely every month.BeginnerCoaching practice

McDonald's argument is that a man with low testosterone does not have a woman's physiology, because the defining feature is the repeating rise and fall of estrogen and progesterone across a cycle of roughly four weeks. He lists appetite, mood, fuel use and recovery among the things reported to shift with it. Researchers make a related point: a working guide for sport science notes that most high-quality data come from men, which limits how well they transfer given the differences in reproductive hormones. How much the swings matter for performance is a separate question, covered under the cycle topic.

McDonald 2015p · Elliott-Sale 2021

Strength and muscle gainWomen gain muscle at the same relative rate as men on the same programmeAcross 29 studies, relative muscle growth differed by under 1 percent between the sexes. Men gained slightly more in absolute terms.BeginnerWell supported

A 2025 meta-analysis pooled 29 studies in which healthy men and women aged 18 to 45 followed the same resistance training. Absolute growth slightly favoured men (standardised difference 0.19). Relative growth, measured against each person's starting size, differed by 0.69 percent, with an interval from -1.50 to 2.88 that includes zero. Training experience did not change the result. An earlier meta-analysis of 10 studies also found no significant sex difference in hypertrophy. McDonald wrote the same of beginners in 2016: equal percentage gains, and smaller absolute ones because the starting point is smaller.

  • Use the same training principles the rest of this guide gives.
  • Track growth as a percentage of where you started.

Refalo 2025 · Roberts 2020c · McDonald 2016q

Strength and muscle gainWomen's upper-body strength rose faster than men's in relative termsIn 17 studies of the same programme for both sexes, women's relative upper-body strength gains were larger. Lower-body gains were similar.IntermediateSome support

A 2020 meta-analysis of young to middle-aged adults found a moderate effect favouring women for relative upper-body strength (effect size -0.60 across 17 studies) and no significant difference for lower-body strength across 23 studies. The authors offer two readings: untrained women may have more room to improve in the upper body, or the result may be an artefact of short studies. A 2023 review likewise reports greater relative strength gains in women depending on age and muscle group. Starting further behind in the upper body can mean quick early progress there.

  • Train the upper body as seriously as the lower body.

Roberts 2020c · Nuzzo 2023

Strength and muscle gainAfter 50, men gain more in absolute terms and women as much or more in relative termsIn 30 studies of adults over 50, men added more absolute strength and size. Relative size gains matched, and women gained more relative leg strength.BeginnerWell supported

The meta-analysis covered 1,410 participants, 651 men and 759 women, who did identical resistance training. Men gained more absolute upper-body strength, lower-body strength and muscle size. Relative muscle growth did not differ, relative upper-body strength did not differ, and women gained more relative lower-body strength. Average study quality was moderate. The authors stress that which sex appears to respond better depends on whether results are shown as absolute or relative change. Both sexes improved. Anyone with a medical condition should clear a new programme with a doctor.

Jones 2021

Strength and muscle gainExpect strength to climb well before visible sizeIn a 16-week trial women's strength rose 24 to 59 percent across four tests while thigh muscle area did not change measurably.BeginnerThin evidence

Eight women and seven men trained three days a week for 16 weeks. The women's strength rose 59 percent in elbow flexion, 42 percent in elbow extension, 24 percent in knee flexion and 34 percent in knee extension. Percentage changes did not differ from the men's. Upper-arm muscle area on CT grew 23 percent in the women and 16 percent in the men, while thigh muscle area rose a non-significant 2.9 percent in both. McDonald reads results like this as the nervous system learning first, with visible growth following later.

  • Use strength records, not the mirror, to judge your first months.

Cureton 1988 · McDonald 2016q

Strength and muscle gainLeaking urine under heavy lifts is common in women lifters and worth getting assessedIn a survey of 480 competitive women powerlifters, 44 percent had leaked urine in the previous three months, most often on the deadlift.BeginnerSome support

A meta-analysis of eight studies put the prevalence of urinary incontinence in female athletes across sports at 36 percent, with a risk 177 percent higher than in sedentary women. In the powerlifting survey, severity rose with number of births, age, competition total and body mass index, and was unrelated to years of training. Women who had had a pelvic floor examination, or who were confident they could do pelvic floor exercises correctly, reported less severe leakage. A survey cannot show cause. It does show that the problem is common and that assessment goes with milder symptoms.

  • Raise it with a doctor or a pelvic health physiotherapist; do not just train around it.

Teixeira 2018 · Wikander 2021 · McDonald 2018b

Strength and muscle gainAvoiding heavy weights for fear of sudden bulkIn women-only trials strength rose a lot and muscle size moderately, and the size change builds over months, not sessions.MistakeBeginnerWell supported

A meta-analysis of 24 studies in women found large effects of resistance training on upper- and lower-body strength and a medium effect on muscle size. After correcting for publication bias the upper-body strength effect stayed large and the lower-body effect was medium. Absolute growth in women is slightly smaller than in men on the same programme. McDonald's coaching observation is that the size change a woman sees is always modest and tends to trail strength, and that many women who start out wary of size become keen on getting stronger once daily tasks feel easier.

  • Use loads that make the planned reps hard.
  • Judge size over months with a tape or photos, and change the plan then if you wish.

Hagstrom 2020 · Refalo 2025 · McDonald 2016q

Strength and muscle gainTreating training for women as a separate methodWhen both sexes followed the same programme, relative gains in size and lower-body strength matched, so the same principles apply.MistakeBeginnerWell supported

A common assumption is that women should train with light weights and high reps for tone, a motive a 2023 review found more often in women. The trials do not support a split by sex. Two meta-analyses of studies that gave men and women identical training found similar relative muscle growth, and one found similar relative lower-body strength gains with larger relative upper-body gains in women. In the women-only analysis, training frequency and volume influenced strength, and no training variable changed the size outcome. Load, volume and effort should follow the goal and training age.

  • Follow the guide's general advice on load, volume and effort.
  • Adjust for your own recovery and schedule.

Roberts 2020c · Refalo 2025 · Hagstrom 2020 · Nuzzo 2023

Muscular potentialFew female college athletes exceed an FFMI of about 24Among 372 female collegiate athletes measured by DXA, mean fat-free mass index was 18.8 and the 97.5th percentile was 23.9.IntermediateSome support

Fat-free mass index (FFMI) is fat-free mass in kg divided by height in metres squared. In the 372 athletes, sport averages ran from 16.6 in cross country to 19.7 in Olympic weightlifting and 20.1 in rugby. A second DXA sample of 266 athletes averaged 16.9, with individual values from 13.3 to 25.5. The larger sample averaged 24 percent body fat. Fat-free mass climbs as fat is gained, so the top values are not all muscle. Neither abstract reports drug testing. For men, the body composition area of this guide gives 25 as the figure where drug-free athletes topped out in the original study.

  • Use these figures as landmarks for trained women, not as targets.

Harty 2019 · Blue 2019

Muscular potentialThe proposed upper limit for women's lean mass rests on one very large athleteA 1998 study put women's ceiling above 0.44 kg of fat-free mass per cm of height. McDonald treats that as an outlier and works from about 0.33.IntermediateThin evidence

The study measured 29 strength-trained female college athletes. Six carried more than 60 kg (132 lb) of fat-free mass and the largest carried 82.1 kg (181 lb), which led the authors to set the theoretical limit above 80 kg and 0.44 kg per cm. McDonald's reading is that one exceptional athlete should not define a goal for others. From the ordinary range in such samples he takes about 0.33 kg per cm (1.84 lb per inch) as the realistic top for most women, and says some will not reach it. At 165 cm (5 ft 5 in) that is about 54 kg (120 lb) of lean mass, which includes water, glycogen and bone.

  • Estimate your own ceiling from height, then treat it as a rough upper bound.

Abe 1998 · McDonald 2017j

Muscular potentialMost women are near their muscular limit after three to four years of good trainingMcDonald's estimate is that three to four years of proper training brings most women close to their ceiling, after which gains become very slow.IntermediateCoaching practice

This is his coaching judgment, not a study result. He pairs it with a planning rule: the nearer you are to your likely ceiling, the smaller the monthly gain worth chasing and the smaller the calorie surplus that makes sense. For a woman with only about 2 kg (5 lb) of potential left, he suggests aiming for as little as 0.25 kg (0.5 lb) of lean mass a month. He also notes that taller and heavier women can carry more lean mass, so height belongs in any estimate. He specifies proper training, so years of casual gym use count for less.

  • Count only your years of structured, progressive training.
  • Shrink the surplus as the monthly gain you can expect shrinks.

McDonald 2017j

Muscular potentialHigher natural androgen levels are more common among elite sportswomenPolycystic ovary syndrome is over-represented in elite sport, which is one reason elite samples overstate what most women can expect.AdvancedSome support

A 2018 endocrine review states that women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), a condition that often raises androgens, are over-represented in elite athletics. Among 90 Swedish Olympic sportswomen, PCOS was the most common diagnosis behind menstrual dysfunction. In a clinic study of endurance athletes, 8 of 25 with disturbed cycles had higher testosterone and a more anabolic body composition than the rest. McDonald's inference is that women with higher testosterone are more likely to sit at the top of the lean-mass range for their height. Only a doctor can diagnose PCOS.

  • Set expectations from ordinary trained women, not from the most muscular elite athletes.

Handelsman 2018 · Hagmar 2009 · Rickenlund 2003 · McDonald 2017j

Menstrual cycle and contraceptionCycle phase changes performance by a trivial amount on averagePooled across 51 studies, performance in the early follicular phase was lower by a trivial margin, and the evidence was rated low quality.IntermediateWell supported

A 2020 meta-analysis of women with regular natural cycles found a pooled effect of -0.06 for the first days of the cycle against all other phases, with an interval that spans zero. The largest contrast, early against late follicular, was -0.14. A separate analysis of 21 strength studies found trivial to small, non-significant differences between phases. A 2024 analysis of 22 studies rated the early follicular phase least favourable for maximal strength, with a small effect of 0.14 for dynamic strength. A 2023 umbrella review judged it premature to conclude that phase appreciably affects strength performance.

  • Train as planned through the cycle unless your own log shows a pattern that repeats.

McNulty 2020 · Blagrove 2020 · Niering 2024 · Colenso-Semple 2023

Menstrual cycle and contraceptionWomen eat more in the luteal phase, by about 170 kcal a day on averageA meta-analysis of 15 datasets found energy intake about 168 kcal a day higher in the luteal phase than in the follicular phase.BeginnerWell supported

The analysis covered 330 women of healthy weight, average age 26, with no history of disordered eating. The standardised difference was 0.69 and statistically significant. The authors caution that studies measured intake and identified cycle phase in inconsistent ways. An earlier review, the one McDonald's 2015 article works through, pointed the same way and also reported more frequent cravings for foods high in carbohydrate and fat in the luteal phase. His article quotes single-study increases from 90 to 500 kcal a day, which shows how much women differ. Which hormone drives the change is not settled.

  • Expect more hunger in the two weeks before a period and plan meals for it.
  • Check your own log, since the size of the effect varies between women.

Tucker 2025 · Davidsen 2007 · McDonald 2015x

Menstrual cycle and contraceptionA diet tailored to the cycle did not beat plain restriction in its one trialIn 60 overweight women over six months, a cycle-tailored programme showed no significant advantage in the main analysis. Half the women dropped out.IntermediateThin evidence

Both groups were prescribed 1,600 kcal a day. One followed a diet and exercise plan adjusted to cycle phase, the other simple restriction. Thirty-one finished. The intention-to-treat analysis found no clinically significant difference. Women who stuck to the tailored plan lost 4.3 kg (9.5 lb) more than controls, which describes adherent women, not everyone assigned to the plan. McDonald relays the earlier review's suggestion to begin a diet after a period, not just before one. His own is to put a diet break or other flexibility in the days before a period, when appetite peaks. These are coaching judgments.

  • Start a diet in the week after your period if the timing is yours to choose.
  • Put planned flexibility where your own hunger peaks.

Geiker 2016 · Davidsen 2007 · McDonald 2015x

Menstrual cycle and contraceptionOral contraceptives make little or no difference to performance or training gainsPooled data show at most a trivial average performance difference against women with natural cycles, and no effect on strength or muscle gain.IntermediateWell supported

A meta-analysis of 42 studies and 590 participants concluded that oral contraceptive use might result in slightly inferior performance on average, with any group-level effect most likely trivial, and that performance was consistent across the pill cycle. Most studies were rated moderate to very low quality. A second meta-analysis of 8 training studies with 325 participants found no significant effect of the pill on hypertrophy, power or strength. Both author groups say the evidence supports no general guidance for or against use. Whether to use, change or stop contraception is a decision for you and your doctor.

  • Do not choose or change contraception for training reasons on this evidence.
  • Discuss side effects or concerns with your doctor.

Elliott-Sale 2020 · Nolan 2024

Menstrual cycle and contraceptionTrials found no large effect of hormonal contraception on body weightCochrane reviews found no evidence of a large weight effect from combined methods, and under 2 kg average gain in most studies of progestin-only methods.BeginnerSome support

The review of combined pills and patches covered 49 trials. The four with a placebo or no-treatment group found no evidence of a causal link with weight change, and the authors concluded that the evidence was insufficient to settle the question but that no large effect was evident. The review of progestin-only methods covered 22 studies and 11,450 women, rated the evidence low quality, and found mean gain under 2 kg (4.4 lb) at 6 or 12 months in most studies. McDonald stresses that methods differ and that individual responses vary widely. Concerns about a method belong with a doctor or pharmacist.

  • Judge weight change on a multi-week trend, not on the first weeks of a new method.
  • Take any concern about a method to your doctor; do not stop it on your own.

Gallo 2014 · Lopez 2016 · McDonald 2015x

Menstrual cycle and contraceptionRebuilding a training plan around cycle phasesReviews of the evidence do not support fixed phase-based training rules. They recommend adjusting to the individual.MistakeIntermediateWell supported

Phase-based programmes put the heaviest work in the follicular phase and lighter work in the luteal phase. A 2023 umbrella review of meta-analyses and systematic reviews found highly variable results and poor, inconsistent methods for confirming cycle phase, and concluded it is premature to say that short-term hormone swings appreciably influence strength or hypertrophy adaptations. The 2020 performance meta-analysis took a similar position: general guidelines cannot be formed, and a personalised approach is advised. Cycle length also varies from month to month, which makes a fixed calendar hard to follow.

  • Keep a consistent programme and adjust single sessions by how you feel on the day.
  • Log symptoms for a few cycles before changing anything permanent.

Colenso-Semple 2023 · McNulty 2020 · McDonald 2016o

Menstrual cycle and contraceptionCounting on a faster metabolism before a period to cover extra eatingResting metabolism rises only slightly in the luteal phase, and in studies since 2000 the rise was not statistically significant.MistakeIntermediateWell supported

A meta-analysis of 26 studies in 318 women found a small effect toward higher resting metabolic rate in the luteal phase (effect size 0.33). In studies published since 2000 the effect shrank to 0.23 and lost significance, and the authors note small samples and weak control of confounders. McDonald's 2015 article cites older reports of a 2.5 to 11.5 percent rise, about 90 to 280 kcal a day, and makes the practical point that the rise in appetite can outrun it. The newer pooled data make that point more strongly: intake rises by about 168 kcal a day on average, while the metabolic rise is small and uncertain.

  • Do not add calories in the luteal phase on the strength of a metabolic boost.
  • If hunger is the problem, move calories within the week and keep the weekly total.

Benton 2020 · Tucker 2025 · McDonald 2015x

Energy availability and periodsLow energy availability, not a body fat figure, shuts down the cycleAthletes at the same low body fat can have normal or absent cycles. The difference lies in the energy left over after training.IntermediateWell supported

The first explanation for lost periods in athletes was a critical level of body fat. McDonald traces how that idea gave way: women at similar leanness did and did not keep their cycles, and getting very lean requires long restriction and heavy training, so leanness and under-fuelling arrive together. In a controlled study of 29 women of normal body composition, five days at an energy availability under 30 kcal per kg of lean mass disrupted the pulses of luteinising hormone that drive the cycle, far too quickly for body fat to have changed. Current consensus statements name energy deficiency as the cause.

  • Do not use a body fat reading to judge whether your intake is adequate.
  • See a doctor about any change in your cycle during a diet or a heavy training block.

Loucks 2003 · De Souza 2026 · Mountjoy 2023 · McDonald 2015i

Energy availability and periodsThe 30 kcal per kg figure marks rising risk, not a switchNewer trials found no single energy availability below which cycles fail. Risk climbs steadily as energy availability falls.IntermediateWell supported

The figure came from five-day laboratory studies of hormone pulses, and McDonald's 2015 article cites a threshold of 20 to 30 kcal per kg of lean mass. A later trial followed 35 women through three cycles of diet and exercise. The odds of a menstrual disturbance fell 9 percent for each 1 kcal per kg of fat-free mass that energy availability rose, and no cut-off appeared. Some women kept ovulating below 30 and some were disturbed above it. The 2025 Female Athlete Triad update therefore moves away from a threshold and links about 45 kcal per kg of fat-free mass a day with the healthy end of the range.

  • Treat 30 as a warning zone, not as a safe floor to diet down to.

Lieberman 2018 · De Souza 2026 · Williams 2026 · Loucks 2003 · McDonald 2015i

Energy availability and periodsA modest rise in intake restored periods in a year-long trialIn 76 exercising women with absent or infrequent periods, eating about 330 kcal more a day made the return of menses about twice as likely.IntermediateSome support

The REFUEL trial randomised the women to raise intake by 20 to 40 percent above baseline needs or to carry on as before, for 12 months. The achieved increase averaged 330 kcal a day, about 18 percent. The higher-intake group was more likely to menstruate during the trial (hazard ratio 1.91) and gained weight, body fat and thyroid hormone T3. The 2025 Triad update concludes that 300 to 600 kcal more a day will likely start recovery, and warns that a returning bleed is not full recovery: ovulation and hormone levels lag until several normal-length cycles in a row. Treatment belongs with a doctor and a dietitian.

  • Work with a doctor and a sports dietitian; do not self-treat a lost cycle.
  • Expect recovery to take months and to need several normal cycles to confirm.

De Souza 2021 · Williams 2026

Energy availability and periodsA bleed on the pill is not a period and says nothing about energy statusThe monthly bleed on combined oral contraception is a withdrawal bleed, so it cannot show that the natural cycle is intact.IntermediateWell supported

A research guide for studies in women tells scientists to call the bleed that oral contraceptive users have a withdrawal bleed, so that users are not wrongly classed as having regular natural cycles. The 2025 Triad update makes the clinical version of the point: combined oral contraceptive pills may mask the return of natural menstruation, and bone loss may continue on them while an energy deficit persists. This is information, not a reason to change contraception. Any such change is a decision to make with a doctor.

  • If you use hormonal contraception, do not rely on bleeding to judge whether you eat enough.
  • Raise any concern about under-fuelling with your doctor.

Elliott-Sale 2021 · Williams 2026

Energy availability and periodsTrusting a calculated energy availability to the last kcalEach part of the sum carries error: logged intake, the energy cost of exercise and the estimate of fat-free mass.MistakeIntermediateSome support

The sum looks simple. Someone eating 2,100 kcal who spends 400 kcal in training and has 50 kg (110 lb) of fat-free mass has (2,100 - 400) / 50 = 34 kcal per kg. A review of the method lists the problems: there is no universal protocol, each component takes resources to measure well, the errors add up, and there is no agreed value that counts as low. The number is useful for spotting a plan that is clearly too lean on paper. It cannot clear someone who has symptoms, and symptoms need a doctor whatever the sum says.

  • Use the sum to sanity-check a diet plan before you start it.
  • Let symptoms, not the arithmetic, decide when to seek medical advice.

Burke 2018

Energy availability and periodsTreating a missed period as a normal sign of hard trainingA lost or irregular cycle is a medical sign with several possible causes, and it is linked to bone loss when energy deficiency is behind it.MistakeBeginnerWell supported

The Endocrine Society guideline describes functional hypothalamic amenorrhea as a diagnosis of exclusion: doctors must rule out other hormonal and systemic causes first, and it lists bone loss and infertility among the complications. The cause cannot be guessed from training load. Among 90 Olympic sportswomen, the most common diagnosis behind menstrual dysfunction was polycystic ovary syndrome, not energy deficiency. The updated Triad consensus adds bone stress injuries to the picture. Three missed periods in a row, or cycles longer than 35 days, meet the research definitions of amenorrhea and oligomenorrhea.

  • See a doctor if your periods stop or become irregular; do not wait for a diet to end.
  • Tell the doctor about your training, diet and any weight change.

Gordon 2017 · Hagmar 2009 · Williams 2026 · Elliott-Sale 2021

Energy availability and periodsBlaming hard training itself for a lost cycleWhen energy intake kept pace with exercise, hard training did not disrupt reproductive hormone pulses. Under-fuelling did.MistakeIntermediateSome support

Nine regularly menstruating women did four days of intense exercise in two trials. With energy availability held at 45 kcal per kg of lean mass, the exercise did not reduce luteinising hormone pulse frequency. With it cut to 10, pulse frequency fell. The authors concluded that the rhythm depends on energy availability, not exercise stress. McDonald draws the lesson in his history of women's sport: athletes who match food intake to activity generally keep their cycles, and the old belief that sport itself damages fertility had the cause wrong. A lost cycle still needs a doctor, because other causes exist.

  • Raise intake along with training load in heavy blocks.
  • See a doctor about a lost or irregular cycle; do not assume the cause.

Loucks 1998 · Williams 2026 · McDonald 2018b

Diets in trialsIn the A TO Z trial the lowest-carb diet lost most, and every group lost littleAmong 311 premenopausal women, 12-month loss averaged 4.7 kg on Atkins and 1.6 to 2.6 kg on the three other diets.BeginnerSome support

The women had a body mass index of 27 to 40 and each was allocated at random to one of four popular diet books spanning very low to very high carbohydrate: Atkins, Zone, LEARN and Ornish. They had weekly classes for two months and were then left to continue for ten more. Atkins beat Zone significantly. The differences among Zone, LEARN and Ornish were not significant, and metabolic markers on Atkins were comparable or better. Lyle McDonald's first comment on the result is about its size: 4.7 kg (10.4 lb) in a year is under half a kilo a month, and the other groups managed about half of that or less.

  • Read a diet trial's absolute losses before its ranking of diets.

Gardner 2007 · McDonald 2009l

Diets in trialsMost of the loss came in the first two months, while the weekly classes ranMcDonald's reading of the A TO Z weight curves: three groups lost during the class period and then stalled for ten months.IntermediateThin evidence

The trial weighed the women at 2, 6 and 12 months. In McDonald's reading of those data, the Zone, LEARN and Ornish groups did all their losing during the eight weeks of dietitian-led classes and lost nothing further in the ten months that followed, while the Atkins group peaked at six months and regained part of the loss by twelve. He draws two lessons. The book mattered less than the structure and contact around it, and unsupervised dieting on a named plan produces small results on average. Both fit the trial's own adherence analysis, in which sticking to the assigned diet predicted loss in every arm.

  • Plan for support after the first weeks: check-ins, a coach or a group.
  • Expect the hard part of a diet to begin when the novelty ends.

Gardner 2007 · Alhassan 2008 · McDonald 2009l

Diets in trialsWithin each A TO Z diet, the women who stuck closest lost the mostThe most adherent third lost 3.7 to 8.3 kg over the year, against 0.4 to 1.9 kg in the least adherent third, whatever the diet.BeginnerSome support

A secondary analysis used 181 women from the Atkins, Zone and Ornish arms and scored adherence as the distance between each woman's reported intake and her diet's macronutrient targets. Adherence correlated with 12-month weight change in every arm (0.34 to 0.42). The most against the least adherent thirds lost 8.3 against 1.9 kg on Atkins, 3.7 against 0.4 kg on Zone and 6.5 against 1.7 kg on Ornish. Only the Atkins contrast was clearly significant. The authors concluded that raising adherence may deserve more emphasis than macronutrient composition. Adherence was scored from self-reported intake.

  • Pick the diet structure you can follow for a year.

Alhassan 2008

Diets in trialsInsulin resistance as a guide to diet choice has mixed supportIn 81 women the insulin resistant adhered and lost less on a low-fat diet, but a larger trial found no interaction between insulin secretion and diet.AdvancedSome support

McDonald's position is that low-carbohydrate diets have no metabolic advantage but make sense for people who are insulin resistant, who often find them easier to keep to. A secondary analysis of 81 overweight women supports the adherence part: insulin-resistant women were less likely to adhere and to lose weight on a low-fat diet than insulin-sensitive women, with no such difference on a low-carbohydrate diet. A later 12-month trial of 609 adults found no significant interaction between diet and insulin secretion. Insulin resistance is a clinical finding. Testing and treatment belong with a doctor.

  • If a doctor has told you that you are insulin resistant, ask which eating pattern they advise.

McClain 2013 · Gardner 2018 · McDonald 2009l

Diets in trialsReading the A TO Z result as proof that low-carb burns more fatIntake was self-reported, adherence drifted and the gap was small, so the trial cannot show a metabolic advantage.MistakeIntermediateSome support

The Atkins group reported eating similar calories to the others, which some read as more loss at equal intake. McDonald's objections: intake came from recalled food records, which under-report; a gap of a few kilos over a year needs only about 50 kcal a day, too little for recall to detect; and in his reading of the weight table most of the lead was there at two months, when cutting carbohydrate sheds water. A meta-analysis of 32 controlled feeding studies found no fat-loss advantage for lower carbohydrate at equal calories, and a later 12-month trial in 609 adults found a non-significant 0.7 kg gap.

  • Choose between low-carb and higher-carb by which you can keep to.
  • Discount the first weeks of loss on any low-carb diet as partly water.

Gardner 2007 · Hall 2017 · Gardner 2018 · McDonald 2009l

Supplements studied in womenNo fat-loss supplement is required, and the proven ones add littleThe few compounds with real evidence shift energy expenditure or weight by small amounts, and only alongside a diet.BeginnerWell supported

McDonald's view is that most fat-loss products do nothing, a handful have a measurable effect, and none replaces a diet and training plan. The pooled data agree on the size. Capsaicin from hot peppers raised energy expenditure by about 59 kcal a day across nine trials, with no effect in studies of people under a body mass index of 25. A Cochrane review found green tea preparations produced a small, non-significant weight loss that is unlikely to matter clinically. Caffeine showed a dose-related association with weight and fat loss across 13 trials with 606 participants, with high variation between studies.

  • Set the diet and training first; treat any supplement as optional.
  • Ask a pharmacist or doctor about interactions with medication or contraception.

Zsiborás 2018 · Jurgens 2012 · Tabrizi 2019 · McDonald 2015o

Supplements studied in womenGreen tea extract did not reduce body fat in trials in womenTwo placebo-controlled trials in women found no effect on weight or fat, and a high-dose trial saw liver function abnormalities in about 5 percent.IntermediateWell supported

In 83 obese premenopausal women on a diet, 300 mg a day of the catechin EGCG for 12 weeks changed neither weight nor fat mass against placebo. In 121 overweight postmenopausal women, a decaffeinated extract supplying 843 mg of EGCG a day for 12 months changed neither fat mass nor body fat percentage. In that study's parent trial, 5.1 percent of women on the extract developed moderate or worse liver function abnormalities, seven times the odds on placebo, and enzymes fell when it was stopped. McDonald's 2015 article rated green tea with caffeine as a mild aid.

  • Do not expect fat loss from green tea extract.
  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist before taking a concentrated extract.

Mielgo-Ayuso 2014 · Dostal 2016 · Yu 2017 · Jurgens 2012 · McDonald 2015o

Supplements studied in womenCaffeine lasts longer in the body on oral contraceptivesWomen on low-dose oral contraceptives cleared caffeine more slowly, with a half-life near 7.9 hours against 5.4 in non-users.IntermediateThin evidence

The study compared long-term users of low-dose estrogen-containing pills with 9 matched women after a single 162 mg dose of caffeine. Plasma clearance was markedly lower in the pill users, and the authors predicted higher steady caffeine levels with regular intake. A separate study of 10 women with natural cycles found slower clearance late in the luteal phase with no change in half-life, an effect the authors thought too small to matter for most women. McDonald's practical point concerns sleep: the same afternoon coffee may linger longer. This is a reason to watch caffeine timing, not to alter contraception.

  • If you use the pill and sleep poorly, try moving your last caffeine earlier in the day.
  • Ask a pharmacist how caffeine fits with any medicine you take.

Abernethy 1985 · Lane 1992 · McDonald 2015o

Supplements studied in womenCaffeine intake was not linked to developing premenstrual syndromeIn a large cohort, women with the highest caffeine intake were no more likely to develop PMS than those with the lowest.BeginnerSome support

Women with premenstrual symptoms are often told to cut caffeine, and McDonald's 2015 article mentions some indication that caffeine worsens symptoms. A case-control study nested in the Nurses' Health Study II later compared 1,234 women who developed clinician-diagnosed PMS with 2,426 controls. After adjustment, total caffeine intake was not associated with PMS: the odds ratio for the highest against the lowest fifth of intake (medians 543 and 18 mg a day) was 0.79, with an interval from 0.61 to 1.04. The study is observational and concerns who develops PMS, not day-to-day symptoms in someone who already has it.

  • Judge caffeine by your own symptoms and sleep.

Purdue-Smithe 2016 · McDonald 2015o

Supplements studied in womenEphedrine produced modest extra weight loss in trials, with clear side effectsA meta-analysis found about 0.9 kg a month more loss than placebo for up to six months, and two to three and a half times the odds of adverse symptoms.AdvancedWell supported

Ephedrine is a medicine, and the herb ephedra contains it. A 2003 meta-analysis pooled controlled trials lasting at least eight weeks. Ephedrine with caffeine gave about 1.0 kg a month more weight loss than placebo, and ephedrine alone 0.6 kg. No trial ran longer than six months. Safety data from 50 trials showed 2.2- to 3.6-fold higher odds of psychiatric, autonomic or gastrointestinal symptoms and of heart palpitations. McDonald regards it as the most potent of the stimulant aids and attributes the reported deaths to misuse. This guide gives no dosing. Use of any such medicine is a matter for a doctor.

  • Do not take ephedrine or ephedra products for fat loss without medical advice.

Shekelle 2003 · McDonald 2015o

Supplements studied in womenYohimbine has one small trial in women behind it and a known anxiety riskTwenty obese women on 1,000 kcal a day lost 3.6 kg with yohimbine against 2.2 kg with placebo over three weeks. The trial was short and tiny.AdvancedThin evidence

Yohimbine blocks alpha-2 receptors, which normally hold back the release of fat from fat cells. The 1991 trial was double-blind and lasted three weeks, and it found no measurable effect on fat release itself. McDonald's view is that it has a use only for already lean dieters working on lower-body fat, and that nobody else should consider it. He also warns that it raises heart rate during exercise, can cause water retention, and is used at high doses in research to provoke anxiety attacks, so anyone prone to anxiety should avoid it. Ask a doctor or pharmacist about interactions before considering it.

  • Avoid yohimbine if you are prone to anxiety or have a heart or blood pressure condition.
  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist before considering it.

Kucio 1991 · McDonald 2015o

Supplements studied in womenA year of melatonin trimmed fat mass slightly in one trial of postmenopausal womenIn 81 postmenopausal women, nightly melatonin for a year lowered fat mass by 6.9 percent against placebo. Body weight did not change.AdvancedThin evidence

The double-blind trial gave 1 or 3 mg nightly or placebo and measured body composition by DXA. Fat mass fell 6.9 percent relative to placebo. Lean mass rose 5.2 percent relative to placebo, a borderline result that became a significant 2.6 percent after adjusting for body mass index. Weight, leptin, insulin and glucose markers did not differ. McDonald's criticism stands: the paper reports percentages without the kilograms behind them, and a large percentage can be a small real change. It is one trial in one population. Ask a doctor or pharmacist about interactions before taking melatonin.

  • Do not take melatonin for fat loss on the strength of one trial.

Amstrup 2016 · McDonald 2020a

Supplements studied in womenMelatonin raised bone density a little in two trials of postmenopausal womenA year of nightly melatonin raised femoral neck bone density by 1.4 percent against placebo, more at 3 mg than at 1 mg.AdvancedThin evidence

The trial randomised 81 postmenopausal women with osteopenia to 1 mg, 3 mg or placebo. Femoral neck density rose 0.5 percent on 1 mg, which was not significant, and 2.3 percent on 3 mg. A second one-year trial combined 5 mg of melatonin with strontium citrate, vitamin D3 and vitamin K2 and reported gains of 4.3 percent at the lumbar spine and 2.2 percent at the femoral neck, so melatonin's own share cannot be separated. Neither trial measured fractures. Low bone density is a medical condition, and any supplement should be cleared with the doctor managing it.

Amstrup 2015 · Maria 2017 · McDonald 2020a

Supplements studied in womenIn menopausal women melatonin helped sleep mainly where sleep was already poorA systematic review of 24 studies found better sleep quality in postmenopausal women with existing sleep problems, and symptom relief at 3 mg or more.IntermediateSome support

The review covered 1,173 participants. It found a fair amount of evidence for favourable effects on bone density and body mass index, improved sleep recordings and subjective sleep quality in postmenopausal women whose sleep was already impaired, and improvement in one or more areas of menopausal symptoms at doses of 3 mg and above. It found no independent effect on blood pressure or glucose markers, and some evidence that triglycerides rise. Menopausal symptoms and sleep problems have several causes and several treatments. A doctor or pharmacist should be asked first, including about interactions.

  • Raise menopausal sleep problems with a doctor; ask about melatonin there if you wish.

Treister‐Goltzman 2021 · McDonald 2020a

Supplements studied in womenIron deficiency is common in female athletes and is found by a blood testA systematic review reports that up to 60 percent of female athletes experience iron deficiency, which lowered endurance performance by 3 to 4 percent.BeginnerSome support

The review covered 23 studies and 669 high-level female athletes, and defined deficiency as serum ferritin under 40 micrograms per litre. Endurance performance and aerobic capacity improved when deficient athletes were treated with iron. Effects on strength and anaerobic power were inconsistent, and most studies had 20 or fewer athletes per group. Fatigue has many causes, and iron status cannot be judged from symptoms. Iron is a treatment for a measured deficiency, so the dose and form are for a doctor to set after a blood test.

  • Ask a doctor for a blood test if you have persistent, unexplained fatigue.
  • Do not start iron tablets on a guess.

Pengelly 2025

Supplements studied in womenTaking more of a stimulant product than the label says to speed fat lossThe serious harms linked to stimulant fat burners came with overdosing or combining them, and extra dose adds risk without adding much loss.MistakeBeginnerSome support

McDonald is blunt on this point: the deaths attributed to ephedrine products involved people taking several times the recommended amount or combining it with alcohol or recreational drugs, and the belief that more is better is the real hazard. Even at trial doses, a meta-analysis found two to three and a half times the odds of palpitations and of psychiatric, autonomic or gastrointestinal symptoms. Stimulants raise heart rate and blood pressure and disturb sleep, and women on oral contraceptives clear caffeine more slowly. The possible gain is small: about 0.9 kg a month in trials of the strongest agent.

  • Never exceed a labelled dose or combine stimulant products.
  • Stop and seek medical advice if you get palpitations, chest pain or severe anxiety.
  • Check with a pharmacist if you take any medication.

Shekelle 2003 · Abernethy 1985 · McDonald 2015o

Supplements studied in womenTreating melatonin as a proven aid for PCOS or premenstrual symptomsPooled trials in polycystic ovary syndrome found no effect on hormones or metabolic markers, and no trial has tested it for premenstrual symptoms.MistakeIntermediateSome support

McDonald's 2020 article was optimistic about polycystic ovary syndrome on the strength of small trials. One, in 56 women taking 5 mg twice a day for 12 weeks, reported lower testosterone and less excess hair growth. A 2024 meta-analysis of six randomised trials found only a rise in antioxidant capacity, with no pooled effect on testosterone, hair growth score, insulin, lipids or pregnancy rate. His article predates it. For premenstrual symptoms he states plainly that nobody had tested melatonin and that his suggestion was speculation. Both conditions are diagnosed and treated by doctors.

  • Discuss PCOS or severe premenstrual symptoms with a doctor before trying any supplement.

Ziaei 2024 · Jamilian 2019 · McDonald 2020a

Reading the researchHormonal status differs between women, and a study should say who it testedNatural cycles, hormonal contraception, cycle disturbances, raised androgens and menopause are different hormonal states.IntermediateCoaching practice

McDonald groups the things that alter a woman's hormonal picture under one heading: contraception, raised androgens as in polycystic ovary syndrome, a disturbed or absent cycle, and menopause. The 2021 research guide asks the same of scientists. It says study designs should account for the menstrual cycle, hormonal contraceptive use, pregnancy and menopause, and should define each group. A finding in women with natural cycles may not hold for women in another state, and a study that does not report status is harder to apply. None of this is a reason to change a medication. That decision belongs with a doctor.

  • Check whether a study says its participants had natural cycles, used contraception or were postmenopausal.

McDonald 2015p · Elliott-Sale 2021

Reading the researchAbout a third of participants in sport science studies are womenAcross 5,261 papers from 2014 to 2020, 34 percent of participants were female, and 6 percent of studies were women-only against 31 percent men-only.BeginnerSome support

The count covered six sport and exercise journals and 12.5 million participants. An earlier audit of three journals over three years found 39 percent female participants. A repeat for 2021 to 2023 found 44 percent, with 7 percent of studies enrolling only women and 19 percent only men. In that newest audit just 5.6 percent of studies took menstrual status into account in their design. The gap is narrowing, slowly. For a reader it means that many training and nutrition findings were established in men first, and some have never been checked in women.

  • Read the participants line of a study before applying its result.

Cowley 2021 · Costello 2014 · Ose 2025

Reading the researchWhy the research base on women is thinOrganised sport admitted women late, and the menstrual cycle makes a well-controlled study slower and more expensive to run.BeginnerCoaching practice

McDonald gives two main reasons. First, there were few sportswomen to study for most of the last century. In his history, women's weightlifting had no world championship until 1987 and no Olympic event until 2000, so athletes and coaching methods alike came from men. Second, a man's hormones are flat from day to day, while a woman's must be measured at a known point in a cycle whose length varies. The only certain marker is the first day of bleeding, confirming ovulation takes hormone tests, and a missed window means waiting a month. A 2021 research guide adds a shortage of specialist knowledge.

McDonald 2016o · McDonald 2018b · Elliott-Sale 2021

Reading the researchA diet rule given as a percentage can fail a smaller personWomen did not load muscle glycogen on a 75 percent carbohydrate diet until total energy was raised too, because their absolute intake was too low.IntermediateThin evidence

In 1995, four days at 75 percent carbohydrate raised muscle glycogen 41 percent in seven male endurance athletes and not at all in eight female athletes. It looked like a sex difference in storage. A 2001 follow-up with six men and six women tested the same percentage with and without about 34 percent extra energy. The men loaded on both diets. The women loaded only with the extra energy, and enzyme activity did not differ by sex. The authors concluded that low energy intake explained the earlier result. McDonald uses the case to show that an apparent sex difference can be a body-size effect.

  • Convert percentage-based advice into grams per kg of your body weight.
  • Check that the absolute amount matches what the study participants ate.

Tarnopolsky 1995 · Tarnopolsky 2001 · McDonald 2016o

Reading the researchA smaller body leaves less room in the diet for extrasSmaller women burn less energy, so tactics sized for a large man can take up most of a day's calories.BeginnerCoaching practice

This is McDonald's practical point, not a trial finding. A lighter person with less lean mass has a lower daily energy expenditure, and during a diet the gap between what she eats and her minimum needs for protein and fat is narrow. A high-carbohydrate day, a generous free meal or a snack allowance that fits easily into a 2,800 kcal budget may not fit into one of 1,700. He argues this is one reason diet advice written around men often needs resizing for women, and why separate phases for fat loss and for performance can work better than trying to do both at once.

  • Scale any fixed-size diet tactic to your own maintenance calories.
  • Set protein first, then see what room is left.

McDonald 2016o

Reading the researchWeight-loss trials are the exception: most participants are womenIn 244 lifestyle weight-loss trials, samples averaged 73 percent women, and 32 percent of trials enrolled women only.BeginnerSome support

The review covered trials published from 1999 to 2009 with 95,207 participants. Only 5 percent of trials enrolled men alone. McDonald points to this as the reverse of the sports science problem: much of what is known about dieting and weight maintenance was learned largely in women, and researchers have asked whether it transfers fully to men. For a woman reading diet research the practical meaning is favourable. Findings on adherence, hunger and regain are more likely to describe people like her than findings from a lifting study are. Most of these trials enrolled people with overweight, not lean athletes.

Pagoto 2012 · McDonald 2016o

Reading the researchTreating old warnings about women and lifting as medical findingsClaims that sport masculinises women or harms fertility began as untested assertion. The sex-specific problems found since have other causes.MistakeBeginnerSome support

McDonald's history traces these claims to early twentieth-century doctors who reasoned from assumption, and notes that examinations of sportswomen at the time found none of the predicted harm. Problems that did appear once many women trained hard have specific causes. Lost cycles follow from too little energy intake, as controlled experiments show. Men's testosterone sits more than 15-fold above women's with no overlap, and muscle gain in women-only trials is moderate. In his reading, the muscular build of some elite athletes reflects who is drawn to a sport and succeeds in it, not what the sport did to them.

  • Weigh a warning by whether a study stands behind it.
  • Take real symptoms, such as a lost cycle or leaking urine, to a doctor.
