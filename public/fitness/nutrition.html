Energy and maintenanceBody weight follows energy balance, but it moves slowlyEat above what you burn and weight rises, eat below and it falls, but the change takes months to show its full size.BeginnerSome support

A mathematical model of adult metabolism found that body weight responds slowly to a change in intake, with a half time of about one year. It also estimated that a persistent gap of only about 30 kJ (7 kcal) a day lies behind the average weight gain seen in the US. In a ward study of 20 adults, weight change tracked energy intake closely (r = 0.8). Small daily gaps add up, and a single day rarely shows.

  • Judge a calorie target over weeks, not days.

Hall 2011 · Hall 2019

Energy and maintenanceCalorie calculators give a starting point, not your maintenanceEven the best resting-rate equation is within 10 percent of measured for most people, and many individuals fall outside it.BeginnerWell supported

A systematic review compared four equations for resting metabolic rate. Mifflin-St Jeor predicted within 10 percent of the measured value for more people than the others, and it still missed for a share of individuals. Total daily need is that resting figure multiplied by an activity level, and the FAO/WHO/UNU report gives about 1.40 to 2.40 as the range sustained by free-living adults. A wide range of activity levels means a wide range of totals.

  • Eat your estimate for two to three weeks while weighing yourself daily.
  • Adjust by 100 to 200 kcal if weight moves the wrong way.

Energy and maintenanceOn a proposed net energy scale, protein counts for less than its label valueLabels count protein at 4 kcal (17 kJ) per gram. On a net energy scale the FAO describes but has not adopted, it is worth 3.2 kcal (13 kJ).AdvancedThin evidence

Label factors (see the Atwater entry) count energy the body absorbs and does not lose in urine. The FAO also describes a net scale that subtracts heat lost in handling each nutrient, from the thermic effect (see the TEF entry) and gut fermentation. On it, protein drops from 17 to 13 kJ a gram, a 24 percent cut, fibre from 2.0 to 1.4 kcal, and fat stays at 9. For most mixed diets the total falls only 4 to 6 percent, and the FAO workshop kept the older factors. McDonald mentions the lower protein value as an open debate.

  • Keep logging with label values, since targets and databases are built on them.

Each diet sat about 3,138 kJ (750 kcal) a day below estimated needs. Losses were 9.3, 9.9 and 8.4 kg, with no difference, and insulin sensitivity improved on all three; 22 people kept their losses over 24 weeks of free living. A report from the CALERIE trial in overweight adults found a low-GL diet helped only those with high insulin secretion.   • Set the calorie deficit first and treat GI as a minor detail.

Energy and maintenanceCalories from sugary drinks were not offset by eating lessIn a crossover trial, 450 kcal a day of jelly beans was matched by eating less, while the same calories as soda were simply added on and weight rose.BeginnerThin evidence

Fifteen adults took 1,880 kJ (about 450 kcal) of carbohydrate a day for four weeks as soda and four weeks as jelly beans. With the solid load they ate less of everything else, a compensation of 118 percent. With soda they ate no less, and body weight and BMI rose only in that period. A review of beverages describes the same weak compensation for drinks sweetened with sugar or high-fructose corn syrup, and for alcohol (see the alcohol energy entry). McDonald's view is that the liquid form is the problem whatever the sugar, and that milk, with its protein and fat, behaves more like food.

  • McDonald's practice: swap sugary drinks for water or diet drinks (see the diet drinks claim).

Energy and maintenanceA planned surplus added fat but no extra lean mass in young elite athletesOver 8 to 12 weeks of added strength work, elite athletes given a surplus meal plan gained more weight and fat than those eating freely, and similar lean mass.IntermediateThin evidence

Thirty-nine elite athletes, about 19 years old, were randomised. The meal-plan group ate about 3,585 kcal a day against 2,964 kcal. Body weight rose 3.9 percent against 1.5 percent and fat mass 15 percent against 3 percent, while lean mass gain did not differ and one-rep maxes rose 6 to 12 percent in both. McDonald's view is that the surplus muscle gain needs is small and shrinks with training age, so extra food mostly adds fat (see the surplus entries in the diet guide).

  • McDonald's practice: check skinfolds and gym strength every two weeks and adjust intake to the results.

ProteinMost lifters do well on 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kg a dayAbout 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg (0.7 to 1.0 g per lb) of body weight a day covers most people who lift to build muscle.BeginnerWell supported

The ISSN position stand calls 1.4 to 2.0 g/kg a day sufficient for most exercising people. A meta-analysis of 74 trials found added protein helped lean mass in adults under 65 who ate 1.6 g/kg or more. A dose-response review of 105 trials (5,402 participants) found each extra 0.1 g/kg added 0.39 kg of lean mass below 1.3 g/kg a day and 0.12 kg above it. Returns shrink as intake rises.

  • Set a daily target in grams from your body weight, then check it weekly.
  • Take the lower half of the range if food budget is tight.

Jäger 2017 · Nunes 2022b · Tagawa 2021 · Iraki 2019

ProteinHigh protein does not harm kidney function in healthy adultsIn healthy adults without kidney disease, higher protein intakes did not reduce kidney filtration in trials.BeginnerWell supported

A meta-analysis pooled 28 trials with 1,358 participants who ate 1.5 g/kg a day or more, or at least 20 percent of energy, against lower intakes. Filtration rate was slightly higher after high-protein eating, and the change from baseline did not differ between groups. The trials were short and had some risk of selection bias. This does not cover people who already have kidney disease. They should ask their clinician.

  • If you have kidney disease or a family history, check your protein plan with a clinician first.

Devries 2018

ProteinSpread protein over several meals, but the split matters less than the totalAbout 0.25 to 0.55 g per kg per meal is a sound target (20 to 40 g for most people), and the daily total is what matters most.IntermediateSome support

The ISSN stand suggests 0.25 g/kg or 20 to 40 g per serving, spread across the day. A bodybuilding review pairs 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg a day with 0.40 to 0.55 g/kg per meal over 3 to 6 meals. Muscle protein synthesis levelled off at about 0.24 g/kg in younger men and 0.40 g/kg in older men. In eight adults, even meals raised 24-hour synthesis 25 percent more than a skewed pattern. A review calls the distribution evidence limited and inconsistent, and a tracer study found 100 g produced a larger, longer response than 25 g, so there is no hard cap per meal.

  • Aim for three to five protein-containing meals.
  • Do not worry about one bigger protein meal.

ProteinPlant protein builds muscle about as well, given enough total proteinWhen total protein is adequate, plant and animal sources give similar muscle and strength gains over months.IntermediateWell supported

A meta-analysis of 18 studies found protein source did not change absolute lean mass or strength, though animal protein favoured percent lean mass, and younger adults gained 0.41 kg more lean mass with animal protein. In a 12-week trial, 19 vegans and 19 omnivores on 1.6 g/kg gained muscle alike. A soy-versus-animal meta-analysis of nine studies agreed. Single-meal studies do show a lower response to soy and wheat, which is why total intake matters.

  • If you eat no animal foods, aim for the upper half of the protein range.
  • Mix soy, legumes and grains across the day.

ProteinMcDonald argues per-meal protein caps ignore breakdown and non-muscle tissueIn young adults, a meal with about 70 g of protein gave better whole-body protein balance than one with 40 g, mostly by cutting protein breakdown.AdvancedThin evidence

McDonald argues that per-meal caps rest on studies measuring muscle protein synthesis after one dose of protein, often whey taken alone, and that they ignore breakdown and the large share of lean mass that is not muscle. In 23 healthy young adults, net whole-body protein balance was greater after about 70 g than after 40 g, mainly through less breakdown, with or without prior lifting. That is whole-body data, not muscle. For later tracer work with larger doses, see the per-meal entry.

ProteinIn six knee-surgery patients, tendon and cartilage made protein at rates near muscle'sIn six patients having knee replacement, basal protein synthesis in tendon, ligament, cartilage and menisci did not differ significantly from muscle.AdvancedThin evidence

During surgery, a labelled amino acid was infused and tissue samples taken. Rates ran from 0.02 to 0.13 percent an hour across tissues: tendon about 0.06, cruciate ligaments 0.04 to 0.07, cartilage and menisci 0.04, against 0.04 for muscle. Only synovium ran higher, and bone from the knee notch lower. The six were in their sixties and measured at rest during surgery, so this says nothing yet about training. McDonald reads it as support for his view that protein needs are not set by muscle alone.

ProteinMcDonald sets lifters at 2.2 g per kg of protein, and high-level athletes higherMcDonald's practice: about 2.2 g per kg (1 g per lb) for recreational lifters outside a diet, and 2.5 to 3.0 g per kg (1.1 to 1.4 g per lb) for high-level strength athletes.IntermediateCoaching practice

His reasons are practical. People who aim at 1 g per lb tend to land below it, and he sees no harm in high intakes as long as carbohydrate and fat still fit. The evidence is less firm than the target. He draws the higher range from a 2004 review, though that review found few outcome data showing 2 to 3 g per kg is needed, and pooled trials show returns shrinking as intake rises (see the protein range entry). With kidney disease, set protein with a clinician (see the kidney entry).

  • McDonald's practice: aim at 1 g per lb, since most people land below their target.

McDonald 2019l · Tipton 2004

ProteinHigher protein did not harm bone, and may help the spine slightlyA meta-analysis of 16 trials and 20 cohort studies found no harm to bone from higher protein, and a possible small gain in lumbar spine density.IntermediateSome support

In McDonald's account, the worry began with early studies feeding purified protein, which saw calcium lost from the body. The pooled analysis found lumbar spine density may be 0.52 percent higher with more protein, from five trials, and no effect at the hip, femoral neck or whole body. Limited evidence did not support an extra benefit from protein combined with calcium and vitamin D, the pairing his 2007 article leans on; the analysis came later. Studies varied, and confounding could not be ruled out.

Shams-White 2017 · McDonald 2007k

ProteinWhey and casein differ most when eaten alone, less in a mixed mealIn a single-protein meal, casein left more protein in the body over 7 hours than whey did. Early results suggest added carbohydrate and fat narrow the gap.AdvancedThin evidence

In the first study, whey raised whole-body protein synthesis by 68 percent and burned more amino acids, while casein cut protein breakdown by 34 percent; net leucine balance over 7 hours favoured casein. That was whole-body data in rested adults, not muscle after training, and McDonald's article adds that the protein was taken alone after an overnight fast. The same group's preliminary results found added fat and carbohydrate blunted the differences, and they suggest a fast protein may suit older people better.

  • McDonald's practice: before or during training, take quick-digesting whey, since slow protein sits in the gut.

Boirie 1997 · Dangin 2002 · McDonald 2008ah

ProteinAfter whole-body training, 40 g of whey beat 20 g for muscle protein synthesisIn 30 resistance-trained young men, 40 g of whey after a whole-body session raised muscle protein synthesis about 20 percent more than 20 g, whatever their lean mass.IntermediateThin evidence

The accepted ceiling was 20 to 25 g, and McDonald's article says that figure came from leg-only training. Here men with lean mass of 65 kg (143 lb) or less and 70 kg (154 lb) or more did a whole-body session, then took 20 or 40 g of whey. Synthesis rose from 0.049 to 0.059 percent an hour with the larger dose, and lean mass made no difference. McDonald's view is that doubling the dose bought a modest gain, but his article notes only the thigh was sampled, so the other trained muscles may have gained too. See the per-meal entry.

  • After a full-body session, consider nearer 40 g than 20 g; the evidence is one short study.

Macnaughton 2016 · McDonald 2016n

ProteinWithout animal foods, plan for vitamin B12, and expect less iron to be absorbedB12 depletion was common among vegetarians, more so vegans. In people with no iron stores, about 5 to 12 percent of iron is absorbed from vegetarian diets, 14 to 18 from mixed.IntermediateSome support

A review of 18 studies of vegetarians found B12 deficiency in 62 percent of pregnant women, 25 to 86 percent of children, 21 to 41 of adolescents and 11 to 90 of older people, and its authors advise regular B12 supplements. The iron figures are estimates. Phytate, polyphenols, calcium, vitamin C and meat changed iron absorption in single-meal studies, less so across varied diets, and iron status matters more than diet. McDonald's view is that lean red meat is a strong source of iron and zinc, and that athletes who drop it, women especially, risk running short.

  • If you eat no animal foods, ask a clinician or dietitian about a B12 source and testing.
  • Take iron only after a blood test and a clinician's advice (see the women's iron entry); McDonald warns excess harms men.

ProteinSoy did not upset thyroid hormones in healthy adults with enough iodineAcross 18 randomised trials, soy did not change thyroid hormones and nudged TSH up very slightly. People treated for an underactive thyroid are a separate case.IntermediateSome support

A 2019 meta-analysis found no change in free T3 or free T4 and a TSH rise of about 0.25 mIU/L, of unclear clinical meaning. An earlier review of 14 trials in healthy adults found no or only very modest effects in all but one. That review notes that soy may cut absorption of thyroid hormone medicine and raise the dose some patients need, though they need not avoid soy. Animal and lab work suggests low iodine makes soy's effect on the thyroid worse. McDonald's view is that trouble comes from very high soy intakes paired with low iodine.

  • If you take thyroid medicine and eat soy often, tell your clinician, as your dose may need checking.
  • If you eat a lot of soy, make sure your iodine intake is adequate.

ProteinSoy protein and isoflavones did not change men's testosteroneA meta-analysis of 41 studies found no effect of soy foods, soy protein or isoflavone extracts on testosterone or oestrogen in men, at any dose or duration tested.BeginnerWell supported

Total testosterone was measured in 1,753 men and oestradiol in 1,000. Total and free testosterone, oestradiol, oestrone and SHBG did not change, and splitting the studies by isoflavone dose and length found no effect either. McDonald's 2009 article agrees that moderate intakes left testosterone alone, but argues that large daily intakes can cause problems and suggests a cap of about 20 to 25 g of soy protein a day. This later analysis found no dose effect on these hormones, though it did not look at other outcomes.

  • Men can use soy as a protein source without worrying about testosterone.

Reed 2021 · McDonald 2009an

ProteinChoose lower-mercury fish, above all in pregnancy and for childrenUS FDA and EPA advice lists seven fish to avoid for their mercury, such as swordfish, shark and orange roughy, and many low-mercury picks such as salmon and sardines.BeginnerRulebook

The advice is written for people who are or may become pregnant or breastfeeding, and for children. In pregnancy and breastfeeding it suggests 2 to 3 servings of 4 oz (113 g) a week from its best-choices list, or 1 from its good-choices list; children get 2 smaller best-choice servings. Canned light tuna is a best choice and albacore a good choice; bigeye tuna, king mackerel, marlin and Gulf of Mexico tilefish are on the avoid list. McDonald's view is that lifters who eat large daily amounts of tuna or other high-mercury fish can go past the safety limits.

  • If you are pregnant or planning to be, plan your fish intake with your clinician or midwife.
  • McDonald's practice: if you eat fish most days, favour low-mercury kinds over large daily amounts of tuna.

ProteinEating eggs raw for their proteinIn five people with an ileostomy, about 51 percent of raw egg protein was absorbed by the end of the small intestine, against 91 percent when cooked.MistakeBeginnerThin evidence

Digestibility is the share of eaten protein the gut takes in. Each person ate 25 g of labelled egg protein once cooked and once raw, and heating raised digestibility sharply. The study was tiny and used a measurement taken at the end of the small intestine, but the gap was large. McDonald also warns that raw eggs carry a risk of salmonella food poisoning.

  • Cook eggs before you eat them.

Evenepoel 1998 · McDonald 2008ae · McDonald 2008af

Carbs, fat and fibreCarbohydrate boosts lifting only in long, high-volume sessionsCarbohydrate intake had little effect on strength training in short fed sessions, and may help when volume is high.IntermediateSome support

A systematic review of 49 studies found that carbohydrate intake per se is unlikely to change strength training performance in a fed state in workouts of up to 10 sets per muscle group. Acute gains showed up mostly in fasted comparisons and higher volumes. For long or very hard sessions, a sports nutrition review gives 30 to 60 g an hour during sessions of more than an hour. Physique-athlete reviews suggest at least 3 to 5 g/kg a day.

  • After protein and fat are set, fill the rest of your calories with carbohydrate you enjoy.

Henselmans 2022 · Burke 2011a · Iraki 2019

Carbs, fat and fibreKeep fat from falling too low: about 15 to 30 percent of caloriesFat of roughly 0.5 to 1.5 g per kg, or 15 to 30 percent of energy, is a sound band, and very low fat has costs.IntermediateSome support

Reviews for lean physique athletes suggest 15 to 30 percent of calories from fat, or 0.5 to 1.5 g/kg in the off-season, with a low end of 10 to 25 percent in contest preparation. A meta-analysis of six trials (206 men) found low-fat diets lowered total testosterone (standardised difference -0.38) against high-fat diets. This is mainly a floor for hormones and diet quality, not a target to push higher.

  • Do not cut fat below about 0.5 g per kg of body weight for long.

Iraki 2019 · Helms 2014b · Roberts 2020a · Whittaker 2021

Carbs, fat and fibreAim for 25 to 29 g of fibre a dayIn large analyses, health benefits from fibre were greatest at about 25 to 29 g a day, with more possibly better.BeginnerWell supported

A series of meta-analyses covered 185 prospective studies and 58 trials with 4,635 adults. Observational data suggested 15 to 30 percent lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in the highest fibre eaters against the lowest, and trials showed lower body weight, blood pressure and cholesterol with more fibre. Risk reduction was greatest at 25 to 29 g a day. Raise intake gradually and drink fluids with it.

  • Add fibre slowly over a few weeks.

Carbs, fat and fibrePlant omega-3 (ALA) converted poorly to DHA in tracer studies, least in menIn tracer studies, young men made some EPA from ALA but no measurable DHA, while young women converted about 21 percent to EPA and 9 percent to DHA.IntermediateThin evidence

Six young men on their usual diet swallowed labelled ALA with a meal. EPA and DPA appeared in the blood, but no labelled DHA showed up over 21 days, and the authors judged that men may depend on DHA eaten ready-made. A matching study in young women estimated net conversion of 21 percent to EPA, 6 percent to DPA and 9 percent to DHA, which the authors linked to the needs of pregnancy. The men's study had only six people. McDonald's view is that flax and other ALA oils are a poor stand-in for fish. For omega-3 and the heart, see the omega-3 entry in the health section.

  • McDonald's practice: do not count flax or other ALA oils as your EPA and DHA.
  • If you want EPA and DHA, eat fatty fish or another ready-made source.

Carbs, fat and fibreMcDonald argues arachidonic acid and DHA may be the real essential fatsLA and ALA are the textbook essential fats, but much of what they do runs through their products. McDonald's reading is that arachidonic acid (AA) and DHA are the ones the body truly needs.AdvancedThin evidence

A 1999 review held that the need for AA is the main reason omega-6 fats are essential, and that DHA, needed for vision and nerve development, is the more likely essential omega-3, though the need may be for 22-carbon omega-3 fats in general. A hospital team saw no fatty acid deficiency in patients fed by drip on a fish-oil emulsion with AA and DHA but almost no LA or ALA, and in mice AA and DHA alone prevented it. In a tracer study of nine adults, 0.3 to 0.6 percent of LA became AA. The evidence is thin and mostly not from healthy adults.

Spector 1999 · Le 2009 · Demmelmair 1999 · McDonald 2020f

Carbs, fat and fibreKeep industrial trans fat as close to zero as you canTrans fat from partially hydrogenated oils harms blood lipids, and intakes of about 2 percent of energy went with a markedly higher rate of heart disease.BeginnerSome support

A 2009 review names partially hydrogenated vegetable oils as the main source in most countries. Trans fat worsened blood lipids and raised markers of inflammation and poor blood vessel function, and some studies linked it to visceral fat and insulin resistance. In observational data, even about 2 percent of energy from trans fat went with markedly more coronary heart disease. Links with diabetes were less consistent. McDonald treats this as the rare fat question with no real debate.

  • Check ingredient lists for partially hydrogenated oil and choose another product.

Micha 2009 · McDonald 2009c

Carbs, fat and fibreMost adults with lactose intolerance tolerated about a cup of milk's lactoseIn a systematic review, 12 to 15 g of lactose, roughly one cup of milk, was well tolerated by most adults with lactose intolerance or malabsorption.BeginnerSome support

Lactose, the sugar in milk, is split by the enzyme lactase. Without enough lactase it reaches the colon and ferments, causing gas and loose stools. The review of 36 trials rated the cup-of-milk finding moderate quality, but most trials enrolled people with malabsorption rather than diagnosed intolerance, and evidence for lactose-reduced milk, probiotics or gradual adaptation was too thin to judge. Milk allergy is a different, immune reaction to milk protein, and this figure does not apply to it. See also the dairy entry in the diet section.

  • If dairy upsets your stomach, have it assessed by a clinician before cutting it out.
  • With a milk allergy, follow your allergist's advice, not the tolerance figure.

Shaukat 2010 · McDonald 2010a

Carbs, fat and fibreMcDonald warns that high fibre can work against big eatersMore fibre lowered how much fat and protein the gut absorbed in a feeding study. McDonald's view is that this and the fullness help dieters and hinder big eaters.IntermediateThin evidence

In 17 people on controlled diets, each eaten for two weeks, raising fibre lowered fat and protein digestibility and the usable energy of the diet. McDonald's estimate is that going from about 18 to 36 g of fibre a day costs around 100 kcal absorbed, an effect he says is not large. His general starting point is 5 to 10 g of fibre a meal. For lifters trying to gain or athletes who burn a great deal, his concern is that bulk and lost absorption make eating enough harder.

  • McDonald's practice: if you struggle to eat enough, eat the salad or vegetables last.

Baer 1997 · McDonald 2009p

Carbs, fat and fibreA lower glycaemic index does not always mean less insulinIn six healthy men, a low-GI bran cereal raised insulin sooner than high-GI corn flakes. Its lower GI came from faster glucose removal, not slower entry.AdvancedThin evidence

Both cereals supplied 50 g of available carbohydrate, and glucose entered the blood at a similar rate. With the bran cereal, insulin was 76 percent higher at 20 minutes and glucose left the blood faster, which flattened the curve. In 30 people with type 2 diabetes, adding about 21 g of protein to 50 g of a slowly digested sugar raised insulin by roughly 190 to 270 percent, but insulin action fell and the glucose response did not change. McDonald's view is that choosing foods by GI to keep insulin down rests on a false assumption.

Schenk 2003 · Ang 2012 · McDonald 2016d

Carbs, fat and fibreThe same cereal scored a different glycaemic index in trained and sedentary menOne breakfast cereal's GI differed by about 23 units between endurance-trained and sedentary young men, so a published value may not fit everyone.AdvancedThin evidence

In 34 young men with normal glucose tolerance, moderately trained men fell between the two groups, and the insulin index did not differ. An earlier study by the same team found the gap for one of three cereals, with a second close to significance. McDonald's reading is that GI runs lower in trained people, and he puts this down to their better insulin sensitivity.

Mettler 2007 · Mettler 2006 · McDonald 2016d

Carbs, fat and fibreHigh-fructose corn syrup behaves much like table sugarHFCS and sucrose are both mixes of fructose and glucose, and in 34 adults they gave similar 24-hour glucose, insulin, leptin and blood fat profiles.BeginnerThin evidence

McDonald's article lists the common forms, HFCS-42 and HFCS-55, named for their fructose percentage; sucrose is half fructose. A review found HFCS no different in any meaningful way from sucrose, honey or juice concentrates, and judged the idea that it uniquely drives obesity unsupported. In eight of the men, though, blood fats after HFCS or sucrose drinks rose about as high as after pure fructose. McDonald's view is that the problem is the calories and the soft drinks that carry HFCS, not the syrup itself (see the liquid calories entry).

  • Count HFCS as added sugar, the same as sucrose or honey.

White 2008 · Stanhope 2008 · McDonald 2009r

Carbs, fat and fibreCutting vegetable oils for fear that omega-6 fat inflames the bodyIn 15 randomised trials of healthy people, adding linoleic acid, the main omega-6 fat, did not raise blood markers of inflammation.MistakeIntermediateSome support

The worry is that linoleic acid becomes arachidonic acid, a raw material for signals that drive inflammation. A systematic review of 15 trials (8 parallel, 7 crossover) in healthy, non-infant groups found no significant change in C-reactive protein, fibrinogen, cytokines, TNF-alpha or adhesion molecules with more linoleic acid. Two trials did see shifts in urinary eicosanoid products, which their authors did not read as more inflammation. The review covered markers, not disease. McDonald's view is that the larger gap in most modern diets is too little omega-3 rather than too much omega-6.

  • McDonald's practice: put effort into omega-3 sources; he leaves open whether omega-6 needs cutting.

Johnson 2012 · McDonald 2009c

Carbs, fat and fibreRuling out a food for its glycaemic index without looking at the portionGI is measured on a serving that holds 50 g of available carbohydrate, so a high value says little about a vegetable eaten in small amounts.MistakeBeginnerCoaching practice

In a GI test, fasted volunteers eat enough of a food to provide 50 g of available carbohydrate. For foods low in carbohydrate that can mean an unrealistic plate: McDonald's estimate for carrots is about a dozen medium ones. Glycaemic load scales GI by the carbohydrate actually eaten, and the international tables list it for stated serving sizes. McDonald's view is that banning carrots or similar foods over their GI makes no sense.

  • McDonald's practice: judge a vegetable by the portion you eat, not by its GI alone.

Foster-Powell 2002 · Mettler 2007 · McDonald 2016d

Carbs, fat and fibreJudging a low-carbohydrate diet by urine ketone stripsStrips detect ketones spilling into urine, not blood levels or fat loss. McDonald's view is that they can read negative during ketosis and that fat lost is what counts.MistakeIntermediateCoaching practice

McDonald's view is that ketosis is defined by blood ketones, that urine readings also shift with how much you drink, and that lean, active people may make too few ketones to show. In his view ketones rise because fat is being broken down, so a darker strip does not mean faster fat loss (see the ketosis term and the ketogenic diet entries in the diet guide). People with diabetes who test ketones for medical reasons should follow their clinician, not this entry.

  • McDonald's practice: judge the diet by fat lost and muscle kept, not by strip colour.

McDonald 2009v · McDonald 2008p

Timing, fluids and alcoholProtein timing around training matters little next to the daily totalEating protein straight after training is fine, but a precise window does not change results once daily intake is met.BeginnerWell supported

A systematic review of 65 studies with 2,907 participants found protein supplementation raised lean body mass in adults and older adults, and subgroup analyses showed no benefit from any specific timing. The ISSN position says the anabolic effect of exercise lasts at least 24 hours. A protein-containing meal before or after training is a reasonable habit, and it is not urgent.

  • Eat a protein meal within a few hours either side of training.

Wirth 2020 · Jäger 2017

Timing, fluids and alcoholPre-workout eating helps in long sessions, and little else about timing is criticalDaily totals decide results, and a meal before training mostly helps if you train fasted or do high volume.IntermediateSome support

The joint position stand of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, Dietitians of Canada and the ACSM says performance and recovery improve with well-chosen type, amount and timing of food and fluids. In the carbohydrate review, the acute benefits appeared mostly in fasted comparisons and workouts above 10 sets per muscle group. Physique reviews suggest a protein dose of 0.4 to 0.5 g/kg before and after sessions as a convenient pattern.

  • Eat a normal meal one to three hours before hard sessions.

Timing, fluids and alcoholBeing under-hydrated lowers strength and muscle enduranceHypohydration reduced strength by about 5.5 percent and muscle endurance by about 8 percent in a meta-analysis.BeginnerWell supported

A meta-analysis of 28 papers found hypohydration, meaning a body fluid deficit, cut muscle endurance by 8.3 percent, strength by 5.5 percent and anaerobic power by 5.8 percent. Dehydrating by exercise hurt more than passive methods, and trained people tended to lose less. This is about ordinary fluid habits. Deliberate dehydration to make a weight class is risky and needs an experienced coach and clinician.

  • Start sessions well hydrated and drink during long or hot ones.
  • Never use extreme dehydration to change weight quickly.

Savoie 2015

Timing, fluids and alcoholHeavy drinking after hard training blunts the muscle repair signalA very large dose of alcohol after training cut muscle protein synthesis by 24 to 37 percent in one small study.IntermediateThin evidence

Eight active men did a hard session of legs and cycling, then drank alcohol at 1.5 g/kg (about 12 standard drinks) with either protein or carbohydrate. Muscle protein synthesis was 24 percent lower with alcohol and protein, and 37 percent lower with alcohol and carbohydrate, than with protein alone. That is one small study at a heavy dose. Effects of one or two drinks were not tested.

  • Keep drinking light on the days you most want to recover.

Parr 2014

Timing, fluids and alcoholAlcohol adds calories that people do not offset by eating lessAcross 22 studies, people did not eat less after drinking, so total energy for the day rose.BeginnerWell supported

A meta-analysis of 22 studies with 701 participants found no compensation for alcoholic energy: food intake rose by 343 kJ (about 82 kcal), and total energy intake rose by 1,072 kJ (about 256 kcal). Alcohol itself provides 7 kcal per gram on the standard Atwater scale, between carbohydrate and fat. Counting drinks in your log is the only way to see this.

  • Log drinks like food, and count mixers.

Kwok 2019 · FAO 2003

Timing, fluids and alcoholCarbohydrate eaten straight after hard exercise refills glycogen fastestAfter depleting exercise, glycogen refilled fastest with carbohydrate taken at once, and a delay of hours could halve the rate. McDonald thinks this matters mainly for same-day sessions.AdvancedSome support

A review of short-term recovery found the highest muscle glycogen synthesis with about 1.0 to 1.85 g/kg of carbohydrate an hour, started straight after exercise and repeated every 15 to 60 minutes for up to 5 hours. A delay of several hours may cut the rate by about half. Added protein raised synthesis only when carbohydrate was under about 1.2 g/kg an hour. McDonald's view is that with a day between hard sessions for the same muscles, timing and GI make no real difference, and he keeps fast, high-GI refuelling for sessions six to eight hours apart.

  • With two hard sessions in one day, eat carbohydrate soon after the first.

Jentjens 2003 · McDonald 2016d

Timing, fluids and alcoholCarbohydrate loading helps in endurance events of more than about 90 minutesRaising muscle glycogen above normal delayed fatigue by about 20 percent in events over 90 minutes, and did not help efforts under 5 minutes or runs and rides of 60 to 90.IntermediateSome support

A 1997 review found that after 60 to 90 minutes of moderate running or cycling, plenty of glycogen is still left in the working muscles, so extra stores add nothing. Beyond 90 minutes, exhaustion tended to coincide with very low muscle glycogen. In races over a set distance, high-carbohydrate diets improved performance by 2 to 3 percent. McDonald's view is that a recreational 5 km runner gains nothing from loading and may only carry extra water, and that slower marathoners, out on the course longest, are the ones at risk of running low.

  • For events under an hour, eat your normal diet beforehand instead of loading.

Hawley 1997 · McDonald 2015k

Timing, fluids and alcoholA short all-out effort and one day of high carbohydrate loaded glycogen in a small studyIn seven endurance-trained athletes, 3 minutes of very hard cycling then 24 hours at about 10 g/kg of high-GI carbohydrate nearly doubled muscle glycogen.AdvancedThin evidence

Published loading plans took two to six days; McDonald's article recounts that the first versions began with several days of low carbohydrate and hard training. Here athletes rode 150 seconds at 130 percent of peak aerobic capacity, then 30 seconds all out, and ate 10.3 g of carbohydrate per kg of body weight over the next day. Glycogen rose from about 109 to 198 mmol/kg wet weight, similar to or above longer plans. It was seven people with no comparison group. McDonald's view is that the total carbohydrate eaten matters more than which plan delivers it.

  • McDonald's practice: test any pre-race meal in training, never for the first time on race day.

Fairchild 2002 · McDonald 2015k

Timing, fluids and alcoholBodybuilders who carb-loaded before a show measured bigger, and many had diarrhoeaAfter three low-carbohydrate days, 15 male bodybuilders who loaded for 24 hours gained muscle thickness and photo scores; 9 who did not load had no change in thickness or girths.AdvancedThin evidence

The study compared men who did and did not load, with no random allocation reported. Ultrasound thickness of the elbow flexors and triceps rose with loading, as did body mass and several girths. Diarrhoea hit 7 of the 15 loaders, 4 of them severely, and constipation was common in both groups during depletion. McDonald calls the effect of loading debatable and recalls a study finding no change in muscle size; this 2019 study, which his article links, found a short-term rise. Big peak-week swings in food and fluid can bring gut upset like this, and cutting fluid risks dehydration.

  • Plan any peak-week carbohydrate load with a sports dietitian or an experienced coach.

de Moraes 2019 · McDonald 2015k

Timing, fluids and alcoholMilk did well as a drink after sweaty endurance sessions in small trialsLow-fat milk was retained better than water or a sports drink after sweating, and chocolate milk beat a matched carbohydrate drink between two cycling bouts.IntermediateThin evidence

Eleven people sweated off 1.8 percent of body mass in warm-room exercise, then drank 150 percent of the loss. Five-hour urine was about 550 to 610 ml after milk against about 1,180 to 1,210 ml after water or a sports drink, and only milk kept them in fluid balance (see the plain water claim). In nine trained male cyclists, chocolate milk and a fluid-replacement drink both gave longer time to exhaustion than a carbohydrate drink matched to the milk for carbohydrate. McDonald's view is that milk suits recovery but not drinking during exercise, and that big athletes may need extra carbohydrate and protein added.

  • After long, sweaty sessions, milk is a sound recovery drink if you tolerate lactose (see the lactose entry).

Shirreffs 2007 · Karp 2006 · McDonald 2008w

Timing, fluids and alcoholMuscle protein synthesis rose and fell back within hours of a protein doseAfter 48 g of whey, synthesis in eight young men peaked at 45 to 90 minutes and returned to baseline while blood amino acids were still high.IntermediateThin evidence

The authors call this the muscle-full effect. In 24 trained men given 80 g of whey over 12 hours after lifting, 20 g every 3 hours raised synthesis 31 to 48 percent more than 10 g every 90 minutes or 40 g every 6 hours. McDonald's view is that protein meals 3 to 5 hours apart suit muscle gain and that eating more often adds nothing. A later tracer study found a much larger dose gave a longer response, so timing depends on dose (see the protein per meal entry).

  • Space protein meals a few hours apart rather than grazing every hour.

Atherton 2010 · Areta 2013 · Trommelen 2023 · McDonald 2009z · McDonald 2009ap

Timing, fluids and alcoholProtein before bed improved overnight protein balance after evening trainingIn 16 young men who lifted in the evening, 40 g of casein 30 minutes before sleep was digested overnight and improved whole-body protein balance.IntermediateThin evidence

All of them had 20 g of protein and 60 g of carbohydrate straight after the session. With the bedtime casein, whole-body protein synthesis rose and net balance turned positive, and muscle protein synthesis ran about 22 percent higher, a result at the edge of significance. It covered one night, so it says nothing direct about long-term growth. McDonald is unsure it matters, since a large evening meal already supplies the night, and he would not cut sleep short to eat.

  • If you train late, a protein-rich snack before bed is a reasonable option.

Res 2012 · McDonald 2009ap

Timing, fluids and alcoholDrinking more than you sweat out during a long raceIn the 2002 Boston Marathon, 13 percent of 488 sampled runners finished with low blood sodium; weight gain during the race and finishing after 4 hours went with it.MistakeIntermediateThin evidence

Hyponatraemia means too little sodium in the blood, and severe cases can kill. In this group, 0.6 percent had critical levels. Gaining weight while running, a sign of drinking more than was lost, went with about four times the odds, and a time over 4 hours with about seven times the odds of a time under 3:30. Sex, type of drink and anti-inflammatory painkillers were not linked. McDonald's view is that slower marathoners are the ones prone to overdrinking, since fast runners need less and have less chance to drink.

  • Weigh yourself before and after long training runs; finishing heavier means you drank too much.
  • Plan race drinking with a sports dietitian or clinician if you will be out over 4 hours.

Almond 2005 · McDonald 2015k

SupplementsCreatine monohydrate is the best-supported lifting supplementTaking 3 to 5 g of creatine monohydrate a day adds lean mass when combined with training, and it is safe for healthy people.BeginnerWell supported

A meta-analysis of 35 trials with 1,192 participants found creatine added 1.10 kg of lean mass when combined with resistance training. Men gained 1.46 kg, and women a non-significant 0.29 kg, though women were fewer in the data. The ISSN position stand finds supplementation up to 30 g a day for 5 years safe in healthy people. The 3 to 5 g daily dose comes from a physique-athlete review. In 31 men, 3 g a day raised muscle creatine over 28 days by about as much as 20 g a day did in 6 days.

  • Take 3 to 5 g of creatine monohydrate every day.

Delpino 2022 · Kreider 2017 · Iraki 2019 · Hultman 1996

SupplementsCaffeine gives a small, real boost to strength and powerIn a meta-analysis, caffeine raised maximal strength and power slightly, mostly in the upper body.BeginnerWell supported

A meta-analysis of 10 studies on strength and 10 on power found caffeine improved both (standardised differences 0.20 and 0.17). The effect was significant for upper-body strength, and not for lower-body. The review also notes poor blinding and little data for women. The ISSN's 2010 stand found about 3 to 6 mg/kg effective, with no further gain at 9 mg/kg or more; a bodybuilding review lists 5 to 6 mg/kg.

  • Test your dose in training before a key session.

Grgic 2018 · Iraki 2019 · Goldstein 2010

SupplementsBeta-alanine and citrulline help in specific cases, with modest effectsBeta-alanine helps efforts of one to four minutes, and citrulline malate added about three reps per exercise.IntermediateSome support

The ISSN stand says 4 to 6 g of beta-alanine daily for two to four weeks improves exercise performance, most clearly in tasks lasting 1 to 4 minutes, and tingling, the main side effect, is reduced by splitting the dose into amounts of about 1.6 g. A meta-analysis of eight studies (137 participants) found 6 to 8 g of citrulline malate taken 40 to 60 minutes before lifting added about 3 repetitions (6.4 percent). Both effects are small next to training and sleep.

  • Consider these only after protein, creatine and training are in place.

Trexler 2015 · Vårvik 2021

SupplementsMost supplements lack good evidence, and some carry risksThe IOC finds only a few supplements, including caffeine and creatine, have good evidence, and the rest are unproven.BeginnerSome support

The IOC consensus says nutrition makes a small but valuable contribution and few supplements have good evidence of benefit; some risk contamination with banned substances. BCAA pills have no human studies of oral BCAA alone on muscle synthesis. A review of 50 testosterone-booster products found no testosterone data for 61.5 percent of them, and data showing a rise for 24.8 percent.

  • Buy from brands with third-party testing if you compete in a tested sport.

Maughan 2018 · Wolfe 2017 · Clemesha 2020

SupplementsHigh-dose vitamins C and E blunted some training adaptations in trialsIn two randomised trials, daily vitamin C and E dulled muscle markers of adaptation, though fitness and muscle growth mostly kept pace with placebo.IntermediateThin evidence

In 54 young adults doing 11 weeks of mostly running, 1,000 mg of vitamin C and 235 mg of vitamin E a day stopped the rise in markers of new mitochondria, while VO2max rose 8 percent in both groups. In 32 recreationally trained adults lifting for 10 weeks, the same doses did not change muscle growth but blunted signalling after a session and held back biceps curl strength. The authors urge caution. McDonald's view is to avoid high-dose antioxidants in main training blocks, with a possible place in a crowded competition season.

  • McDonald's practice: get antioxidants from fruit and vegetables, not high-dose pills, while training to improve.

Paulsen 2014b · Paulsen 2014a · McDonald 2011a · McDonald 2010s

SupplementsMany athletes test low in vitamin D, but the count depends on the cut-offAt a cut-off of 32 ng/mL, 56 percent of 2,313 athletes were low, more in winter and in indoor sports. The IOM counts 20 ng/mL as enough for nearly everyone.IntermediateSome support

The meta-analysis pooled 23 studies; risk above 40 degrees north rose only once Middle East studies were excluded, and any link to injury was unclear. The Institute of Medicine's 2011 report set an adult RDA of 600 IU a day (800 IU over 70), tied to a blood level of at least 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L). It found higher levels gave no consistent extra benefit, saw some risk at both extremes, and judged deficiency overestimated. McDonald's articles aim for higher levels and treat supplements as close to essential for indoor athletes. For trial results on vitamin D pills, see the vitamin D entry in the health guide.

  • If you train indoors or through dark winters, ask a clinician whether a vitamin D test is worth doing.
  • Do not take high-dose vitamin D without a clinician's advice.

Farrokhyar 2015 · Ross 2011 · McDonald 2011a · McDonald 2010s

SupplementsGlutamine added no strength or lean mass in a training trialIn 31 young adults lifting for six weeks, a large daily glutamine dose did no better than placebo for strength, lean mass or muscle breakdown.IntermediateThin evidence

The glutamine group took 0.9 g per kg of lean mass a day against the same amount of maltodextrin. Both groups raised their squat by about 30 percent and bench press by about 14 percent, and lean mass rose about 2 percent in each. Urinary 3-methylhistidine, a marker of muscle protein breakdown, did not differ. McDonald agrees that glutamine does not build muscle, and he rates its evidence for immune support as mixed.

Candow 2001 · McDonald 2010s

SupplementsTaking antioxidant pills as health insuranceIn 78 randomised trials with 296,707 people, antioxidant pills did not lower deaths, and beta-carotene and vitamin E slightly raised them in the better trials.MistakeBeginnerWell supported

The Cochrane review covered beta-carotene, vitamins A, C and E, and selenium, taken for 28 days to 12 years by healthy people and patients with various diseases, mean age 63. Overall there was no clear effect on deaths. In the 56 trials at low risk of bias, deaths rose by 4 percent, beta-carotene and vitamin E raised mortality, and higher vitamin A doses went with more deaths; vitamin C and selenium showed no effect. McDonald's view is that pills cannot replace the fruit and vegetables that supply these nutrients.

  • Check any high-dose vitamin plan with a clinician or dietitian before you start.

Bjelakovic 2012 · McDonald 2011a

Tracking foodLogging food is linked to better results, and frequency matters mostPeople who logged more often lost more, and a rough log kept consistently beats a perfect one kept rarely.BeginnerSome support

A systematic review of 22 studies found a significant association between self-monitoring and weight loss, though the evidence was weak because of method limits. In a 24-week program of 142 people, those losing 5 percent or more of their weight logged in 2.4 times a day against 1.6 for the rest, and time per day fell from about 23 to 15 minutes. These are observational links, not proof logging causes the loss.

  • Log at the time you eat, and aim for most days, not perfect days.

Burke 2011b · Harvey 2019

Tracking foodUS labels may legally run up to 20 percent above the declared caloriesA US food is misbranded only if its calories exceed the declared value by more than 20 percent.BeginnerRulebook

Under 21 CFR 101.9(g)(5), a food is deemed misbranded if the measured calories, sugars, fat, saturated fat, trans fat, cholesterol or sodium exceed the label by more than 20 percent. Measured snack foods did better than that: in 24 products the median was 4.3 percent above the label, in the legal range. Other countries have their own tolerances, so check yours. Treat any label as an estimate.

  • Expect small errors, and weigh food rather than trust serving counts.

Tracking foodUnder-reporting is the norm, even among athletes and dietitiansSelf-reported intake is usually below true intake, by around a fifth in athletes and by more in some groups.BeginnerWell supported

A systematic review of 59 studies of 6,298 adults, using doubly labeled water as the reference, found most reported significant under-reporting. In athletes, intake was 19 percent too low on average. In 20 women, dietitians under-reported by 223 kcal a day and controls by 429. Ten people who said they could not lose weight on under 1,200 kcal actually under-reported by 47 percent, a small group.

  • If weight is not changing, assume your log is low before blaming metabolism.

Burrows 2019 · Capling 2017 · CHAMPAGNE 2002 · Lichtman 1992

Among 105 people diagnosed with an eating disorder, about 75 percent used My Fitness Pal and 73 percent of those said it contributed to their illness. In 122 men, app users had higher eating-disorder symptoms than non-users, and 493 students showed the same link. These are cross-sectional surveys, so the app may attract people already at risk. Take the signal seriously either way.

  • Stop tracking and talk to a clinician if logging brings guilt, fear or rules you cannot break.

  • Use palm, fist, cupped hand and thumb, and check once against a scale.

  • Add 10 to 20 percent for restaurant meals when your goal is losing weight.
