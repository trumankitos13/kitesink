The world keeps turning

The Living Regions

Every region breathes with its own slow seasonal rhythm — the desert blooms after monsoon, the coast rises and falls with the tide. Weather nudges it along, and a thriving rhythm quietly lifts your park's beauty.

  • Tidewater

    The Gulf & Atlantic shore runs on the tide. When the water goes out, a beachcombing flat opens along the shore. A storm at high tide can surge over shore-side sites and flood them shut — you're always warned first, and it only ever closes a site, never destroys it.

  • Snowpack & Avalanche

    As Rockies winters deepen, more sites get snowed in overnight — and when the pack on the ridge gets truly heavy, an avalanche can bury an exposed site below it. Your Maintenance Ranger earns their keep plowing it back open.

  • Desert Drought

    Through the dry seasons the Sonoran desert goes parched — springs draw down and wildfire risk climbs. Relief comes with the monsoon and the flash flood, or you can dig a water tank to keep the park green and fire-safe.

  • The Park Comes Alive

    Your park keeps living when you're not clicking. Region birds drift across the sky — pelicans over the gulf, hawks over the desert, geese over the north woods. After dark, campfires glow at every occupied site.

  • Once a year

    Signature Festivals

    Every region throws its own annual festival — the Foliage Festival in the mountains, Bluebonnet Days on the plains, a Gulf Shrimp Boil on the coast. Deck the park out and word of mouth spreads far and wide.

  • Region-flavored

    Signature Outings

    Host your region's headline guided outing — a Gator Airboat Tour in the bayou, a Fossil Dig on the plains, a Tidepool Walk on the coast, a Foliage Drive in the mountains — for an extra-happy, memorable crowd.

  • Newcomer → Beloved

    Familiar Faces

    Every regular who keeps coming back builds a bond. A handful of named friends turn up over time — a sunrise painter, a record-chasing angler, a thru-hiker — each with a little story that unfolds across their visits.

Who shows up

Five kinds of camper

Each archetype wants different ground, pays differently, and peaks in a different season. Read your region's mix, and build for who's coming.

  • Sightseers

    Peak · Summer → Fall

    Want it beautiful — any site will do. Tip big when the grounds are pretty; travel with family more than most.

  • Anglers

    Peak · Spring → Summer

    Want a site near water — a short walk to the shore. No lake on the map, and expect sour reviews.

  • Buck Hunters

    Peak · Fall

    Want quiet, wooded-edge sites and pay the most. Arrive in a huge fall wave, then vanish for the year.

  • Bird Hunters

    Peak · Fall

    Want open-edge ground near the fields — split off from Buck Hunters, and pay nearly as well.

  • Backpackers

    Peak · Summer

    Low rate, high volume, forgiving. Want a trailhead site and rent from the gear shack.

A career bigger than one park

The Park Service

Every region carries a Commission — the Service's charge for that posting, from "stock every lake" in Minnesota to "storm-proof the Outer Banks." Complete one and every park you'll ever run gets a permanent demand bump.

Reach Level 15 and 85 Park Rating and you can enshrine your park in the National Registry — a ceremony, not a wipe. The Park Service screen lays out your whole career: legacies, commissions, and a cross-park Stamp Book.

And at the top of the map, one posting sits under glass: Yellowstone — geyser basins, wolf packs and bison jams. Enshrine three parks to earn it.
