I’m not a coach or a dietitian. These are working notes from someone who trains, each tied to the paper it comes from. They’re general information, not medical advice.

    Notes

    Training, in eight findings.

    Mostly meta-analyses. Where the evidence is thin, the card says so.

    Weekly volume

    12–20 sets

    per muscle per week · one suggested range

    Growth rises with weekly sets, with less return from each one you add. Strength flattens sooner. The 12–20 range comes from a small review of trained men; the newest 67-study analysis found diminishing returns but no clear ceiling.

    Pelland 2026 · Schoenfeld 2017a · Baz-Valle 2022
    Effort

    Close to failure

    not necessarily all the way

    Growth tends to improve as sets end nearer to failure, while strength gains look similar across a wide range of reps in reserve. Momentary failure itself showed no clear edge over stopping short.

    Robinson 2024 · Refalo 2023
    Load

    Any load

    for size, if the set is hard

    With sets taken to failure, light (60% of 1RM or less) and heavy loads built similar amounts of muscle. Heavier loads were better for one-rep-max strength.

    Schoenfeld 2017b · Lopez 2021 · Currier 2023
    Frequency

    Your call

    once weekly volume is matched

    With weekly volume equated, how often a muscle was trained made no significant difference to growth. More frequent practice does appear to help strength.

    Schoenfeld 2019 · Pelland 2026
    Rest between sets

    Over 60 s

    little extra past 90 s

    A small growth benefit to resting longer than a minute, and no appreciable difference beyond about 90 seconds. The direct comparisons all overlap zero, so treat it as a nudge.

    Singer 2024
    Range of motion

    Full, by default

    the edge is trivial

    Full range beat partial range by a trivial margin on average, with an interval that includes zero. In a 2025 within-subject trial, lengthened partials and full range grew muscle about equally.

    Wolf 2023 · Wolf 2025
    Deloads

    Unproven

    two small trials so far

    Coaches define a deload as a spell of reduced training stress to shed fatigue. In trials, a full week off mid-block didn’t help growth and slightly hurt strength, and reduced-volume weeks made no difference in untrained men.

    Bell 2023 · Coleman 2024 · Pancar 2026
    The health floor

    150–300 min

    moderate activity a week

    WHO guidance for adults: that, or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity, plus muscle-strengthening work for all major groups on two or more days. For steps, risk of death falls steeply up to roughly 5,000–7,000 a day, then flattens.

    WHO 2020 · Ding 2025
    Notes

    Nutrition, in eight numbers.

    The same ones the calculator above uses.

    Energy per gram

    4 · 4 · 9 · 7

    kcal: protein, carbohydrate, fat, alcohol

    The Atwater general factors that food labels are built on.

    FAO 2003
    Protein

    1.6–2.2 g/kg

    per day, if you lift

    A 49-trial meta-analysis put the point of diminishing returns near 1.6 g/kg a day, with a wide interval (1.0 to 2.2). Later analyses found smaller gains continuing above it. 1.6–2.2 is the range the ISSN gives for people who resistance train.

    Morton 2018 · Tagawa 2021 · Nunes 2022 · ISSN 2026
    Protein, lean and dieting

    1.9–3.2 g/kg

    per day, in a deficit

    For lean, resistance-trained people in a deficit, the ISSN’s 2026 position is that this range can maximise lean-mass retention. An earlier review of six studies put it at 2.3–3.1 g per kg of fat-free mass.

    ISSN 2026 · Helms 2014a
    Rate of loss

    0.5–1% a week

    of body weight

    The rate recommended for keeping muscle while getting lean. In a trial with elite athletes, the slower group (about 0.7% a week) gained lean mass while the faster group (about 1.0%) did not.

    Helms 2014b · Garthe 2011
    Surplus

    360–480 kcal

    a day, as a cautious start

    Nobody has established the best surplus. A narrative review suggests starting at about 1,500–2,000 kJ a day; in one small trial, bigger surpluses mainly added fat.

    Slater 2019 · Helms 2023
    Maintenance estimates

    ± 10%

    is as good as equations get

    Mifflin-St Jeor predicted resting energy within 10% of the measured value for more people than any other equation tested, and still misses for many individuals. Multiply by an activity level of 1.4 to 2.4, then correct against the scale.

    Mifflin 1990 · Frankenfield 2005 · FAO/WHO/UNU 2004
    Creatine and caffeine

    3–5 g

    creatine monohydrate a day

    That dose raises muscle creatine stores over three to four weeks. Caffeine at 3–6 mg per kg consistently improves performance; very high doses mostly add side effects.

    Kreider 2017 · Guest 2021
    Labels and logs

    +20%

    legal slack on a US calorie label

    US rules treat a label as misbranded only when a food’s actual calories exceed the declared value by more than 20%. Food logs can be further off: in one small study, “diet-resistant” adults under-reported intake by 47%.

    21 CFR 101.9(g)(5) · Lichtman 1992
    Claims, checked

    Twenty things people say.

    A verdict, the reason, and the paper. Open one to read it.

    You have to train to failure to grow.Overstated

    Getting close matters: growth tends to improve as sets end nearer to failure. But momentary failure itself showed no clear advantage over stopping short (effect size 0.12, with an interval spanning zero).

    Robinson 2024 · Refalo 2023

    Light weights tone, heavy weights build.Not supported

    When sets are taken to failure, light and heavy loads build similar amounts of muscle. What heavy loads do better is one-rep-max strength.

    Schoenfeld 2017b · Lopez 2021 · Currier 2023

    Every muscle needs training twice a week.Depends

    For size, not on its own: with weekly volume matched, frequency made no significant difference. Higher frequency helped when it also meant more total work, and it does seem to help strength.

    Schoenfeld 2019 · Pelland 2026

    Only the last five reps of a set count.Unproven

    “Effective reps” is a coaching heuristic with no primary study behind it. The soft version, that you need to get reasonably close to failure, fits the evidence. The hard version, a cut-off at five reps, hasn’t been tested, and the data show a gradual relationship with no threshold.

    Nuckols, Stronger By Science · Robinson 2024

    You need a deload every fourth week.Unproven

    Scheduled deloads are standard coaching practice, and a panel of coaches has agreed a definition. The outcome trials are few and small: a week of no training mid-block didn’t improve growth and slightly reduced strength gains; reduced-volume deload weeks made no measurable difference in untrained men.

    Bell 2023 · Coleman 2024 · Pancar 2026

    A calculated 1RM is as good as a tested one.Depends

    Prediction equations track tested maxes well from sets of ten or fewer; one study recommended using no more than ten reps and found a five-rep max predicted best. Another found the common equations under-estimated the deadlift. Good enough for choosing training loads.

    Reynolds 2006 · LeSuer 1997

    Zone 2 is the best cardio intensity for health.Overstated

    Higher cardiorespiratory fitness is strongly associated with lower mortality, with no upper limit of benefit observed. That is a finding about being fit, not about one training zone. A 2025 review concluded that current evidence does not support Zone 2 as the optimal intensity.

    Mandsager 2018 · Lang 2024 · Storoschuk 2025

    You need 10,000 steps a day.Overstated

    A 57-study meta-analysis found the mortality curve bending at around 5,000–7,000 steps a day; 7,000 versus 2,000 was associated with a 47% lower risk of death from any cause. An earlier analysis put the plateau at 6,000–8,000 for adults over 60 and 8,000–10,000 for younger adults. These are observational data. More is fine; 10,000 isn’t a threshold.

    Ding 2025 · Paluch 2022

    Low-carb beats low-fat for fat loss.Not supported

    In a 12-month trial of 609 adults, the healthy low-fat and healthy low-carb groups lost 5.3 kg and 6.0 kg; the difference wasn’t significant. In controlled-feeding studies with calories matched, lower-fat diets came out ahead by about 16 g of body fat a day, which is statistically real and practically nothing.

    Gardner 2018 · Hall 2017 · ISSN 2026

    Intermittent fasting burns more fat than ordinary dieting.Overstated

    With calories matched, the results are small and mixed. A 12-month trial found 8.0 kg versus 6.3 kg lost, not a significant difference. A controlled-feeding trial found none. A 2026 meta-analysis found about 1.5 kg extra. A 16:8 trial with no calorie target found no weight benefit. The ISSN’s position is that fasting variants show no differential effect against continuous restriction.

    Liu 2022 · Maruthur 2024 · Fernandes-Alves 2026 · Lowe 2020 · ISSN 2026

    You must eat protein within an hour of training.Overstated

    A meta-analysis found an apparent timing effect that disappeared once total protein intake was accounted for. Total daily protein was the strongest predictor of results.

    Schoenfeld 2013

    Your body can only use 30 g of protein per meal.Not supported

    In a tracer study, 100 g of protein after exercise produced a larger and longer-lasting anabolic response than 25 g, with little of the extra oxidised. That is an acute study, not a long-term outcome trial. Around 0.4–0.55 g per kg per meal over four meals is a practical way to reach a daily target, not a ceiling.

    Trommelen 2023 · Schoenfeld 2018

    Six small meals a day stoke your metabolism.Not supported

    A meta-analysis found an apparent benefit of eating more often that traced back to a single study; without it, nothing reliable remained. The ISSN’s position is that meal frequency isn’t crucial, though it suggests at least three protein-rich meals for athletes.

    Schoenfeld 2015 · ISSN 2026

    Fasted cardio burns more fat.Not supported

    In a four-week trial of 20 young women on matched diets, fasted and fed cardio produced the same fat loss. It is a small, short study, so read this as unproven, not disproven.

    Schoenfeld 2014

    High-protein diets damage your kidneys.Not supported

    Across 28 trials and 1,358 adults without kidney disease, the change in kidney filtration rate did not differ between higher- and lower-protein diets. This says nothing about people who already have kidney disease; that is a conversation for a doctor.

    Devries 2018

    You can spot-reduce fat.Mostly not

    Six weeks of abdominal training did not reduce abdominal fat. Twelve weeks of single-leg training reduced fat in the arms and trunk but not in the trained leg. A meta-analysis of 13 studies found no local effect. One small 2023 trial did find more trunk-fat loss with abdominal endurance work than with matched running, so the question isn’t fully closed.

    Vispute 2011 · Ramírez-Campillo 2013 · Ramírez-Campillo 2022 · Brobakken 2023

    Diet breaks speed up fat loss.Depends

    One trial in men with obesity found better fat loss from dieting in two-week blocks with two-week breaks. A trial in resistance-trained adults found no difference in fat or lean mass, though hunger was lower with breaks. Pooled across 12 trials: no difference in fat lost, and a smaller drop in resting metabolic rate.

    Byrne 2018 · Peos 2021 · Poon 2025

    I track everything and can’t lose weight, so my metabolism is broken.Usually not

    In a classic study of ten “diet-resistant” adults, measured energy expenditure was within 5% of predicted. They were under-reporting food by 47% and over-reporting activity by 51%. That was a small, specific group. Metabolism does adapt during a diet, but logging error is the first thing to rule out.

    Lichtman 1992 · Trexler 2014

    Sleep doesn’t matter if the calories are right.Not supported

    In a two-week crossover study of ten adults on the same deficit, cutting time in bed from 8.5 to 5.5 hours reduced the share of weight lost as fat by 55% and increased the fat-free mass lost by 60%. Small and lab-based, but the direction is clear.

    Nedeltcheva 2010

    A bigger surplus builds more muscle.Overstated

    In an eight-week trial comparing maintenance, a 5% surplus and a 15% surplus, faster weight gain mostly meant faster fat gain. The trial was small (17 finishers), and the review literature says the optimal surplus is unknown.

    Helms 2023 · Slater 2019
