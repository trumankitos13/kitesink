Resting energy starts from KiteSink’s own working numbers: 10, 9 or 8 kcal per lb of body weight by body fat category, with day levels of × 1.1 to 1.5 and training added in the activity multiplier. The published equations are there to choose instead; the best of them lands within 10% of measured resting energy for most people, not all. Run it for two or three weeks, watch the trend on the scale, then adjust. Mifflin 1990 · Frankenfield 2005 · Cunningham 1991 · FAO/WHO/UNU 2004

        RecoveryCoaching practice

        After an injury, wait a week longer than you think and test one exercise at a time

        McDonald's two rules: when it feels healed, give it another week, then bring back one set of one exercise at a time.

        • Reintroduce one exercise per session for the injured area and check how it feels the next day.
        • See a clinician if pain persists, returns or limits daily movement.

        RecoverySome support

        Extreme pain after a punishing new session can be a medical emergency

        Exertional rhabdomyolysis is rare but can be life-threatening. Dark urine after very hard unaccustomed exercise needs immediate medical care.

        • Introduce new exercises and high-rep work with one or two easy sets.
        • Seek urgent medical care for dark urine, severe swelling or pain far beyond normal soreness.

        Also in the guide: why your old weights are the wrong place to restart, and how little training holds most of your strength when time is short.

              Dehydration cut strength in a meta-analysis, and an IPF weigh-in about two hours before lifting leaves little time to recover, so a first meet is a poor place to try one.

                Treat an FFMI of 25 as a rare ceiling, not as a drug test

                Lyle McDonald's position is that a few genetically unusual lifters pass 25 without drugs, and that for nearly everyone else it marks the practical limit.

                Body compositionSome support

                Reading a high FFMI at high body fat as muscle

                Fat-free mass rises as people get fatter, because extra fat brings water, connective tissue and other non-muscle lean tissue with it. FFMI rises too, without more muscle.

                Body compositionSome support

                Planning a target weight as if every kilo lost will be fat

                The target-weight sum assumes lean mass stays put. Some water, glycogen and lean tissue always go too, so you reach the number less lean than planned.

                The goal weight assumes every kilo lost is fat. How much of a loss is lean depends on how lean you start, as Forbes’s review of feeding studies shows; its people were not lifting, so no second figure is worked out here. FFMI landmarks are from published samples measured in different ways: rough guides, not grades.

                HealthSome support

                Creatine plus resistance training added lean mass and some strength in older adults

                Across 22 trials in adults averaging 57 to 70 years, creatine added 1.37 kg (3.0 lb) of lean mass and small strength gains.

                • Talk to a clinician before use if you have kidney disease or take medication.

                NutritionThin evidence

                Caffeine dose often studied

                ISSN 2010: about 3 to 6 mg per kg works, 9 or more adds nothing; 5 mg/kg is 2.3 mg per lb. Half-life was 7.9 h on low-dose oral contraceptives, 5.4 h without.

                WomenThin evidence

                Caffeine lasts longer in the body on oral contraceptives

                Women on low-dose oral contraceptives cleared caffeine more slowly, with a half-life near 7.9 hours against 5.4 in non-users.

                • If you use the pill and sleep poorly, try moving your last caffeine earlier in the day.
                • Ask a pharmacist how caffeine fits with any medicine you take.

                HealthWell supported

                Vitamin D pills did not prevent fractures, falls or major disease in trials

                Large trials and meta-analyses of vitamin D supplements found no effect on fractures, falls or major disease in general adult groups.

                • Ask a clinician before supplementing, especially if you have a bone or kidney condition.

                NutritionSome support

                Many athletes test low in vitamin D, but the count depends on the cut-off

                At a cut-off of 32 ng/mL, 56 percent of 2,313 athletes were low, more in winter and in indoor sports. The IOM counts 20 ng/mL as enough for nearly everyone.

                • If you train indoors or through dark winters, ask a clinician whether a vitamin D test is worth doing.
                • Do not take high-dose vitamin D without a clinician's advice.

                HealthSome support

                Omega-3 had little effect on death or heart events but lowered triglycerides

                A Cochrane review of 86 trials found little or no effect on mortality or cardiovascular events, with about a 15% drop in triglycerides.

                NutritionThin evidence

                High-dose vitamins C and E blunted some training adaptations in trials

                In two randomised trials, daily vitamin C and E dulled muscle markers of adaptation, though fitness and muscle growth mostly kept pace with placebo.

                • McDonald's practice: get antioxidants from fruit and vegetables, not high-dose pills, while training to improve.

                NutritionWell supported

                Taking antioxidant pills as health insurance

                In 78 randomised trials with 296,707 people, antioxidant pills did not lower deaths, and beta-carotene and vitamin E slightly raised them in the better trials.

                • Check any high-dose vitamin plan with a clinician or dietitian before you start.

                NutritionThin evidence

                Glutamine added no strength or lean mass in a training trial

                In 31 young adults lifting for six weeks, a large daily glutamine dose did no better than placebo for strength, lean mass or muscle breakdown.

                WomenSome support

                Iron deficiency is common in female athletes and is found by a blood test

                A systematic review reports that up to 60 percent of female athletes experience iron deficiency, which lowered endurance performance by 3 to 4 percent.

                • Ask a doctor for a blood test if you have persistent, unexplained fatigue.
                • Do not start iron tablets on a guess.

                WomenSome support

                Melatonin trials in women at a glance

                Controlled evidence in three groups of women, with doses as the studies report them. Not a recommendation. Ask a doctor or pharmacist first.
