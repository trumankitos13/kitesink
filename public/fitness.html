      HypertrophyTraining for muscle size. 34 entries.

      Hypertrophy · VolumeMore weekly sets build more muscle, with diminishing returnsMuscle growth rises with weekly hard sets per muscle, but each added set gives less than the one before it.BeginnerWell supported

      A meta-regression of 67 studies and 2,058 participants (79% men, average age about 25) found that gains in muscle size rose as weekly set volume rose, with diminishing returns. Strength gains levelled off much sooner. The model gave a set that only trains a muscle as a helper half the credit of a direct set.

      • Add sets slowly, and only when progress at your current number has stalled.

      Pelland 2026

      Hypertrophy · Volume12 to 20 weekly sets per muscle is a reasonable range for trained liftersA review of trained young men found 12-20 sets per muscle per week a sensible target. Treat it as a guide, not a cap.IntermediateSome support

      A systematic review of seven trials in men with at least a year of lifting compared under 12, 12-20 and over 20 weekly sets (six trials were pooled). Moderate and high volumes did not differ for the quadriceps or biceps. High volume did better for the triceps. The newer 67-study analysis found diminishing returns but no clear ceiling, so 20 is not a hard limit.

      • If you train a muscle fewer than 12 sets a week and progress is slow, test a small increase.

      Baz-Valle 2022 · Pelland 2026

      Hypertrophy · VolumeAlmost any structured programme beats no training, and multiple sets rank highest for sizeIn a network meta-analysis, every prescription beat no exercise, and multi-set programmes ranked highest for hypertrophy.BeginnerWell supported

      The hypertrophy network pooled 119 trials and 3,364 healthy adults. All 12 prescriptions beat non-exercise controls and promoted size comparably. The highest-ranked for hypertrophy was higher-load, multiple-set, twice-weekly training. Rankings are not proof that the top option is better than the rest.

      • Choose a plan you can follow for months, with more than one set per exercise.

      Currier 2023

      Hypertrophy · VolumeJumping to very high volume straight awayMore sets add less each time, so a big jump from your current volume mostly adds fatigue.MistakeIntermediateThin evidence

      The dose-response analysis shows diminishing returns on weekly sets. A small trial of 16 trained people found a volume built from each person's own logs beat a fixed 22 weekly sets. Coaches therefore add sets in small steps and watch whether performance holds.

      • Add 1-3 sets per muscle per week, then check performance for two weeks.

      Pelland 2026 · Scarpelli 2022

      Hypertrophy · EffortSets ended closer to failure tended to build more muscleIn a meta-regression, hypertrophy improved as sets ended nearer to failure, while strength gains were similar across a wide range.IntermediateWell supported

      The analysis estimated reps in reserve (RIR, the reps left before failure) for each study from its description, so the exact shape of the curve is unclear, as the authors state. Muscle size had a clear negative slope with RIR. For strength, the intervals included zero, meaning no clear relationship.

      • End most hard sets with only a few reps left in reserve.

      Robinson 2024

      Hypertrophy · EffortGoing to absolute failure has not shown a clear edge over stopping just shortTraining to momentary failure was not clearly better for muscle than stopping close to it.IntermediateWell supported

      A meta-analysis of 15 studies found no advantage for momentary failure (effect size 0.12, interval -0.13 to 0.37). In a trial of 18 trained people over 8 weeks, legs trained to failure or to about 1-2 reps in reserve grew the same. A 13-study review found no hypertrophy difference when volume was equal.

      • Use 1-2 reps in reserve on most sets, and save true failure for safe exercises such as machines.

      Refalo 2023 · Refalo 2024 · Vieira 2021

      Hypertrophy · EffortReps in reserve is a useful guide, but most lifters misjudge itRIR tracks how hard a set was, but people tend to underpredict how many reps they have left, by about one rep on average.IntermediateSome support

      In 29 squatters, an RIR-based effort rating moved in step with bar speed. A scoping review and meta-analysis of 12 studies and 414 people found lifters underpredicted reps to failure by 0.95 reps on average, with wide variation. Guesses were better close to the end of a set and on sets of 12 reps or fewer.

      • Rate RIR on sets of about 12 reps or fewer.
      • Occasionally check yourself with one set to failure on a safe exercise.

      Zourdos 2016 · Halperin 2022 · Pelland 2022

      Hypertrophy · EffortThe effective-reps idea is a useful rule of thumb, not a measured lawThe idea that only the last few reps near failure drive growth fits the direction of the data. No fixed cut-off has been shown.IntermediateThin evidence

      Effective (or stimulating) reps is a coaching model. It matches the finding that sets nearer failure tend to build more size. The meta-regression showed a gradual slope with estimated RIR, not a cliff, and its authors note the RIR estimates are rough. The reviews of RIR methods find they differ in accuracy, so a hard rule such as "the last five reps" is not supported.

      • Use the idea to remind yourself to make sets hard, not to count reps.

      Robinson 2024 · Pelland 2022 · Halperin 2022

      Hypertrophy · EffortWhere the effective-reps model stops workingThe model assumes a rep near failure is worth the same at any load and that you can tell which reps those are. Both are shaky.AdvancedThin evidence

      It also leaves out exercise choice, muscle length and fatigue across sets. A trial that took sets to failure with heavier and lighter loads found similar fibre glycogen depletion in both fibre types, which fits with load mattering less at failure. Sets that stopped about 1-2 reps short still grew muscle as well as failure sets, and RIR is hard to judge exactly.

      • Treat the model as a teaching tool and judge your plan by results over months.

      Morton 2019 · Refalo 2024 · Halperin 2022

      Hypertrophy · EffortEnding every set far from failureSets that stop well short of failure leave growth on the table, and self-chosen loads are often light.MistakeBeginnerSome support

      The meta-regression found muscle size improved as sets ended closer to failure. People who choose their own weights average about 53% of 1RM. Sets that stop when the work first feels uncomfortable can sit many reps from failure. Many beginners find it hard to judge how many reps remain.

      • Raise the load or reps until the last rep of each working set looks slow and hard.

      Robinson 2024 · Steele 2022

      Hypertrophy · Load and repsLight and heavy loads build similar muscle when sets are hardMuscle growth was similar with light and heavy loads when sets were taken close to failure.BeginnerWell supported

      A meta-analysis of 45 studies found similar hypertrophy for loads above and at or below 60% of one-rep max (1RM). Another of 28 studies (747 adults), using sets to volitional failure, agreed. In 49 resistance-trained men over 12 weeks, 20-25 reps at about 30-50% 1RM grew muscle as much as 8-12 reps at 75-90%.

      • Pick a load you can control through the full range and take the set close to failure.

      Refalo 2021 · Lopez 2021 · Morton 2016

      Hypertrophy · Load and repsHeavier loads give better strength gainsIf one-rep-max strength matters to you, train with heavier loads at least part of the time.BeginnerWell supported

      The network meta-analysis found loads above 80% of 1RM maximised strength gains. Pooled results of sets to failure showed high-load training beat low-load for strength. A separate analysis found higher loads improved 1RM strength, with an effect size of 0.34. Muscle size did not follow the same pattern.

      • Include some sets in the 5-8 rep range if you also want a stronger one-rep max.

      Currier 2023 · Lopez 2021 · Refalo 2021

      Hypertrophy · Frequency and splitsWith weekly sets matched, training a muscle more often adds little growthOnce total weekly sets are equal, how often you train a muscle makes little difference to size.BeginnerWell supported

      The meta-regression found the effect of frequency on hypertrophy compatible with a negligible effect. In 21 people given the same weekly volume over 9 weeks, two sessions a week and four sessions a week gave the same muscle and strength gains. In 24 men, training one or three times a week gave similar thigh results.

      • Pick the number of weekly sessions you can keep, then spread your sets across them.

      Pelland 2026 · Hamarsland 2022 · Neves 2022

      Hypertrophy · Frequency and splitsFull-body and split routines give similar resultsA meta-analysis found no meaningful difference in strength or muscle size between split and full-body routines.BeginnerWell supported

      Fourteen studies with 392 participants compared the two. Gains in bench press and leg strength, arm and thigh muscle area and lean mass were similar. The review could not say what happens when volume differs, so choose the layout by schedule and preference.

      • Choose the split that fits your week and that you enjoy.

      Ramos-Campo 2024

      Hypertrophy · Exercises and restUse a full range of motion by default; lengthened partials also workFull range of motion is the safe default. Partial reps done in the stretched half held up well in newer trials.BeginnerSome support

      A meta-analysis of 16 studies found full range beat partial range for lower-limb muscle growth (effect size 0.88) and strength. No clear difference appeared in muscle thickness overall. A 12-week trial across 15 sites (163 and 134 people in the two arms) found lengthened-partial and full-range training practically equivalent for arm and thigh size, and an 8-week trial in 13 trained people agreed.

      • Move through the full range you can control, and keep partial reps for the stretched end.

      Pallarés 2021 · Gschneidner 2025 · Havers 2025

      Hypertrophy · Exercises and restLoading a muscle in its stretched position grew more in several trialsExercises that load a muscle when it is long gave bigger gains than versions that load it short, in trials that compared them.IntermediateSome support

      Seated leg curls grew the whole hamstrings more than prone leg curls (+14% against +9%, 20 adults, 12 weeks). Overhead cable triceps extensions beat neutral-arm ones (+19.9% against +13.9%, 21 adults, 12 weeks). In 42 young women, calf raises done in the lower, stretched half grew the medial calf more than full or top-half range.

      • Include one exercise per muscle that loads it stretched, such as seated leg curls or overhead triceps extensions.

      Maeo 2021 · Maeo 2023 · Kassiano 2023

      Hypertrophy · Exercises and restRest long enough to repeat your reps; 2 to 5 minutes gave similar growth in trained menLonger rest keeps reps up across sets. In trained men, resting 2 or 5 minutes gave similar muscle gains.IntermediateSome support

      A review of 35 studies found 3-5 minutes rest allowed more reps over several sets. In 13 strength-trained men, 2 and 5 minute rests gave similar muscle and strength gains over two 3-month blocks. A bench press needed 3-5 minutes to hold reps, a single-joint exercise about 2. In 12 untrained men, 2.5 minutes beat 1 minute for arm size.

      • Rest about 3 minutes between heavy compound sets and about 2 minutes between isolation sets.

      de Salles 2009 · Ahtiainen 2005 · Senna 2016 · Buresh 2009

      Hypertrophy · Exercises and restLift under control; very slow reps are not neededControlled reps work. Reps of 10 seconds or more are best avoided, and slowing the lowering phase had no clear extra effect on size.IntermediateSome support

      An umbrella review of 14 meta-analyses suggested avoiding very slow reps of 10 seconds or more. A meta-analysis of nine studies found uncertain effects of slower lowering on hypertrophy. A review of six studies in 119 untrained people was mixed, and a tempo review notes the evidence is not clear-cut.

      • Lower the weight in about 2 seconds and lift without bouncing.

      Bernárdez-Vázquez 2022 · Amdi 2025 · Hackett 2018 · Wilk 2021

      Hypertrophy · Exercises and restCutting the range of motion to lift heavierShortening the rep to add weight tended to give up size in the lower body. Partials in the stretched half are different.MistakeBeginnerSome support

      A meta-analysis of 16 studies found full range gave larger lower-limb muscle gains than partial range. Lengthened partials, done on purpose in the stretched half with control, matched full range in a large 12-week trial. Half reps that skip the stretched part, done only to move more weight, are the mistake.

      • Lower under control to the stretched position and drive up without bouncing.

      Pallarés 2021 · Gschneidner 2025

      Hypertrophy · Exercises and restCutting rest so short that your reps fallVery short rests lower the reps you can do on later sets, and with it the total work.MistakeBeginnerSome support

      In a trial, a 1-minute rest gave fewer total reps over five sets than 3-5 minutes on a bench press. A review of 35 studies found 3-5 minutes allowed more reps over several sets. Short rests are fine for small isolation moves, but for compound lifts they cost volume.

      • Rest long enough to repeat most of your first set's reps.

      Senna 2016 · de Salles 2009

      Hypertrophy · Special techniquesDrop sets match straight sets for growth and can save timeDrop sets built about the same muscle as normal sets in a pooled analysis, so the case for them is time and variety.IntermediateSome support

      A meta-analysis of six studies (142 adults) found both drop sets and normal sets increased muscle size, with no significant difference between them. A systematic review of advanced techniques also lists drop sets as a time-saving tool but says evidence is too thin for firm dosing guidance.

      • Add one drop set at the end of an isolation exercise when you are short on time.

      Sødal 2023 · Krzysztofik 2019

      Hypertrophy · Special techniquesSupersets let you do the same work in less timeSupersets gave similar volume and similar strength and size results in shorter sessions.BeginnerSome support

      A meta-analysis of 19 studies with 313 people compared supersets with straight sets. Total reps and volume load were similar and sessions were shorter. Supersets felt harder and raised markers of exertion. Pairing opposing muscles kept volume up better than pairing the same muscle.

      • Pair opposing muscles, such as chest and back, when you are short on time.

      Zhang 2025

      Hypertrophy · Special techniquesRest-pause and cluster sets are fatigue and time tools, not growth boostersPooled reviews found cluster sets and similar set structures give about the same size and strength as straight sets.IntermediateSome support

      A review of 17 studies found no set structure beat another for strength or hypertrophy, and a review of 29 studies found cluster sets matched traditional sets, possibly with less fatigue. One trial in 18 trained people found rest-pause grew thigh thickness more but not other sites.

      • Use cluster or rest-pause sets when you want heavy reps with less fatigue, not as a growth hack.

      Prestes 2019 · Jukic 2021 · Davies 2021

      Hypertrophy · Special techniquesBlood-flow restriction can build size with light loads, but needs careLight loads with a cuff that restricts blood flow built muscle similarly to heavy training, but built less strength.AdvancedSome support

      A meta-analysis found similar hypertrophy for high-load training and low-load training with blood-flow restriction (BFR), while strength favoured high load. A position stand sets out methods and safety for practitioners. Cuff width and pressure matter, and people with vascular or clotting conditions should involve a clinician first.

      • Learn cuff width and pressure from a qualified practitioner, and ask a clinician if you have a vascular condition.

      Lixandrão 2018 · Patterson 2019

      Hypertrophy · ProgrammingAdding reps and adding weight both work as progressionIn new lifters, progressing by reps or by load gave the same strength and muscle gains over 10 weeks.BeginnerThin evidence

      Thirty-nine previously untrained people (20 men, 19 women) had one leg progress by load and the other by reps. Leg extension 1RM and thigh muscle area rose by similar amounts in both. This was a short trial in beginners, so it may not transfer to advanced lifters.

      • Add reps within a range first, then raise the load and drop back to the low end of the range.

      Chaves 2024

      Hypertrophy · ProgrammingPut the exercises that matter most firstReps and volume are higher for an exercise placed early in a session, and pre-exhaustion gave no extra benefit.BeginnerSome support

      A review of exercise order found total repetitions were higher when an exercise was done at the start of a session. A 12-week trial in 39 trained people found pre-exhaustion (isolation before compound) gave no greater benefit than putting compound exercises first.

      • Start with the lifts and muscles you most want to improve.

      Simão 2012 · Fisher 2014

      Hypertrophy · ProgrammingWarm up with lighter sets of the first lift; save long stretches for laterWarming up helps performance. Long static stretches right before lifting can reduce it slightly.BeginnerSome support

      A meta-analysis of 32 studies found warm-ups improved performance in 79% of measured outcomes. A review found static stretches of 60 seconds or more per muscle lowered performance by 4.6%, against 1.1% for shorter holds, when tested right afterwards. A squat trial found extra heavy or fast warm-up sets gave no clear gain in total reps or volume.

      • Do two or three lighter, increasing sets of your first lift before working sets.
      • Hold long stretches for after training.

      Fradkin 2010 · Behm 2016 · Souza 2024

      Hypertrophy · ProgrammingDeloads are common practice, but trials do not yet show they are neededCoaches use deloads regularly. The one small trial in beginners found cutting volume in weeks 4 and 8 neither helped nor hurt.IntermediateThin evidence

      A deload is a period of reduced training stress, defined by consensus among 21 coaches who finished a Delphi study. In 246 strength and physique athletes, deloads came about every 5.6 weeks and lasted about 6.4 days. In 19 untrained men, deload weeks did not change muscle growth over 8 weeks. Experienced lifters were not tested.

      • Take a lighter week when performance drops for several sessions or joints ache, rather than on a fixed date.

      Bell 2023 · Rogerson 2024 · Pancar 2026

      Hypertrophy · ProgrammingPeriodising your training helps strength more than muscle sizeWhen volume was equal, planned changes in volume and load helped strength but not muscle size.IntermediateWell supported

      A meta-analysis of 35 studies found periodised training had a small advantage for 1RM strength over non-periodised training. Hypertrophy did not differ between periodised and non-periodised, or between linear and undulating, plans. Undulating beat linear plans for 1RM only in trained people.

      • Vary rep ranges across the week if it keeps training interesting, but do not expect it to change size.

      Moesgaard 2022

      Hypertrophy · ProgrammingMuscle size gains in the first weeks partly reflect swellingMeasured size rises in the first weeks, but some of that early gain is swelling from training stress, not new muscle tissue.BeginnerSome support

      In 25 sedentary men, thigh muscle area rose 9.6% over 8 weeks, and the authors judged real hypertrophy likely appeared around weeks 3-4. Other researchers found early increases came with swelling markers, and a review argues true hypertrophy appears after roughly 18 sessions. The timing is debated.

      • Judge progress over months, not the first three weeks.

      DeFreitas 2011 · Damas 2016 · Damas 2018

      Hypertrophy · ProgrammingHow to read a training study before you act on itCheck who was studied, for how long, how muscle was measured and how failure was defined before copying a result.IntermediateCoaching practice

      Most volume and frequency data come from short trials in young people: in the 67-study set, the average age was about 25 and 79% were men. Studies also define failure differently and often do not report reps in reserve. A single trial is weak evidence next to a meta-analysis, and an interval that includes zero means no clear difference.

      • Ask who was studied and for how many weeks before applying a finding.

      Pelland 2026 · Pelland 2022

      Hypertrophy · ProgrammingRepeating the same weights and reps with no logWithout a record, you cannot tell whether you are adding reps or load, which is what progression means.MistakeBeginnerCoaching practice

      In 39 beginners, adding load and adding reps each raised strength and thigh muscle area over 10 weeks. Both methods need you to know last week's numbers. Writing down weight, reps and effort for each set takes little time and makes stalls easy to spot.

      • Log weight, reps and reps in reserve for every working set.

      Chaves 2024

      Hypertrophy · RecoveryShort sleep lowers lifting performanceSleep loss lowered exercise performance in a large meta-analysis, and one small study found lower muscle protein synthesis after a sleepless night.BeginnerSome support

      A meta-analysis of 69 publications defined sleep loss as 6 hours or less in 24 and found an average 7.56% drop in performance. A review of 17 studies found restricted sleep reduced force on compound lifts. In 13 adults, one night without sleep cut muscle protein synthesis by 18%. Long-term growth effects were not tested.

      • Protect regular, full nights of sleep, especially before heavy sessions.

      Craven 2022 · Knowles 2018 · Lamon 2021

      Hypertrophy · RecoveryJudging a session by how sore you areSoreness does not show that a session worked, and its absence does not show that it failed.MistakeBeginnerSome support

      A review found muscle damage is not what drives hypertrophy, and protocols causing little damage still grow muscle. Soreness is largest early on and fades as you adapt. Chasing it can lead to piling on volume or exercises you cannot recover from.

      • Judge sessions by reps, load and effort recorded in a log.

      Damas 2018

      PowerliftingA bigger squat, bench press and deadlift. 28 entries.

      Powerlifting · Percentages and zonesHeavy loads build strength better than light loadsFor strength, heavy and moderate loads beat light loads, so most working sets should sit in the heavy to moderate range.BeginnerWell supported

      A network meta-analysis of 28 studies in 747 healthy adults, all taken to failure, found muscle growth was similar across loads. Strength gains were larger with high loads (8 repetitions or fewer to failure) and moderate loads (9 to 15) than with low loads (over 15). High versus moderate was not clearly different. Light work still has a place for skill and recovery.

      • Keep most working sets on the competition lifts in the 1-8 repetition range.
      • Use light sets for warm-ups, skill work and easy days.

      Lopez 2021

      Powerlifting · Percentages and zonesTesting a true max every few weeksFrequent max attempts add fatigue and risk without adding much information.MistakeIntermediateCoaching practice

      This is coaching convention. A true max is the heaviest single you can complete with legal technique. It is a hard, slow-to-recover effort, and a bad day gives a misleading number. Work from a training max set from recent RPE 8 to 9 sets, and keep true maxes for meets or planned tests every few months.

      • Test at a meet or a planned test day, not on impulse.
      Powerlifting · Percentages and zonesRunning percentages off an old or lucky maxA percentage is only as good as the max behind it, so reset the number when strength changes.MistakeBeginnerCoaching practice

      This is coaching convention. If the max is months old, the percentages are too light or too heavy, and a lucky one-off single makes the plan too hard to finish. Reps at a given percentage also vary between lifters, so use RPE alongside the plan and rebase when weights feel clearly off.

      • Rebase percentages after every cycle using your best recent set, not one lucky rep.
      Powerlifting · RPE and velocityRPE with reps in reserve lets you pick the load by feelA set rated RPE 9 left one repetition in reserve and RPE 10 left none; the scale worked on all three lifts in powerlifters.IntermediateSome support

      The scale pairs effort with repetitions in reserve (RIR): RPE 10 is 0 RIR and RPE 9 is 1 RIR. In 29 lifters, experienced squatters rated a 1RM higher than novices, and bar speed fell as RPE rose. In 15 powerlifters, RPE at 1RM was 9.6 to 9.7 on all three lifts, and RPE tracked percent of 1RM closely (r = 0.88 to 0.91).

      • Rate each top set right after it ends, before looking at the bar speed or the numbers.

      Zourdos 2016 · Helms 2017

      Powerlifting · RPE and velocityLoading by RPE or bar speed works about as well as percentagesTrials and a meta-analysis found no clear strength difference between autoregulated loading and fixed-percentage loading.IntermediateSome support

      A meta-analysis found no significant 1RM difference between autoregulated and standard percentage loading in resistance-trained people (mean difference 2.07 kg, 95% CI -0.32 to 4.46). In an 8-week trial of 21 trained men, RPE-based and percent-based loading both raised squat and bench press 1RM, and the gap between them was not significant. Both are valid ways to set load.

      • Use RPE when daily readiness swings; use percentages when you want a fixed plan to compare weeks.

      Hickmott 2022 · Helms 2018 · Larsen 2021

      Powerlifting · RPE and velocityVelocity loss limits did not change strength gains in a large reviewEnding a set when bar speed has dropped by a set percentage is a valid way to cap fatigue; the threshold changed strength little.AdvancedSome support

      A meta-analysis of 19 longitudinal studies found the velocity loss chosen within sets did not change strength gains, while higher velocity loss went with more muscle growth. A second meta-analysis found thresholds of 25% or less gave slightly more 1RM strength (2.32 kg, 95% CI 0.33 to 4.31). Bar speed at 1RM differs by lift (0.23, 0.10 and 0.14 m/s for squat, bench and deadlift in 15 powerlifters), so each lift needs its own profile.

      • Record bar speed on your own lifts for several weeks before using a velocity cut-off.

      Jukic 2023 · Hickmott 2022 · Helms 2017

      Powerlifting · RPE and velocityStrength does not need sets taken to failureStopping sets short of failure gave similar strength gains to failure training, so heavy work can end with repetitions in reserve.IntermediateWell supported

      Is there one best diet for weight loss?Not supported

      Does eating late at night make you gain fat?Overstated

      Can you out-train a bad diet?Overstated

      In pooled trials, exercise alone produced modest weight loss, so it is a weak tool for offsetting a poor diet.

      Glossary

      54 terms, in plain words.

      Accessory exercise
      An exercise outside the three competition lifts, chosen to build a muscle or fix a weak point. Powerlifting
      Adaptive thermogenesis (metabolic adaptation)
      A fall in energy use beyond what the loss of body mass explains, seen during and after weight loss. Studies find it ranges from small to modest. Diet
      ApoB (apolipoprotein B)
      A protein carried on each cholesterol-bearing particle that can clog arteries. The blood level reflects the number of these particles and can differ from LDL cholesterol. Health
      Atwater factors
      Standard energy values per gram that food labels use: 4 kcal for protein and carbohydrate, 9 for fat, 7 for alcohol. They are averages, not measurements of your food. Nutrition
      Bomb out
      To miss all three attempts on a lift; in a full-power meet that usually means no total. Meet day
      Chief referee
      The referee who gives the commands on the platform; two side referees also judge each lift. Meet day
      Classic (raw)
      A division with a non-supportive singlet and no supportive suits, shirts or knee wraps; belt, knee sleeves and wrist wraps are allowed within limits. Meet day
      Daily undulating periodisation (DUP)
      A plan that varies rep ranges and loads between sessions within the week, such as heavy, medium and light days. Powerlifting
      Deload
      A period of reduced training stress meant to lower fatigue and prepare you for later training, as defined by a coach consensus. Hypertrophy
      Deload
      A planned lighter week or block, with less volume or load, to clear fatigue between harder blocks. Powerlifting
      Diet break and refeed
      A diet break is a planned stretch, often one to two weeks, at maintenance calories. A refeed is shorter, usually one or two days of higher carbohydrate. Diet
      Dynapenia
      The age-related loss of muscle strength. In later life it runs faster than the loss of muscle size, so strength and mass are tracked separately. Health
      Effect size and confidence interval
      Effect size is how big a difference is, in standard deviations. The interval shows a plausible range. If it spans zero, there is no clear difference. Hypertrophy
      Energy deficit
      Eating less energy than you use. It is the one requirement for losing body fat. A daily deficit of 500 kcal is about 3,500 kcal a week. Diet
      Ergogenic aid
      Something taken to improve exercise performance or training results. Creatine and caffeine are the best-supported examples. Nutrition
      Flight (group)
      A group of lifters who take their attempts together; larger sessions split into flights, with lower previous totals lifting first in IPF rules. Meet day
      Hard set
      A working set taken close to failure, usually within a few reps. Warm-up sets and easy sets do not count towards weekly volume. Hypertrophy
      Hazard ratio (HR)
      A figure comparing risk between groups over time. 1.0 means no difference, 0.80 means 20% lower risk and 1.20 means 20% higher. It shows association, not cause. Health
      HbA1c (glycated haemoglobin)
      A blood test that shows average blood sugar over about the past 2 to 3 months. It is given as a percentage, and published ranges separate normal, prediabetes and diabetes. Health
      Kilojoule (kJ) and kilocalorie (kcal)
      Two units of food energy. One kcal, the calorie on most labels, equals 4.184 kJ. Many countries print kJ. Nutrition
      Lactate threshold
      The effort above which lactate starts to build up in the blood faster than it clears. The first threshold marks the end of easy effort and varies between people. Health
      Lengthened-position training
      Loading a muscle while it is stretched, as at the bottom of a curl or squat. Several trials found it grew more than loading it short. Hypertrophy
      Leucine
      An essential amino acid that acts as a trigger for muscle protein synthesis. Animal proteins contain more than most plant proteins, per gram of protein. Nutrition
      Lot number
      A number drawn at weigh-in; if two lifters choose the same weight, the lower lot number lifts first. Meet day
      Macronutrients (macros)
      The three nutrients that provide most energy: protein, carbohydrate and fat. Alcohol is sometimes counted as a fourth source of energy. Nutrition
      Maintenance calories
      The daily energy intake at which your body weight stays steady over weeks. Also called total daily energy expenditure. Nutrition
      MET (metabolic equivalent)
      A unit of effort. 1 MET is resting energy use, and brisk walking is about 3 to 5 METs. Fitness is often reported as the peak METs reached on a treadmill test. Health
      Momentary muscular failure
      The point where you cannot finish another rep despite maximal effort. Volitional failure means you stop by choice. Hypertrophy
      NEAT (non-exercise activity)
      Non-exercise activity thermogenesis: energy spent on walking, standing, fidgeting and chores. It can fall during a diet without you noticing. Diet
      One-rep max (1RM)
      The heaviest load you can lift for one full rep. Training loads are often given as a percent of it, such as 70% of 1RM. Hypertrophy
      One-repetition maximum (1RM)
      The heaviest weight you can lift once with legal technique; the base for percentage programming. Powerlifting
      Opener (first attempt)
      The first of your three attempts on a lift; chosen to be easy enough that you lift it with room to spare. Meet day
      Periodisation
      Planned changes in volume, load or exercises over weeks and months, such as linear steps or weekly undulation. Hypertrophy
      Progressive overload
      Gradually asking more of the muscle over time, by adding reps, load or sets, so it keeps having a reason to adapt. Hypertrophy
      Rack height sheet
      The form where you record squat and bench rack and foot-block settings at weigh-in, so loaders set them before your attempt. Meet day
      Rate of perceived exertion (RPE)
      A 1-10 effort scale. On the RIR-based version, RPE 10 means 0 RIR, RPE 9 means 1 RIR, and so on. Hypertrophy
      Rating of perceived exertion (RPE)
      A 1 to 10 rating of how hard a set was; in strength training it is tied to how many repetitions were left. Powerlifting
      Recomposition
      Gaining muscle and losing fat at the same time, so body weight changes little while the body changes shape. Diet
      Repetitions in reserve (RIR)
      The number of reps you could still have done when the set ended. 0 RIR means failure. 2 RIR means two more reps were possible. Hypertrophy
      Repetitions in reserve (RIR)
      How many more clean repetitions you could have done when the set ended; 2 RIR means two more were possible. Powerlifting
      Resting metabolic rate (RMR)
      The energy your body uses at rest to keep you alive, usually 60 to 70 percent of a day for inactive people, estimated by equations or measured. Nutrition
      Sarcopenia
      A condition of low muscle strength with low muscle quantity or quality, found mostly in older adults. A clinician diagnoses it using strength tests and a body measure. Health
      Set point and settling point
      Two models of body weight: a defended set point, or a settling point where weight drifts with food and activity. A review finds both incomplete. Diet
      Spotter/loaders
      Helpers who load the bar and spot squat and bench; two to five are on the platform at a time in IPF rules. Meet day
      Sticking point
      The position in a lift where the bar slows most or where failures happen. Powerlifting
      Taper (peaking)
      A planned drop in training stress, mostly volume, in the last days or weeks before a meet so fatigue clears and strength shows. Powerlifting
      Thermic effect of food (TEF)
      The energy used to digest, absorb and store food, about 5 to 15 percent of daily energy expenditure on a mixed diet, and higher with protein and alcohol. Nutrition
      Time-restricted eating
      Eating all meals within a set daily window, such as eight hours, and fasting for the rest. It is one form of intermittent fasting. Diet
      Velocity-based training (VBT)
      Setting load or stopping sets from measured bar speed, using a device that reports mean concentric velocity in metres per second. Powerlifting
      VO2 max
      The highest rate at which your body can use oxygen during hard exercise, in mL of oxygen per kg of body weight per minute. It is the standard lab measure of aerobic fitness. Health
      Volume
      The amount of work. In this page, usually the number of hard sets per muscle per week. Volume load is sets times reps times weight. Hypertrophy
      Waist-to-height ratio
      Waist circumference divided by height, both in the same units. A ratio of 0.5 means the waist is half your height. Health
      Working max (training max)
      A weight you can repeat on a normal day, often set below a true max, and used as the base for training percentages. Powerlifting
      Zone 2
      Easy aerobic effort just below the first lactate or ventilatory threshold. You breathe harder but can still speak in sentences. Zone numbers vary between systems. Health
