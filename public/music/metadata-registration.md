# Metadata and registration: KiteSink music field guide

ISWC, ISRC, IPI and the registrations that route the money. Each entry gives the point, the reasoning, what to do, who it is for, where it applies, how well sourced it is, and its sources.
Grades: Sourced (law or official) and Directional (reported or industry practice).

## Guidance

### An ISRC is 12 characters: a prefix, a year and a five-digit number

**Two letters and three letters or digits make the prefix, two digits give the year, and five digits finish it. IFPI describes no check digit.**

The prefix belongs to the registrant and is allocated by an ISRC agency. The year element takes the final two digits of the assignment year. The five-digit designation code is picked by the registrant from its range and must not repeat within a year. On paper the code takes the letters ISRC in front and hyphens at three points, which are not part of the code. IFPI's validity test is only whether the string fits this layout.

- Store the 12 characters with no spaces or hyphens.
- Check the format at /music/tools#identifiers.

ISRC · Beginner · Sourced (official) · For: artists, producers, publishers and labels, administrators · Where: International · Sources: [IISRCRAIFPI n.d.-b](https://isrc.ifpi.org/en/isrc-standard/isrc-structure), [IISRCRAIFPI n.d.-c](https://isrc.ifpi.org/images/downloads/ISRC_Handbook.pdf)

### The recording owner assigns the ISRC, directly or through a manager

**Normally it is the original owner who assigns a recording's ISRC. Membership of a trade body is not needed, and a distributor can assign on the owner's behalf.**

IFPI says independent artists, labels and groups can all assign ISRCs, that only the owner may assign to a recording, and that an appointed ISRC manager can do it on the owner's instruction. Most distributors can supply ISRCs or deliver the owner's own. Prefixes come from the agency for your territory; in the US that is the US ISRC Agency at usisrc.org.

- Ask your distributor which ISRCs it assigned, and keep a copy.

ISRC · Beginner · Sourced (official) · For: artists, producers, publishers and labels, administrators · Where: International, US · Sources: [IISRCRAIFPI n.d.-f](https://isrc.ifpi.org/en/why-use-isrc/using-isrc), [IISRCRAIFPI n.d.-a](https://isrc.ifpi.org/en/faqs), [US ISRC Agency (RIAA) n.d.](https://www.usisrc.org/)

### Check whether a recording already has an ISRC before you assign one

**IFPI's International ISRC Database and SoundExchange's ISRC Search are both free to search. Neither is guaranteed to list every code.**

IFPI says its database is powered by repertoire data that rights owners submit to SoundExchange, is updated daily, and matches more loosely than its paid assignment service. A track can be missing because its owner never submitted data or a distributor did not enter it. Codes from IFPI's own online assignment appear after around 10 days.

- Search by artist and title, not only by code.
- Ask a former distributor or label for your ISRCs before you move.

### Seven facts to keep on file for every ISRC

**IFPI says anyone assigning ISRCs must keep the code, main artist, title, version title, duration, content type and first publication date.**

The registrant must give this reference data to IFPI or its designee on request. Content type tells a sound recording from a music video. The first publication date, known as the P-date, ties the code to a specific recording. The version title carries details such as live or extended mix, which is where a remix is told apart from the original.

- Keep these seven fields for every track in one sheet.

### An ISWC is T, nine digits and a check digit, under ISO 15707:2022

**The code names a song as a creation, not a recording. It shows no shares and does not confer copyright.**

CISAC says the ISWC is a letter T, nine digits for the work and a numeric check digit, written after the letters ISWC, with hyphens or dots allowed for readability. The standard is ISO 15707:2022. The code is permanent: it does not change when a writer moves society or the work enters the public domain. The checker here tests the digit against a published formula, so a pass means consistent, not that the code exists.

- Test an ISWC at /music/tools#identifiers before you reuse it.

ISWC · Intermediate · Sourced (official) · For: songwriters, artists, producers, publishers and labels, administrators · Where: International · As of 20 November 2026 · Sources: [CISACISWCIA n.d.-f](https://www.iswc.org/iswc), [CISACISWCIA n.d.-c](https://www.iswc.org/cmos), [CISACISWCIA n.d.-e](https://www.iswc.org/iswc-usage)

### Only a registration agency can allocate an ISWC, not the writer or publisher

**ISWCs come from the society or agency a work is registered with. For new works, registering the work is the step that gets the number.**

CISAC says creators and publishers may not allocate their own ISWCs, and that a number is assigned as part of registration once all creators are uniquely identified. A publisher can ask its society for access to CISAC's ISWC Allocation Service to get codes before registering. Since a 2020 modernisation ISWCs are assigned centrally, and CISAC says codes can be ready within hours. A society's own numbering system is not replaced, so you will see both.

- Register the work with your society, then read the ISWC from its catalogue.

ISWC · Beginner · Sourced (official) · For: songwriters, artists, producers, publishers and labels, administrators · Where: International · Sources: [CISACISWCIA n.d.-a](https://www.iswc.org/get-iswc), [CISACISWCIA n.d.-f](https://www.iswc.org/iswc), [CISACISWCIA n.d.-e](https://www.iswc.org/iswc-usage), [CISACISWCIA n.d.-d](https://www.iswc.org/creators-publishers)

### A work can end up with two ISWCs, and the preferred one is the one to use

**CISAC says complex registrations have occasionally produced more than one ISWC for a work, and its data marks the preferred one.**

CISAC's open data lists each Preferred ISWC and any archived code merged into it, and asks users to use the preferred one. Since the 2020 changes, duplicates can be merged, or separated again if joined in error. If two codes appear for one song in your files, ask the society which is preferred rather than choosing one.

- Refresh stored ISWCs against the society's catalogue each year.

### IPI numbers identify writers and publishers across the societies' systems

**The IPI system gives each interested party a base number and each name they use a name number. Societies issue them to members.**

CISAC says the IPI system identifies a person or company with an interest in a work across roles such as composer, arranger and publisher, and that SUISA administers it under CISAC and BIEM rules. The MLC says the base number is kept private and a name number stands for a public name, such as a stage name. SOCAN says it assigns IPIs to its members and that CAE number is the older term.

### ISNI names a public identity, and anyone can apply through an agency

**An ISNI identifies a public identity, such as an artist name or a band. CISAC calls it voluntary, and isni.org says it is not free.**

isni.org says an ISNI identifies public identities of people, organisations and groups, has 16 characters ending in a check character that can be X, and is issued only through registration agencies, which charge fees. CISAC says pseudonyms get separate ISNIs, linked in the database, and that ISNI can act as a bridge to codes like the IPI. The checker here tests the check character.

### CWR is the bulk file format publishers use to register works with societies

**Common Works Registration (CWR), a CISAC file format, carries many work registrations at once. The MLC aims it at hundreds or thousands of works.**

CISAC oversees CWR, and a sender has to be set up with CISAC before using it. Publishers and administrators send registrations in it and get status back from the society. The MLC says it may not suit you without thousands of works; a writer with a few songs uses a portal. Its user guide, dated May 2024 and still the one its help page linked on 20 November 2026, takes versions 2.1 and 2.2 and lists 3.1 as pending. SOCAN says a sub-publisher abroad registers your songs there, by CWR or other means. CISAC's layouts and code lists are not reproduced here.

- Ask your society or administrator which CWR version it takes.

### ERN delivers the release; DSR reports what it earned

**ERN goes from a label or distributor to the store with the release and its terms. DSR comes back from the store with sales and usage.**

DDEX says ERN tells a digital service about releases and resources, such as recordings and videos, and the terms on which they can be offered. DSR is the report the service sends back so the rights owner can invoice for rights used. As of 20 November 2026 DDEX names ERN 4.3.2 as the latest published version and urges 4.3.x. DSR is a flat-file standard with profiles for different business models. The AI page covers the ERN flags for generative AI.

- Ask your distributor which ERN version it sends.

DDEX · Beginner · Sourced (official) · For: artists, publishers and labels, administrators · Where: International · As of 20 November 2026 · Sources: [DDEX n.d.-c](https://kb.ddex.net/implementing-each-standard/electronic-release-notification-message-suite-(ern)/), [DDEX n.d.-b](https://kb.ddex.net/implementing-each-standard/digital-sales-reporting-message-suite-(dsr)/), [DDEX n.d.-a](https://kb.ddex.net/about-ddex-standards/ddex-standards/)

### DDEX messages are checked against a schema, and invalid ones should be rejected

**DDEX offers a free online validator. Recipients should reject invalid messages, and senders should validate before sending.**

DDEX lists grounds for rejection: failing the XML schema, breaking the profile rules, breaking extra rules the two sides agreed, plain inconsistencies such as different display artists at release and track level for a single, and blank tags in fields that must be filled. Recipients should not guess missing data. DDEX says its validator does not store uploaded messages.

- Run a validation before every delivery.

### What a US copyright registration adds, as Circular 1 states it

**Registration adds a public record, a route to court for US works and, if made in time, stronger remedies. Protection itself does not depend on it.**

Circular 1 lists the advantages: a public record of the authorship and ownership facts; registration, or refusal, before a suit over a US work; prima facie evidence of validity when it comes before publication or within the first five years after it; and eligibility for statutory damages and attorneys' fees when it precedes infringement or comes within three months of publication. Circular 56 says registration is not required for protection. When to register, or sue, is a question for a copyright lawyer.

### Group options let one fee cover several songs, several recordings or an album

**Up to ten unpublished songs, or ten unpublished recordings, can go on one application if the same authors are all named as claimants.**

Circulars 50 and 56 describe group registration of unpublished works by the same authors. For songs, the Office says naming a performing group alone is not enough: every individual who contributed authorship must be named. Circular 56 also covers registering an album as a collective work, and a unit of publication for recordings packaged with artwork. Circular 34 has the rules.

### Each co-writer has to be registered with their own society too

**Registering a song with one society does not register your co-writers elsewhere. Each registers it with their own society.**

GEMA says each creator must register a work with their own society, so a SACEM member registers with SACEM, and asks you to enter co-creators' IP name numbers and pass on the ISWC. SOCAN says that if your co-writer is not a member, you can register the song with the shares and be paid your portion, but that co-writer is not paid until joining SOCAN or a society it works with abroad.

- Share the ISWC and splits with every co-writer.

Society registration · Intermediate · Sourced (official) · For: songwriters, publishers and labels, administrators · Where: Germany, Canada, International · Sources: [GEMA n.d.-d](https://www.gema.de/en/w/help/creators/works-repertoire/submit-a-notification-for-your-works/register-works-creators-various-countries), [SOCAN n.d.-a](https://www.socan.com/frequently-asked-questions/)

### GEMA: shares add to 100 percent per role, and late registrations are not paid

**GEMA's portal wants shares per role that total 100, and a work registered after the deadline is left out of the distribution.**

GEMA says performing and mechanical shares, if entered, must add to 100 percent per role, and are mandatory only in the publisher section. Blank creator shares are split evenly by role. For works used in a calendar year, the cut-off falls on 31 January in the year after use, with other deadlines for some categories. A late work is not counted and gives no right to claim, which GEMA ties to its distribution plan. GEMA says the ISWC follows once the work reaches registration status 1.

- Register new works as soon as the split is agreed.

### A society's own work number is not the ISWC

**SOCAN gives its own Work Number, and a temporary code is replaced by the international ISWC a few weeks after registration.**

SOCAN says a new work first gets a temporary code, replaced by the ISWC a few weeks later and visible in the member portal catalogue. The Work Number is SOCAN-specific and appears on statements and in its public repertoire search. In Canada, SOCAN says, an ISRC for a recording can be requested from SOPROQ.

- Give the ISWC, not a society's work number, to people outside that society.

### Style guide: credit featured artists in the artist field, spelled the same everywhere

**The Music Biz style guide, version 2.1, advises putting featured artists in the artist role rather than the title, with one spelling per name.**

Music Biz's Music Metadata Style Guide, version 2.1 of September 2015, advises crediting featured artists at the artist role level unless a contract says otherwise, using only 'feat.' and 'with' where a title shows them, one spelling of each artist and label name across deliveries, and a copyright line of first publication year and holder. It is voluntary guidance, and each store applies its own rules.

## In order

### Register a song with the Copyright Office: the application points to get right

**Choose Work of the Performing Arts, give the title as written, state who wrote what, and name any pre-existing material.**

Circular 50 says to give the title exactly as it is printed on the work, say whether it is published, and give the year of completion. The author is whoever wrote the music and lyrics, or the employer where the song was made for hire. Describe authorship as lyrics, music or arrangement, and list pre-existing material in the limitation of claim. An application needs a form, a nonrefundable fee and a deposit copy.

- Check current fees on copyright.gov before you file.

At release · US copyright registration · Intermediate · Sourced (official) · For: songwriters, publishers and labels · Where: US · As of 20 November 2026 · Sources: [U.S. Copyright Office n.d.-d](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ50.pdf)

### Register a recording: choose Sound Recording and send the right deposit

**Choose Sound Recording and name its authors. Upload the file if it is unpublished or digital-only; send the CD or LP if it came out on one.**

Circular 56 says an unpublished or digital-only recording should be uploaded as a digital file, up to 500 MB each, with no physical copy as well. A recording published on CD or LP is deposited in that physical format. One published in the US may need a pair of best-edition copies, with CD ranked first. A derivative recording, such as a mashup, must name its pre-existing material in the limitation of claim. The basics page covers who counts as an author.

- Confirm who the authors are before you file.

## Common mistakes

### Mistake: putting an ISWC in an ISRC field, or the reverse

**An ISRC names one recording and an ISWC names the song. Each code answers a different question, so the wrong one breaks the link.**

IFPI says the ISRC is not used for compositions or lyrics. CISAC says the ISWC does not identify recordings or sheet music. A song has one ISWC, but it can have many recordings, such as a studio take and a remix, each with its own ISRC. A mix-up leaves a recording unattached to its song, or the reverse.

- Copy each code from the register that issued it.
- Test a code at /music/tools#identifiers before you submit it.

Identifiers · Beginner · Sourced (official) · For: songwriters, artists, producers, publishers and labels, administrators · Where: International · Sources: [IISRCRAIFPI n.d.-g](https://isrc.ifpi.org/en/why-use-isrc/when-not-to-assign), [CISACISWCIA n.d.-f](https://www.iswc.org/iswc)

### Mistake: reading the ISRC's year as the year the track was recorded

**The two digits show when the code was assigned. IFPI says the recording year belongs in the metadata, not in the code.**

IFPI says the year of reference is normally, but not always, the year the final master was prepared, and it can differ from the year of recording. It exists so that codes from earlier years cannot be assigned twice. A recording released without an ISRC can be given one later, and the year is then the year of assignment.

- Keep the recording year in its own field.
- Never edit an ISRC to match a date.

### Mistake: registering a work with a writer who has no IPI, then waiting for an ISWC

**An ISWC needs every creator identified. A co-writer outside any society can leave the work without a code at first.**

CISAC says registration needs complete metadata, including the IPI name number and role of every creator, and that a code is allocated only when all creators have been uniquely identified. GEMA gives the same reason when a work lacks an ISWC: a contributor who is not in a society, so not in the IPI database, means no ISWC initially.

- Collect each co-writer's society and IPI number at the writing session.

### Mistake: giving a base number where a form asks for an IPI name number

**People often say IPI for either number. Check which one a form wants, because the base number is the private one.**

The MLC notes that people usually mean the base number when they say IPI, yet the public number is the name number. GEMA asks for each co-creator's IP name number when you register a work, and says you can look one up in a creator search. The split-sheet generator on this site asks for the name number and flags a base number.

- Ask your society for the name number of each name you write under.

### Mistake: sending a CWR registration that omits owners or does not reach 100 percent

**The MLC wants every party on a work listed, ownership that totals 100 percent, and no unknown flag on a party you control.**

The MLC's CWR guide (May 2024) asks for ownership records for every party, controlled or not. For each right, ownership shares must total 100 percent, give or take 0.06 points, or zero, and collection shares may not pass 100 percent. Unknown writer or publisher markers are accepted for parties you do not control. A controlled party marked unknown is rejected, because those records form your claim, and a file whose name was used before is rejected too.

- Check totals and party flags before you send a file.

### Mistake: entering a writer or publisher name that does not match the IPI record

**Registers match people on name and number. Use the spelling your society holds, with the IPI number where a form asks for it.**

The MLC's CWR guide says a publisher sending files must give its name as the IPI database lists it for its IPI name number. In the MLC portal you can search writers by name or IPI number, and it shows existing writers that match what you enter, so you avoid duplicates. GEMA says to look a co-creator up in a creator search, and to enter the creator manually if identical names leave you unsure. The MLC says accurate matching data is what gets you paid correctly and on time.

- Copy names from your society's record, not from memory.

## When a recording needs a new ISRC and when it keeps its code

A creative change to the recording needs a new ISRC; a new format, release or owner does not. Rows follow IFPI's 2021 Handbook, which qualifies the FAQ's 10-second rule.

| What happened | New ISRC | Why, per the ISRC Handbook (2021) |
|---|---|---|
| A remix or different mix | Yes | The musical content is different |
| A live recording of a studio song | Yes | It is a completely different recording |
| A different studio take | Yes | Takes are different recordings, even with the same players |
| An edit that mutes or replaces words | Yes | An edited version is a new recording |
| A cover by other artists | Yes | It is a new recording of the work |
| A music video with the same audio | Yes | A video differs from the audio recording |
| Verses or choruses added or cut | Yes | The edit changes the creative content |
| Length changed only by speed or fade-out | Only if 10 seconds or more | Adding or cutting only silence, ambience or applause never needs one |
| Same recording in another format or bit rate | No | The code names the recording, not the encoding |
| Same recording on a compilation or reissue | No | Releases are told apart by product codes |
| Recording sold or licensed to a new owner | No | The ISRC stays with the recording |
| A remaster | Only with creative input | Uniform level, EQ, compression or noise fixes do not count |

## Which US copyright application fits a song, a recording or both

The Copyright Office treats the song and the recording as two works. One application can cover both only if both are on the same phonorecord and one party claims both.

| Situation | How Circular 56A says to apply |
|---|---|
| An artist records someone else's song | Apply for the recording only |
| A songwriter whose song others record | Apply for the song only |
| A songwriter records their own song | One application for both is possible |
| A record company owns the song and the recording | It may use one application for both |
| The same owner also sells sheet music and the album | Two applications, each with its own deposit |
| The Single Application | Same phonorecord, one performer-author, and sole owner of both |

## Before release: a metadata checklist for the song and the recording

Tick what applies. The registers differ, so check each one separately, and use the same names and splits in all of them.

**The recording**

- One ISRC per distinct recording, kept on every format and release
- A new ISRC for each remix, edit and new take
- Existing ISRCs reused when a recording changes distributor or label
- Artist, title, version, duration, type and first publication date on file

**The song**

- Every writer named, with a role, and shares that total 100 percent
- Each writer's society and IPI name number collected
- An alternative title only for a language variation, a live version or a same-writer remix, the MLC's rule
- The ISWC copied from your society's catalogue once issued

**The registers**

- The registers on the basics page's checklist done, with the same shares in each
- Each co-writer registered with their own society
- A decision on Copyright Office registration for the song and the recording

## Identifier checker

Paste or type one identifier. The checker says what kind it is, whether it is in the right form, whether the check digit adds up where the standard has one, and gives the form to store and the form to print. A code can pass every check and still belong to another song or person, so look it up before you rely on it. Nothing you type is sent anywhere or kept.

The checker itself: https://kitesink.com/music/metadata-registration#identifiers

### ISRC (International Standard Recording Code)

- Identifies: A sound recording or music video. Each different recording has its own.
- Form: 12 characters: a 5-character prefix (2 letters, then 3 letters or digits), 2 digits for the year the code was assigned, and a 5-digit number. Written for people as ISRC AA-6Q7-20-00047. The word ISRC, spaces and hyphens are not part of the code, and lower case is read as capitals.
- Check digit: None. ISRC has no check digit, so a checker can only test the form.
- Look it up: [ISRC Search (IFPI)](https://isrcsearch.ifpi.org/)
- Sources (opened 20 November 2026): [IFPI: ISRC Handbook, 4th edition (2021), sections 3.3 and 5](https://www.ifpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/ISRC_Handbook.pdf); [IFPI: ISRC structure](https://isrc.ifpi.org/isrc-standard/isrc-structure); [International ISRC Agency: Bulletin 2015/01, validating an ISRC](https://isrc.ifpi.org/images/downloads/Bulletin_2015-01_-_Validating_ISRC_Codes.pdf)

### ISWC (International Standard Musical Work Code)

- Identifies: A musical work: the song as written, not any one recording of it.
- Form: The letter T, a 9-digit work number and one check digit (ISO 15707:2022). Usually written ISWC T-034.524.680-1; the hyphens and dots are only there to help reading.
- Check digit: ISO 15707:2022 says the check digit is a weighted modulus 10 and sets out the formula in an annex its public preview leaves out. The checker uses the formula in the ISO committee's 1999 papers: 1 plus each work digit times its position (1 to 9), and the check digit makes the total a multiple of 10. It gives the standard's own example, so a pass reads “consistent with the published formula”.
- Look it up: [ISWC-Network (CISAC)](https://iswcnet.cisac.org/)
- Sources (opened 20 November 2026): [ISO 15707:2022, clause 4 (preview)](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/83125/8adf68d27507472b892f3370f710813e/ISO-15707-2022.pdf); [ISWC International Agency: the ISWC](https://www.iswc.org/iswc); [ISO/TC 46/SC 9 N 268 (1999): the check digit formula](https://groups.niso.org/higherlogic/ws/public/download/2544/sc9n268.pdf)

### ISNI (International Standard Name Identifier)

- Identifies: The public identity of a person or organisation, such as a performer, a writer or a label. A pseudonym can have its own.
- Form: 16 characters: 15 digits and a check character, which is a digit or X. Written ISNI 0000 0001 2124 1960; the word ISNI and the spaces are not part of it.
- Check digit: The check character comes from the first 15 digits by the ISO/IEC 7064 MOD 11-2 method (ISO 27729, Annex A). X stands for 10.
- Look it up: [ISNI search](https://isni.org/page/search-database/)
- Sources (opened 20 November 2026): [ISO 27729:2024, clause 4 and Annex A (preview)](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/87177/3bea4cd1719644159883964978cc57fa/ISO-27729-2024.pdf); [ISNI: FAQs](https://isni.org/page/faqs/); [IANA: ISNI namespace registration](https://www.iana.org/assignments/urn-namespaces/urn-formal/isni)

### IPI (Interested Party Information)

- Identifies: A writer or publisher in the IPI system the CISAC societies share. Each name has an IPI name number; the IPI base number ties all of one party's names together.
- Form: Name number: 11 digits. An older 9-digit number is written with two leading zeros. Base number: usually written I-NNNNNNNNN-C, the letter I, nine digits and a final digit.
- Check digit: No check-digit rule is published for either, so the checker tests the form only. A number in the right form may still be wrong.
- Look it up: ask your PRO or society for the IPI numbers they hold for you.
- Sources (opened 20 November 2026): [SUISA: What is the IPI number? (17 April 2025)](https://blog.suisa.ch/en/what-is-the-ipi-number/); [Harry Fox Agency: What is an IPI number?](https://help.harryfox.com/what-is-an-ipi-number); [CISAC: IPI](https://www.cisac.org/services/information-services/ipi); [Wikipedia: Interested Parties Information (the base number pattern; a secondary source)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interested_Parties_Information)
