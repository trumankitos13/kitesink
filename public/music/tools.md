## Mechanical rate calculator

For physical copies and permanent downloads made and distributed in the US. Per copy, the rate is the larger of the per-work rate and the per-minute rate times the playing time, with a part minute counted as a whole one (37 CFR 385.11). This is the statutory rate; a licence agreed directly with the publisher can set a different one.

Rates as of 20 November 2026.

| Year | Per work | Per minute or part minute | Streaming, first prong (illustration) | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 12 cents | 2.31 cents | 15.1% of revenue | [Copyright Royalty Board 2022](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2022-12-16/html/2022-27237.htm), [37 CFR 385.21](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/385.21) |
| 2024 | 12.4 cents | 2.38 cents | 15.2% of revenue | [Copyright Royalty Board 2023](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-12-21/html/2023-28075.htm), [37 CFR 385.21](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/385.21) |
| 2025 | 12.7 cents | 2.45 cents | 15.25% of revenue | [Copyright Royalty Board 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-11-27/html/2024-27819.htm), [37 CFR 385.21](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/385.21) |
| 2026 | 13.1 cents | 2.52 cents | 15.3% of revenue | [Copyright Royalty Board 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-12-01/html/2025-21695.htm), [37 CFR 385.21](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/385.21) |

Example: a 5:01 track counts as 6 minutes; at 2.52 cents a minute that is 15.12 cents, more than a 13.1-cent per-work rate, so 15.12 cents per copy.

Ringtones: 24 cents per ringtone, whatever its length. Sources: [37 CFR 385.11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/385.11)

Streaming mechanicals are not a per-stream rate. Under 37 CFR 385.21 a service works out a royalty pool for each offering, the larger of a share of its revenue and other measures the rule sets, takes off what it pays for performance rights, and divides the rest among the works by their share of plays. The percentages above are the first prong only, shown as an illustration.

## Unclaimed-money checklist

Places that may hold royalties for you. Check each one that fits who you are and where your music earns.

- [ ] **The MLC** (US, mechanical): Unclaimed accrued US digital mechanicals under the blanket licence, 2021 usage onward. What to look for: Use the MLC's tools to register songs, claim missing shares and propose matches for unmatched recordings. Money still unclaimed is later shared out by market share. Cost: Free to join, per the MLC. [Unclaimed accrued royalties](https://www.themlc.com/unclaimed-accrued-royalties)
- [ ] **The MLC** (US, mechanical): Historical unmatched US streaming mechanicals from 2007 to 2020. What to look for: Join the MLC, register your works, then use its claiming and matching tools to find missing shares and propose links to unmatched uses. Matched money is paid with interest. Cost: Free to join, per the MLC. [Historical royalties](https://www.themlc.com/historical-royalties)
- [ ] **The MLC** (US, mechanical): US digital mechanicals held for rightsholders who have not yet joined the MLC. What to look for: Search your name in the Missing Member Lookup. If it appears, sign up as an MLC Member to collect the digital audio mechanicals owed to you. Cost: Free lookup and free to join. [Missing Member Lookup](https://www.themlc.com/missing-members-lookup)
- [ ] **Copyright owners, usually publishers, paid MLC unclaimed money** (US, mechanical): The songwriter's share of any unclaimed accrued royalties a publisher receives from the MLC. What to look for: When your publisher or administrator receives an MLC unclaimed distribution, ask for the payment or credit due to you and the usage behind it. If it is disputed, involve a music lawyer. Cost: The statute sets no fee or claim process. [17 USC 115(d)(3)(J)(iv)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/115)
- [ ] **SoundExchange** (US, neighbouring rights): US digital performance royalties for unregistered artists and sound recording owners. What to look for: Use the search on SoundExchange's page to see if you appear on one of its unregistered lists. Then register for free and claim your recordings in SX Direct. Cost: Free to register. [Are you owed royalties?](https://www.soundexchange.com/what-we-do/for-artists-labels-and-producers/#are-you-owed-royalties)
- [ ] **CMRRA** (Canada, mechanical): Uses by online music services in Canada of recordings or work shares that CMRRA has not yet matched. What to look for: Apply for a CMRRA Direct account (approval may need as long as 3 business days), open Unclaimed Works, match recordings to your works and add missing ownership shares. Cost: No fee stated on the page. [Unclaimed Works Portal](https://cmrra.ca/unclaimed-works/)
- [ ] **PPL** (UK, neighbouring rights): UK recorded-music royalties for performers missing from a recording's line-up. What to look for: Join PPL as a performer and, if you are not on a track's line-up, claim in myPPL. PPL may ask for evidence. If the track itself is missing, ask its rightsholder to add it. Cost: No fee stated on the page. [PPL performer claims](https://www.ppluk.com/royalties/uk-royalties/performers/)

Who can claim, the rules and the sources: https://kitesink.com/music/claimable.md

## Identifier checker

Paste or type one identifier. The checker says what kind it is, whether it is in the right form, whether the check digit adds up where the standard has one, and gives the form to store and the form to print. A code can pass every check and still belong to another song or person, so look it up before you rely on it. Nothing you type is sent anywhere or kept.

The checker itself: https://kitesink.com/music/tools#identifiers

### ISRC (International Standard Recording Code)

- Identifies: A sound recording or music video. Each different recording has its own.
- Form: 12 characters: a 5-character prefix (2 letters, then 3 letters or digits), 2 digits for the year the code was assigned, and a 5-digit number. Written for people as ISRC AA-6Q7-20-00047. The word ISRC, spaces and hyphens are not part of the code, and lower case is read as capitals.
- Check digit: None. ISRC has no check digit, so a checker can only test the form.
- Look it up: [ISRC Search (IFPI)](https://isrcsearch.ifpi.org/)
- Sources (opened 20 November 2026): [IFPI: ISRC Handbook, 4th edition (2021), sections 3.3 and 5](https://www.ifpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/ISRC_Handbook.pdf); [IFPI: ISRC structure](https://isrc.ifpi.org/isrc-standard/isrc-structure); [International ISRC Agency: Bulletin 2015/01, validating an ISRC](https://isrc.ifpi.org/images/downloads/Bulletin_2015-01_-_Validating_ISRC_Codes.pdf)

### ISWC (International Standard Musical Work Code)

- Identifies: A musical work: the song as written, not any one recording of it.
- Form: The letter T, a 9-digit work number and one check digit (ISO 15707:2022). Usually written ISWC T-034.524.680-1; the hyphens and dots are only there to help reading.
- Check digit: ISO 15707:2022 says the check digit is a weighted modulus 10 and sets out the formula in an annex its public preview leaves out. The checker uses the formula in the ISO committee's 1999 papers: 1 plus each work digit times its position (1 to 9), and the check digit makes the total a multiple of 10. It gives the standard's own example, so a pass reads “consistent with the published formula”.
- Look it up: [ISWC-Network (CISAC)](https://iswcnet.cisac.org/)
- Sources (opened 20 November 2026): [ISO 15707:2022, clause 4 (preview)](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/83125/8adf68d27507472b892f3370f710813e/ISO-15707-2022.pdf); [ISWC International Agency: the ISWC](https://www.iswc.org/iswc); [ISO/TC 46/SC 9 N 268 (1999): the check digit formula](https://groups.niso.org/higherlogic/ws/public/download/2544/sc9n268.pdf)

### ISNI (International Standard Name Identifier)

- Identifies: The public identity of a person or organisation, such as a performer, a writer or a label. A pseudonym can have its own.
- Form: 16 characters: 15 digits and a check character, which is a digit or X. Written ISNI 0000 0001 2124 1960; the word ISNI and the spaces are not part of it.
- Check digit: The check character comes from the first 15 digits by the ISO/IEC 7064 MOD 11-2 method (ISO 27729, Annex A). X stands for 10.
- Look it up: [ISNI search](https://isni.org/page/search-database/)
- Sources (opened 20 November 2026): [ISO 27729:2024, clause 4 and Annex A (preview)](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/87177/3bea4cd1719644159883964978cc57fa/ISO-27729-2024.pdf); [ISNI: FAQs](https://isni.org/page/faqs/); [IANA: ISNI namespace registration](https://www.iana.org/assignments/urn-namespaces/urn-formal/isni)

### IPI (Interested Party Information)

- Identifies: A writer or publisher in the IPI system the CISAC societies share. Each name has an IPI name number; the IPI base number ties all of one party's names together.
- Form: Name number: 11 digits. An older 9-digit number is written with two leading zeros. Base number: usually written I-NNNNNNNNN-C, the letter I, nine digits and a final digit.
- Check digit: No check-digit rule is published for either, so the checker tests the form only. A number in the right form may still be wrong.
- Look it up: ask your PRO or society for the IPI numbers they hold for you.
- Sources (opened 20 November 2026): [SUISA: What is the IPI number? (17 April 2025)](https://blog.suisa.ch/en/what-is-the-ipi-number/); [Harry Fox Agency: What is an IPI number?](https://help.harryfox.com/what-is-an-ipi-number); [CISAC: IPI](https://www.cisac.org/services/information-services/ipi); [Wikipedia: Interested Parties Information (the base number pattern; a secondary source)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interested_Parties_Information)

## Split-sheet generator

A split sheet records what the writers of a song agreed: who wrote it, in what role, and each writer's share. It is not a publishing contract, and this page is not legal advice. For anything beyond recording the split, get a music lawyer.

Fill it in together, check that the shares make exactly 100 percent, then print it and have every writer sign and date it. Give every writer a copy.

A sheet holds the song title, any alternate titles and the date agreed; the ISWC, and the ISRC and artist of the recording, if there are any; and whether the song uses samples or interpolations, and which. Samples and interpolations need clearance from the owners of the work they use. This sheet does not clear them.

For each writer it holds their legal name, role, PRO or society, IPI name number, publisher and the publisher's role, and share, with a line to sign and date. Shares are percentages of the song, to two decimal places, and must add up to exactly 100. Split evenly gives three writers 33.34, 33.33 and 33.33. Pick each writer's role for what they wrote. A producer who co-wrote the song gets the role for their part, with "Also credited as the producer" ticked.

Writer roles: These are the writer roles societies use when a song is registered. Arranger, adaptor and translator are for a new version of a song that already exists. If that song is still in copyright, its owners must agree to the new version.

- Composer: Wrote music for the song, but none of the words.
- Author (lyricist): Wrote words for the song, but none of the music.
- Composer and author: Wrote both music and words for the song.
- Arranger: Made a new arrangement of the music of a song that already existed.
- Adaptor: Reworked the words of a song that already existed, other than by translating them.
- Translator: Translated the words of a song that already existed into another language.

Publisher roles:

- Original publisher: The publisher the writer signed their share of this song to. A writer who publishes themselves names their own company.
- Administrator: A company that registers the song and collects for the writer or their publisher under an admin deal, usually without owning it.

## Other tools worth knowing

AI tools have a directory of their own, with what each one does with your files and your data: https://kitesink.com/music/ai-tools.md

### Works and repertoire searches

- [SESAC Repertory](https://www.sesac.com/repertory/) (SESAC): Search works SESAC represents by title, work number, writer, publisher or artist; shows SESAC's share and its writers and publishers. You accept extra terms of use before searching; SESAC warns results may be incomplete. Access: Free, no login. For: songwriters, publishers and labels, administrators. Where: US. Checked 20 November 2026.

### Unclaimed money

- [MLC Missing Member Lookup](https://www.themlc.com/missing-members-lookup) (The MLC): Search a name to see whether the MLC may hold US digital mechanicals for a rightsholder who has not joined. Leaves out historical unmatched money. Access: Free, no login. For: songwriters, publishers and labels, administrators. Where: US. Checked 20 November 2026.
- [MLC royalty dashboards](https://www.themlc.com/dashboards) (The MLC): Public MLC pages charting royalty totals: blanket royalties since 2021, historical 2007-2020 by rate period and service, and unclaimed 2021 usage by month. Each dashboard sits on a public MLC page that opened without sign-in. Access: Free, no login. For: songwriters, publishers and labels, administrators. Where: US. Checked 20 November 2026.
- [MLC Matching and Claiming tools](https://www.themlc.com/tools) (The MLC): Portal tools to claim missing shares of registered songs and to propose matches between unmatched recordings and your songs. Sign in to the Member Hub (matching also in the Songwriter Hub). The MLC says connecting with it is free. Access: Free, login needed. For: songwriters, publishers and labels, administrators. Where: US. Checked 20 November 2026.

### Metadata and file formats

- [Django Music Publisher](https://github.com/matijakolaric-com/django-music-publisher) (Matija Kolaric): Open-source software you install and run yourself to keep song metadata, register and license works and process publishing royalties. MIT licence per the repository; installed from PyPI and needs technical skill to run. Access: Free, no login. For: publishers and labels, administrators. Where: International. Checked 20 November 2026.

### Splits and agreements

- [Music Business Toolbox](https://musicbusinesstoolbox.com/) (Music Business Toolbox): Music business templates and calculators, including split agreements, budgets and contracts, in a paid subscription with a small free set. Split, budget and contract tools are in the paid tier; the free tier has a checklist and guides. Access: Paid. For: songwriters, artists, producers. Where: International. Checked 20 November 2026.
