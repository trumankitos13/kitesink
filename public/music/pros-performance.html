What PROs doUS law gives song owners the exclusive right to perform in publicSection 106(4) gives the owner of a musical work the exclusive right to perform it publicly, live or by transmission. A PRO licenses that right for its members.BeginnerSourced

Section 101 treats a performance as public if it happens where the public can attend or a sizeable group beyond family and friends gathers, or if it is transmitted to the public, whether people receive it together or apart. Section 106 makes the right subject to the exceptions in sections 107 to 122, so not every public play needs a licence. The basics page sets this right beside the mechanical right.

  • Ask a music lawyer before relying on an exception for a specific use.

What PROs doCollective licensing exists because song-by-song deals do not scaleA PRO pools many owners' rights so a user can cover many songs with one licence, and a writer does not chase every bar, station and service alone.BeginnerSourced

The US Department of Justice describes ASCAP and BMI as pooling their members' copyrights and licensing them collectively, so users avoid separate deals with every writer and publisher. WIPO's training material makes the same point: where works are performed in public or broadcast, collective management is the only practical route for both sides.

What PROs doGMR says the venue, not the performer, must hold the licenceGMR states that the operator of a venue, not the musicians or DJs playing there, is responsible for licensing the music performed on its premises.IntermediateSourcedAs of 20 November 2026

Global Music Rights n.d.-b

What PROs doMany societies take your performing right, not just a mandate to collectIn many English-speaking countries a society takes over ownership of the performing right outright, so it holds that right and can enforce it.AdvancedSourced

WIPO's 2012 training material describes a range. Some bodies act only as agent for the rights holder. In many English-speaking countries the society takes a full assignment and becomes owner of the performing right. In Germany it holds rights in trust, and the US practice it describes is a non-exclusive licence. Terms differ by society, so read the membership terms before you sign, and ask a music lawyer what an assignment means for your catalogue.

US landscapeA licence from one US PRO does not cover another PRO's songsGMR says a venue licensed by ASCAP, BMI or SESAC still needs a GMR licence to play GMR writers' music, and the reverse also holds.IntermediateSourcedAs of 20 November 2026

GMR's FAQ says its licence is separate from those of ASCAP, BMI and SESAC, and that theirs do not cover GMR clients' songs. A user playing songs from several PROs needs a licence from each. Where co-writers belong to different PROs, the DOJ says ASCAP and BMI favour licensing only the share they control; see fractional licensing below.

Consent decreesThe ASCAP and BMI consent decrees shape what each can doThe decrees bar exclusive licensing, let members license their works directly, and require ASCAP and BMI to accept qualifying members.IntermediateSourcedAs of 15 January 2021

The DOJ says the decrees date from 1941 in their earliest form, with the latest changes in 1994 (BMI) and 2001 (ASCAP). They also require the same terms for similarly placed users, cap membership agreements at five years, and shield users from infringement suits over works missing from or wrongly shown in the public repertories. Stated as of the January 2021 closing statement.

Consent decreesSESAC and GMR are not bound by the consent decreesThe DOJ notes that the two decrees bind only ASCAP and BMI, so other PROs work without those limits. Terms can differ.AdvancedSourcedAs of 15 January 2021

In its 2021 statement the DOJ listed this among critics' concerns and also noted that more PROs had risen in the market. The decrees set rules on membership terms and licence access for ASCAP and BMI; the other two PROs run under their own terms. Read each PRO's agreement, and use a music lawyer for any decision about assigning rights.

Consent decreesUnder fractional licensing, each PRO licenses only its own share of a songA user then needs a licence from every PRO with a share of the song, or from the owners directly. The Second Circuit held that BMI's decree permits this.AdvancedSourcedAs of 15 January 2021

ASCAP and BMI favour fractional licensing; licensees favour full-work licensing, where one licence covers the whole song. The DOJ once took the view that the decrees required full-work licensing, and the Second Circuit disagreed on BMI's decree. The 2021 statement says BMI is not obliged to license full works and that the debate continues. For writers, co-writers' PRO choices affect how a work is licensed.

RegisteringRegistering the writer side and never checking who holds the publisher shareMany songs have at least a writer and a publisher. If the publisher side of a registration is empty or wrong, the song may not pay as you expect.MistakeIntermediateDirectional

WIPO notes most works have a composer and a publisher, and that societies need every rights holder documented. Societies differ on how they treat the publisher share of a song with no publisher, and this guide states no rule for any US PRO. Ask your society how it treats an unpublished song.

PaymentsSocieties may pay from samples or proxy data when counting every play is impracticalWIPO says ideal pay-per-play is not always practical, so societies may pay from a sample of uses or from a proxy source such as radio data.AdvancedSourced
