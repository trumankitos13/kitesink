Mechanical rates, per copy.

The US statutory rate per copy for a year, a playing time and a number of copies. Not legal advice.

For physical copies and permanent downloads made and distributed in the US. Per copy, the rate is the larger of the per-work rate and the per-minute rate times the playing time, with a part minute counted as a whole one (37 CFR 385.11). This is the statutory rate; a licence agreed directly with the publisher can set a different one. Rates as of 20 November 2026.

Streaming: an illustration, not a rate

Streaming mechanicals are not a per-stream rate. Under 37 CFR 385.21 a service works out a royalty pool for each offering, the larger of a share of its revenue and other measures the rule sets, takes off what it pays for performance rights, and divides the rest among the works by their share of plays.

In 2027 the first prong is 15.35% of the service's revenue for the offering. 37 CFR 385.21

Identifiers

Check a code before you send it.

ISRC, ISWC, ISNI and IPI: what each one identifies, whether a code is well formed, and where to look it up.

Paste or type one identifier. The checker says what kind it is, whether it is in the right form, whether the check digit adds up where the standard has one, and gives the form to store and the form to print. A code can pass every check and still belong to another song or person, so look it up before you rely on it. Nothing you type is sent anywhere or kept.

Or try an example from the standards:

The four formats, and where each rule comes from

ISRC International Standard Recording Code

Identifies
A sound recording or music video. Each different recording has its own.
Form
12 characters: a 5-character prefix (2 letters, then 3 letters or digits), 2 digits for the year the code was assigned, and a 5-digit number. Written for people as ISRC AA-6Q7-20-00047. The word ISRC, spaces and hyphens are not part of the code, and lower case is read as capitals.
Check digit
None. ISRC has no check digit, so a checker can only test the form.
Look it up
ISRC Search (IFPI)

Sources: IFPI: ISRC Handbook, 4th edition (2021), sections 3.3 and 5 · IFPI: ISRC structure · International ISRC Agency: Bulletin 2015/01, validating an ISRC, opened 20 November 2026.

ISWC International Standard Musical Work Code

Identifies
A musical work: the song as written, not any one recording of it.
Form
The letter T, a 9-digit work number and one check digit (ISO 15707:2022). Usually written ISWC T-034.524.680-1; the hyphens and dots are only there to help reading.
Check digit
ISO 15707:2022 says the check digit is a weighted modulus 10 and sets out the formula in an annex its public preview leaves out. The checker uses the formula in the ISO committee's 1999 papers: 1 plus each work digit times its position (1 to 9), and the check digit makes the total a multiple of 10. It gives the standard's own example, so a pass reads “consistent with the published formula”.
Look it up
ISWC-Network (CISAC)

Sources: ISO 15707:2022, clause 4 (preview) · ISWC International Agency: the ISWC · ISO/TC 46/SC 9 N 268 (1999): the check digit formula, opened 20 November 2026.

ISNI International Standard Name Identifier

Identifies
The public identity of a person or organisation, such as a performer, a writer or a label. A pseudonym can have its own.
Form
16 characters: 15 digits and a check character, which is a digit or X. Written ISNI 0000 0001 2124 1960; the word ISNI and the spaces are not part of it.
Check digit
The check character comes from the first 15 digits by the ISO/IEC 7064 MOD 11-2 method (ISO 27729, Annex A). X stands for 10.
Look it up
ISNI search

Sources: ISO 27729:2024, clause 4 and Annex A (preview) · ISNI: FAQs · IANA: ISNI namespace registration, opened 20 November 2026.

IPI Interested Party Information

Identifies
A writer or publisher in the IPI system the CISAC societies share. Each name has an IPI name number; the IPI base number ties all of one party's names together.
Form
Name number: 11 digits. An older 9-digit number is written with two leading zeros. Base number: usually written I-NNNNNNNNN-C, the letter I, nine digits and a final digit.
Check digit
No check-digit rule is published for either, so the checker tests the form only. A number in the right form may still be wrong.
Look it up
Ask your PRO or society for the IPI numbers they hold for you.

Sources: SUISA: What is the IPI number? (17 April 2025) · Harry Fox Agency: What is an IPI number? · CISAC: IPI · Wikipedia: Interested Parties Information (the base number pattern; a secondary source), opened 20 November 2026.

Split sheet

Agree the split, then write it down.

For the co-writers of one song: who wrote it, in what role, and each writer's share, ready to print and sign.

Not a contract

A split sheet records what the writers of a song agreed: who wrote it, in what role, and each writer's share. It is not a publishing contract, and this page is not legal advice. For anything beyond recording the split, get a music lawyer.

Fill it in together, check that the shares make exactly 100 percent, then print it and have every writer sign and date it. Give every writer a copy.

The generator needs JavaScript. A split sheet can also be written by hand: the song title, any other titles and the date agreed; the ISWC, and the ISRC and artist of the recording, if there are any; whether the song uses samples or interpolations, and which; then for each writer their legal name, role, PRO or society, IPI name number, publisher and the publisher's role, and share, with the shares adding up to 100 percent; and a line for each writer to sign and date.

The song

Only if the song already has one. A song gets its ISWC when it is first registered, so a new song will not have one yet.

The code of the recording this split is for, if it has one.

Does the song use a sample or an interpolation?

A sample uses part of an existing recording. An interpolation re-records part of an existing song.

Samples and interpolations need clearance from the owners of the work they use. This sheet does not clear them.

The writers

Shares are percentages of the song, to two decimal places, and must add up to exactly 100. Split evenly gives three writers 33.34, 33.33 and 33.33. Pick each writer's role for what they wrote. A producer who co-wrote the song gets the role for their part, with "Also credited as the producer" ticked.

What the roles mean

These are the writer roles societies use when a song is registered. Arranger, adaptor and translator are for a new version of a song that already exists. If that song is still in copyright, its owners must agree to the new version.

Composer
Wrote music for the song, but none of the words.
Author (lyricist)
Wrote words for the song, but none of the music.
Composer and author
Wrote both music and words for the song.
Arranger
Made a new arrangement of the music of a song that already existed.
Adaptor
Reworked the words of a song that already existed, other than by translating them.
Translator
Translated the words of a song that already existed into another language.

The publisher's role, when the writer has a publisher for this song:

Original publisher
The publisher the writer signed their share of this song to. A writer who publishes themselves names their own company.
Administrator
A company that registers the song and collects for the writer or their publisher under an admin deal, usually without owning it.
