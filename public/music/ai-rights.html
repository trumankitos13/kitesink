Who owns AI-assisted musicUS copyright needs a human author, and the courts have said soIn the US, a work with no human author cannot be registered, and the Supreme Court declined in March 2026 to revisit that.BeginnerSourcedAs of 20 November 2026

The D.C. Circuit decided Thaler v. Perlmutter (No. 23-5233) on 18 March 2025. It held that the Copyright Act requires eligible work to be authored first by a human being, and it upheld the refusal to register an image credited to a software system. The Supreme Court's docket for No. 25-449 shows the petition denied on 2 March 2026. The case concerned an image, not a song, and it did not say how much human input is enough.

  • Treat a track made by a tool with no real human input from you as outside US copyright protection.
  • Ask a music lawyer how the rule applies if your human input is partial.

Who owns AI-assisted musicA text prompt alone does not make you the author of the outputThe Copyright Office says that, with today's tools, prompts alone do not give a user enough control to be the author of what comes back.BeginnerSourcedAs of 20 November 2026

Part 2 of the Office's AI report (29 January 2025) says human authors keep rights in what they wrote or played that shows in an output, in a creative selection or arrangement of outputs, and in creative changes they make. Whether the human input is enough is decided case by case. The Office saw no need for a new right for AI-generated content. This is the Office's view, not a court ruling.

  • Keep dated project files that show what you wrote, played and edited yourself.
  • Treat a song generated from a prompt alone as unprotected in the US.

Voice and likenessTennessee's ELVIS Act protects a person's voice, including a simulated oneSince 1 July 2024, Tennessee law covers a voice whether real or simulated, and reaches tools whose main purpose is copying one person's voice.IntermediateSourcedAs of 20 November 2026

Public Chapter 588 (HB 2091) amends Tennessee's personal rights law. It defines voice as a sound readily identifiable with an individual, real or simulated. It creates liability for publishing or distributing a voice or likeness with knowledge it was not authorised, and for offering a tool whose primary purpose is producing a specific person's voice or likeness. A company holding an exclusive recording or distribution contract can also sue. Uses such as news, criticism and parody are treated as fair where the First Amendment protects them. It is state law; a lawyer should say whether it reaches a case.

At release · Registering AI-assisted worksRegistering a song that contains AI material with the US Copyright OfficeUse the Standard Application, claim only your own authorship, and exclude AI material that is more than a minor part of the work.StepIntermediateSourcedAs of 20 November 2026

The Office's guidance (88 FR 16190, effective 16 March 2023) says to describe the human authorship in the Author Created field and not to name an AI tool or its maker as an author. AI content beyond a minor amount should be excluded, which can be done under Limitation of the Claim, Other, Material Excluded. If you are unsure, you can give a general statement that the work contains AI material, and the Office will follow up. An earlier registration can be corrected by a supplementary registration. The guidance covers all kinds of works and does not name music.
