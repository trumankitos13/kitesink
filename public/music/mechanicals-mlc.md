# Mechanicals and the MLC: KiteSink music field guide

Reproduction royalties, US rates and the Mechanical Licensing Collective. Each entry gives the point, the reasoning, what to do, who it is for, where it applies, how well sourced it is, and its sources.
Grades: Sourced (law or official) and Directional (reported or industry practice).

## Guidance

### Digital mechanicals now run through the MLC, physical ones still use NOIs

**Since 2021 a digital service licenses US mechanicals from the MLC under a blanket license. Physical copies still use the song-by-song notice route.**

For digital phonorecord deliveries on or after the license availability date, 17 USC 115 sends a service to the blanket license process through the MLC. The Copyright Office FAQ adds that it no longer accepts NOIs for digital uses and that NOIs remain for non-digital uses such as CDs and vinyl. The statute keeps one exception: a record company may still get an individual download license by notice. Direct deals can sit outside this route.

- Check which route a release uses before chasing a payment

### Past five minutes, the per-minute rate sets the price of a copy

**For copies and downloads the rate is the larger of a flat amount and a per-minute amount, so a track over five minutes costs more per copy than a shorter one.**

For 2026 the larger of two figures applies: a flat 13.1 cents, or 2.52 cents multiplied by the minutes, counting any part of a minute as a whole one. A track of exactly five minutes works out to 12.6 cents by the minute formula, so the 13.1 cent rate wins. A track of 5:01 counts as six minutes, which gives 15.12 cents. This is arithmetic on the published rates, not a separate rule.

- Round each track up to whole minutes before comparing the two amounts

### The physical and download rate is adjusted every January for inflation

**The Judges publish a cost-of-living notice at least 25 days before 1 January. It indexes base rates of 12 cents and 2.31 cents to the CPI-U published in November 2022.**

The 2026 notice used a CPI-U of 324.800 against the November 2022 base of 298.012, which gave 13.1 cents and 2.52 cents. It notes that the Bureau of Labor Statistics could not collect October 2025 data during a lapse in appropriations, so the index published on 24 October 2025 was used. On 19 November 2026 the regulation still showed the 2026 rate, so no 2027 figure is given here.

- Look for the next Federal Register notice from the Copyright Royalty Board in December

### Streaming mechanicals are worked out in four steps from a pool

**Take the greater of the revenue and content-cost prongs, subtract performance royalties, apply any floor, then split the pool across plays.**

37 CFR 385.21(b) sets out the steps for each offering: the all-in royalty, the deduction of performance royalties the service pays, the payable pool after any floor, and the per-work share. Mixed service bundles use the content-cost prong alone. The MLC does the last allocation. For that split only, a play of a track over five minutes counts extra: 1.2 plays for 5:01 to 6:00, plus 0.2 for each further minute or part of one.

- Read the regulation itself for any Offering-specific term

### The MLC does not take a fee from mechanical royalties

**The MLC says digital services fund its operating budget and that it takes no fees out of the mechanical royalties it pays.**

The MLC's audits page says its budget is paid by an assessment that the Copyright Royalty Board sets, separate from the royalties the services owe, and that it deducts no fees from royalties. The Copyright Office FAQ also names voluntary contributions from the services. This is how the MLC describes itself, so check your own statements.

### The MLC database is free to search, but its ownership data can be wrong or partial

**Public search is free by law, and bulk and API access must be offered too. The MLC warns that listed ownership may be incomplete or out of date.**

37 CFR 210.32 requires a free, searchable online database and bulk access through APIs. Bulk access is free to licensed services and the Register; others may be charged up to the MLC's marginal cost. The MLC's terms say shares shown may not reflect the whole ownership, that some works in the portal cannot be licensed through the MLC, and that it will not delete accurate data. Members report errors to MLC support; a represented writer tells their publisher or administrator.

- Report errors to MLC support or to your publisher or administrator

### April 2027 is when market-share distributions begin, one month at a time

**The MLC plans to start in April 2027 with January 2021 usage, then take one more month of usage each month. It is a start date, not a cut-off.**

The MLC's September 2026 release says it will allocate each month's remainder song by song, pro rata, separately for each DSP and offering, with interest at the statutory rate. The June 2026 one-sheet adds that it will keep reprocessing unmatched royalties into regular distributions. These are the MLC's plans; the schedule can change.

- Watch the MLC's unclaimed dashboard for the monthly figures
- Register and match your songs before your usage months come up

### Claiming before a month's royalties are distributed gets you the accrued amount

**If an owner is found before the accrued royalties are included in a market-share distribution, the MLC pays them to that owner with interest.**

17 USC 115(d)(3)(I) says that when an owner is located for an unmatched work, or a share of one, the MLC updates its records and, if the royalties have not yet gone into an unclaimed-royalties distribution, pays them with proportionate interest and a cumulative statement. The text does not say what happens to a claim after that point, so ask a music lawyer if an old amount matters.

- Claim and match songs well ahead of any distribution

### The MLC must hold unmatched royalties for at least three years first

**The statute sets a three-year minimum hold, in an interest-bearing account, before unmatched money can go into a market-share distribution.**

The hold runs from the date of receipt, or from the date a service accrued the money before transferring it, whichever ends sooner. The account earns interest each month at the federal short-term rate for the benefit of the owners. A longer hold is allowed; the statute sets only the minimum.

Unclaimed money · Intermediate · Sourced (law) · For: songwriters, publishers and labels, administrators · Where: US · Sources: [17 USC 115](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/115)

### Publishers must pass a share of unclaimed distributions to songwriters

**Owners who receive an unclaimed distribution must pay or credit songwriters by reported usage, and never under half of what the owner receives for that songwriter's works.**

17 USC 115(d)(3)(J)(iv) applies despite contrary terms in an agreement. The allocation follows each song's reported usage in the periods covered, and the floor is 50 percent of the payment the owner receives attributable to that songwriter's works. Contract terms otherwise apply, so a music lawyer should read any agreement.

- Ask your publisher how it will credit these payments

### Historical royalties cover 2007 to 2020 and are separate from blanket royalties

**Services handed over unmatched money for 2007 to 2020 streams in early 2021. The MLC began paying matched amounts in 2022 and has not started market-share distributions for them.**

The Music Modernization Act (MMA) limited a service's liability if it transferred by 15 February 2021, and 21 services chose to. The historical page gives both May and June 2022 as the start of matched payments. It says leftover historical money will go through market share later, and that this had not begun as of its November 2026 data. The one-sheet says the MLC will look to start on it once blanket market-share distributions are under way. The Missing Member Lookup leaves historical money out.

- Search the Matching Tool for old unmatched uses of your songs

### The Copyright Office recommended holding unclaimed money longer than the minimum

**The Office's July 2021 report recommends no first distribution until at least five years after claiming was fully available. The MMA requires the MLC to give the report substantial weight.**

The Register's report also recommends independent checks of market-share calculations, wide publicity for the advance notice of a distribution, and close tracking of match and engagement metrics. These are recommendations; the statute's minimum hold is three years. The MLC says that by its April 2027 start it will have tried for more than six years to match January 2021 usage.

### MLC statements come in two layouts: by recording or by work

**Royalty Detail shows rows per sound recording from the services' reports. Work Summary rolls the same money up per musical work. Both are .tsv files.**

The MLC's Help Center links example files for both layouts. The .tsv format opens in common spreadsheet software. Royalty Detail is the one to use when tracing a payment to a service's usage line; Work Summary shows what each song earned.

### The MLC can audit digital services, at most once in any three calendar years

**An audit covers up to three full calendar years, starts with a notice filed at the Copyright Office, and is done by an independent qualified auditor.**

Under 17 USC 115(d)(4)(D) the MLC pays for the audit unless the auditor finds an underpayment of at least 10 percent, in which case the service bears the reasonable costs. The MLC says it will pass on recovered royalties in full and mark audit adjustments on members' statements. Owners can audit the MLC too, at their own cost, once a year, covering up to the three preceding calendar years.

- Look for adjustments marked as audit results on your MLC statement

## In order

### Serve the notice before you distribute physical copies

**For a compulsory license on vinyl or CDs, serve an NOI on the owner before, or within 30 days after, making copies, and always before distributing any.**

The statute puts the notice before distribution. If the Copyright Office records name an owner and an address, serve that owner. If they do not, the notice is filed with the Office instead. Missing the notice rules out the compulsory license, and without a voluntary (direct) license from the owner, making and selling the copies is infringement. Involve a music lawyer if the situation is unusual.

- Search the Copyright Office records for the owner before serving
- Note the 30-day outer limit and the rule to serve before distributing

## Common mistakes

### Treating a mechanical license as permission to use someone's recording

**Section 115 covers the song only. Copying another person's recording needs separate permission from whoever owns that recording.**

A song and a recording of it are two separate works with separate owners. The compulsory license lets you record your own version of a published song. It does not let you copy, sample or reissue an existing recording. Whoever controls that recording has to agree separately, and a music lawyer should check any case that mixes the two.

- Clear the song and the recording as two separate permissions
- Record your own version if you only hold a mechanical license

The license · Beginner · Sourced (official) · For: artists, producers, publishers and labels · Where: US · Sources: [U.S. Copyright Office 2018](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ73.pdf), [17 USC 115](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/115)

### Reading 13.1 cents as what a stream pays

**The 13.1 cent figure is for physical copies and downloads. Streaming mechanicals come from a pool formula, not a fixed amount per play.**

Subpart B sets a per-work rate for each physical copy or permanent download. Subpart C works out a royalty pool for each streaming offering, then divides it across plays. What one stream earns depends on the service, the offering and the pool, so no single per-stream figure appears in the regulation.

- Use the Subpart B rate only for copies and downloads

### Assuming a copyright registration puts your song in the MLC

**A Copyright Office registration and an MLC registration are different. You, or your publisher or administrator, need the MLC record to be paid blanket-license digital mechanicals.**

The Copyright Office FAQ says owners with a copyright registration still need to register with the MLC. It also says a song need not be registered with the Office to be eligible for royalties through the MLC, though physical-copy royalties under the notice route depend on the owner appearing in the Office's records. Registration has other benefits in copyright law.

- Register or claim the song with the MLC as its own task

## Getting paid digital mechanicals by the MLC: a checklist

- Search the Public Work Search for each song
- Check the Missing Member Lookup for your name

**Get the songs right**

- Claim missing shares with the Claiming Tool
- Register songs that are not in the database
- Resolve any overclaim flagged on a song

**Keep it current**

- Update banking and tax details in Member Settings
- Check the Matching Tool for unmatched recordings
- Download a catalogue export after each change

## Terms

Defined on the glossary: https://kitesink.com/music/glossary.md

- **Blanket license (digital)**: Since 1 January 2021, a qualifying digital service can get one license through the MLC that covers every song open to the compulsory license, in place of song-by-song notices.
- **Compulsory (mechanical) license**: A US legal route to record and distribute your own version of a song once recordings of it have been released in the US with the owner's permission. You must meet the conditions and pay the statutory rate.
- **Market-share distribution**: How the statute says remaining unclaimed accrued royalties are paid: to identified owners in proportion to their relative market shares in the usage reports, with a share passed on to songwriters.
- **Notice of intention (NOI)**: The notice a person serves on a song's copyright owner, or files with the Copyright Office when the owner cannot be found in its records, to use the compulsory license for physical copies.
- **Overclaim**: A song where the shares claimed by all members together come to more than 100 percent. The MLC's Overclaims Tool lets owners amend or give up shares and upload documents while it is resolved.
- **Phonorecord and digital phonorecord delivery (DPD)**: A phonorecord is an object that holds recorded sound, such as vinyl or a CD. A DPD is a digital transmission that leaves the recipient with a specific copy: a permanent or limited download, or an on-demand stream.
- **The DLC (Digital Licensee Coordinator)**: The non-profit that represents licensed digital services. It names a non-voting member of the MLC board and can take part in Copyright Royalty Board proceedings that set the assessment funding the MLC.
- **The MLC (Mechanical Licensing Collective)**: The non-profit the Copyright Office designated on 8 July 2019 to run the blanket license: it collects digital mechanicals, matches them to songs and owners, distributes them, and keeps the public database.
- **Unclaimed accrued royalties**: Money the MLC has collected but still cannot match to an owner or pay out when the legal minimum holding period ends. The MLC counts both unmatched and unclaimed amounts in it.

## Mechanical rate calculator

For physical copies and permanent downloads made and distributed in the US. Per copy, the rate is the larger of the per-work rate and the per-minute rate times the playing time, with a part minute counted as a whole one (37 CFR 385.11). This is the statutory rate; a licence agreed directly with the publisher can set a different one.

Rates as of 20 November 2026.

| Year | Per work | Per minute or part minute | Streaming, first prong (illustration) | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 12 cents | 2.31 cents | 15.1% of revenue | [Copyright Royalty Board 2022](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2022-12-16/html/2022-27237.htm), [37 CFR 385.21](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/385.21) |
| 2024 | 12.4 cents | 2.38 cents | 15.2% of revenue | [Copyright Royalty Board 2023](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-12-21/html/2023-28075.htm), [37 CFR 385.21](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/385.21) |
| 2025 | 12.7 cents | 2.45 cents | 15.25% of revenue | [Copyright Royalty Board 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-11-27/html/2024-27819.htm), [37 CFR 385.21](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/385.21) |
| 2026 | 13.1 cents | 2.52 cents | 15.3% of revenue | [Copyright Royalty Board 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-12-01/html/2025-21695.htm), [37 CFR 385.21](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/385.21) |

Example: a 5:01 track counts as 6 minutes; at 2.52 cents a minute that is 15.12 cents, more than a 13.1-cent per-work rate, so 15.12 cents per copy.

Ringtones: 24 cents per ringtone, whatever its length. Sources: [37 CFR 385.11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/385.11)

Streaming mechanicals are not a per-stream rate. Under 37 CFR 385.21 a service works out a royalty pool for each offering, the larger of a share of its revenue and other measures the rule sets, takes off what it pays for performance rights, and divides the rest among the works by their share of plays. The percentages above are the first prong only, shown as an illustration.
