General information on how music rights and royalties work, not legal advice. For a contract, a termination notice or a dispute, talk to a music lawyer.

Mechanical rates, per copy.

The US statutory rate per copy for a year, a playing time and a number of copies. Not legal advice.

For physical copies and permanent downloads made and distributed in the US. Per copy, the rate is the larger of the per-work rate and the per-minute rate times the playing time, with a part minute counted as a whole one (37 CFR 385.11). This is the statutory rate; a licence agreed directly with the publisher can set a different one. Rates as of 20 November 2026.

Streaming: an illustration, not a rate

Streaming mechanicals are not a per-stream rate. Under 37 CFR 385.21 a service works out a royalty pool for each offering, the larger of a share of its revenue and other measures the rule sets, takes off what it pays for performance rights, and divides the rest among the works by their share of plays.

In 2027 the first prong is 15.35% of the service's revenue for the offering. 37 CFR 385.21

The licenseDigital mechanicals now run through the MLC, physical ones still use NOIsSince 2021 a digital service licenses US mechanicals from the MLC under a blanket license. Physical copies still use the song-by-song notice route.BeginnerSourcedAs of 19 November 2026

For digital phonorecord deliveries on or after the license availability date, 17 USC 115 sends a service to the blanket license process through the MLC. The Copyright Office FAQ adds that it no longer accepts NOIs for digital uses and that NOIs remain for non-digital uses such as CDs and vinyl. 17 USC 115 · Copyright Office: MMA FAQ

Before release · The licenseServe the notice before you distribute physical copiesFor a compulsory license on vinyl or CDs, serve an NOI on the owner before, or within 30 days after, making copies, and always before distributing any.StepIntermediateSourced

The statute puts the notice before distribution. If the Copyright Office records name an owner and an address, serve that owner. If they do not, the notice is filed with the Office instead. Missing the notice rules out the compulsory license, and without a voluntary (direct) license from the owner, making and selling the copies is infringement. Involve a music lawyer if the situation is unusual.

  • Search the Copyright Office records for the owner before serving
  • Note the 30-day outer limit and the rule to serve before distributing

17 USC 115

The licenseTreating a mechanical license as permission to use someone's recordingSection 115 covers the song only. Copying another person's recording needs separate permission from whoever owns that recording.MistakeBeginnerSourced

A song and a recording of it are two separate works with separate owners. The compulsory license lets you record your own version of a published song. It does not let you copy, sample or reissue an existing recording. Whoever controls that recording has to agree separately, and a music lawyer should check any case that mixes the two.

  • Clear the song and the recording as two separate permissions
  • Record your own version if you only hold a mechanical license

U.S. Copyright Office 2018 · 17 USC 115

RatesPast five minutes, the per-minute rate sets the price of a copyFor copies and downloads the rate is the larger of a flat amount and a per-minute amount, so a track over five minutes costs more per copy than a shorter one.IntermediateSourcedAs of 19 November 2026

For 2026 the larger of two figures applies: a flat 13.1 cents, or 2.52 cents multiplied by the minutes, counting any part of a minute as a whole one. A track of exactly five minutes works out to 12.6 cents by the minute formula, so the 13.1 cent rate wins. A track of 5:01 counts as six minutes, which gives 15.12 cents. This is arithmetic on the published rates, not a separate rule.

  • Round each track up to whole minutes before comparing the two amounts

37 CFR 385.11 · Copyright Royalty Board 2025

RatesThe physical and download rate is adjusted every January for inflationThe Judges publish a cost-of-living notice at least 25 days before 1 January. It indexes base rates of 12 cents and 2.31 cents to the CPI-U published in November 2022.IntermediateSourcedAs of 19 November 2026

The 2026 notice used a CPI-U of 324.800 against the November 2022 base of 298.012, which gave 13.1 cents and 2.52 cents. It notes that the Bureau of Labor Statistics could not collect October 2025 data during a lapse in appropriations, so the index published on 24 October 2025 was used. On 19 November 2026 the regulation still showed the 2026 rate, so no 2027 figure is given here.

  • Look for the next Federal Register notice from the Copyright Royalty Board in December

37 CFR 385.11 · Copyright Royalty Board 2025

RatesStreaming mechanicals are worked out in four steps from a poolTake the greater of the revenue and content-cost prongs, subtract performance royalties, apply any floor, then split the pool across plays.AdvancedSourcedAs of 19 November 2026

37 CFR 385.21(b) sets out the steps for each offering: the all-in royalty, the deduction of performance royalties the service pays, the payable pool after any floor, and the per-work share. Mixed service bundles use the content-cost prong alone. The MLC does the last allocation. For that split only, a play of a track over five minutes counts extra: 1.2 plays for 5:01 to 6:00, plus 0.2 for each further minute or part of one.

  • Read the regulation itself for any Offering-specific term

37 CFR 385.21

RatesReading 13.1 cents as what a stream paysThe 13.1 cent figure is for physical copies and downloads. Streaming mechanicals come from a pool formula, not a fixed amount per play.MistakeBeginnerSourcedAs of 19 November 2026

Subpart B sets a per-work rate for each physical copy or permanent download. Subpart C works out a royalty pool for each streaming offering, then divides it across plays. What one stream earns depends on the service, the offering and the pool, so no single per-stream figure appears in the regulation.

  • Use the Subpart B rate only for copies and downloads

The MLCThe MLC does not take a fee from mechanical royaltiesThe MLC says digital services fund its operating budget and that it takes no fees out of the mechanical royalties it pays.BeginnerSourcedAs of 19 November 2026

The MLC's audits page says its budget is paid by an assessment that the Copyright Royalty Board sets, separate from the royalties the services owe, and that it deducts no fees from royalties. Registration and matchingThe MLC database is free to search, but its ownership data can be wrong or partialPublic search is free by law, and bulk and API access must be offered too. The MLC warns that listed ownership may be incomplete or out of date.IntermediateSourcedAs of 19 November 2026

After release · Registration and matchingClaim your share or register the work, then add your detailsJoin the MLC, use the Claiming Tool for songs that exist, use the Works Registration Tools for songs that do not, and keep banking and tax details current.StepBeginnerSourced

The MLC says connecting is free. Its tools page lists individual, bulk and CWR registration for new works, and says banking details and tax forms live in Member Settings. MLC: tools · MLC: Missing Member Lookup

Every year · Registration and matchingCheck the Matching Tool for recordings not yet tied to your songsUnmatched recordings can earn royalties the MLC holds. Propose matches in the Matching Tool, and look again, as new usage data arrives continuously.StepIntermediateSourced

The MLC says it matches by both automated and manual work and that what it cannot match shows up in the Matching Tool, which sits in the Member Hub and also in the Songwriter Hub. Members propose matches between a recording and a registered song, and once the MLC approves one, the member receives the royalties accrued for those uses. Historical unmatched data can also be searched there.

  • Search the Matching Tool by your own song and artist names
  • Re-check after each new release or catalogue change

Unclaimed moneyApril 2027 is when market-share distributions begin, one month at a timeThe MLC plans to start in April 2027 with January 2021 usage, then take one more month of usage each month. It is a start date, not a cut-off.IntermediateSourcedAs of 19 November 2026

The MLC's September 2026 release says it will allocate each month's remainder song by song, pro rata, separately for each DSP and offering, with interest at the statutory rate. The June 2026 one-sheet adds that it will keep reprocessing unmatched royalties into regular distributions. These are the MLC's plans; the schedule can change.

  • Watch the MLC's unclaimed dashboard for the monthly figures
  • Register and match your songs before your usage months come up

MLC: unclaimed accrued royalties · TMLC 2026b · TMLC 2026a

Unclaimed moneyClaiming before a month's royalties are distributed gets you the accrued amountIf an owner is found before the accrued royalties are included in a market-share distribution, the MLC pays them to that owner with interest.IntermediateSourcedAs of 19 November 2026

17 USC 115(d)(3)(I) says that when an owner is located for an unmatched work, or a share of one, the MLC updates its records and, if the royalties have not yet gone into an unclaimed-royalties distribution, pays them with proportionate interest and a cumulative statement. The text does not say what happens to a claim after that point, so ask a music lawyer if an old amount matters.

  • Claim and match songs well ahead of any distribution

17 USC 115

Unclaimed moneyThe MLC must hold unmatched royalties for at least three years firstThe statute sets a three-year minimum hold, in an interest-bearing account, before unmatched money can go into a market-share distribution.IntermediateSourced

The hold runs from the date of receipt, or from the date a service accrued the money before transferring it, whichever ends sooner. The account earns interest each month at the federal short-term rate for the benefit of the owners. A longer hold is allowed; the statute sets only the minimum.

17 USC 115

Unclaimed moneyPublishers must pass a share of unclaimed distributions to songwritersOwners who receive an unclaimed distribution must pay or credit songwriters by reported usage, and never under half of what the owner receives for that songwriter's works.AdvancedSourcedAs of 19 November 2026

17 USC 115(d)(3)(J)(iv) applies despite contrary terms in an agreement. The allocation follows each song's reported usage in the periods covered, and the floor is 50 percent of the payment the owner receives attributable to that songwriter's works. Contract terms otherwise apply, so a music lawyer should read any agreement.

  • Ask your publisher how it will credit these payments

Unclaimed moneyThe Copyright Office recommended holding unclaimed money longer than the minimumThe Office's July 2021 report recommends no first distribution until at least five years after claiming was fully available. AuditsThe MLC can audit digital services, at most once in any three calendar yearsAn audit covers up to three full calendar years, starts with a notice filed at the Copyright Office, and is done by an independent qualified auditor.AdvancedSourcedAs of 19 November 2026

Under 17 USC 115(d)(4)(D) the MLC pays for the audit unless the auditor finds an underpayment of at least 10 percent, in which case the service bears the reasonable costs. The MLC says it will pass on recovered royalties in full and mark audit adjustments on members' statements. Owners can audit the MLC too, at their own cost, once a year, covering up to the three preceding calendar years.

  • Look for adjustments marked as audit results on your MLC statement
