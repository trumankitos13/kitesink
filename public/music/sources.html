Where this comes from.

How entries are graded and all 102 sources, from statutes and rulings to the collecting bodies' own documents. Last updated 20 November 2026.

General information on how music rights and royalties work, not legal advice. For a contract, a termination notice or a dispute, talk to a music lawyer.

GradingAll sources 102
Grading

How entries are graded.

Each entry records one of four grades and shows one of two labels. An entry takes the grade of its weakest load-bearing source.

Sourced
The law, or the responsible body's own document, states it.
Law. A statute, regulation, court decision or Copyright Royalty Board determination states it.
Official. The responsible body's own published document states it: the MLC, a PRO or society, SoundExchange, the Copyright Office, CISAC, DDEX, IFPI or ISO.
Directional
Trade press, a secondary source, an estimate or common practice. A good guide, not a settled fact.
Reported. Trade press or a credible secondary source only, or a figure its source calls an estimate.
Industry practice. Common industry convention with no direct source.
Sources

Articles and trade press are credited where they are the source of a point. An entry that rests on them alone is graded Directional. Every entry is written in this guide's own words.
