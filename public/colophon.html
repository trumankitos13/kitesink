How it's built.

A short note on the materials. KiteSink is small and hand-made on purpose — this is what it's made of and why.

01Static by hand

Every page is hand-written static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript — no framework, no build step, no bundler. What you read is what's on disk. It keeps the whole thing legible, fast to ship, and easy to reason about a year from now.

02Type

Set in Geist for display and body, and Geist Mono for labels and small print, both served from Google Fonts. One family carries the voice of the workshop — with Newsreader, a serif, stepping in on the music pages (Song of the Day, the listening club).

03Shared chrome

The header, footer, and stylesheet live in exactly one place. /ks.js injects the shared nav and footer into placeholders on every page, and /ks.css holds the reset, the accessibility defaults, and the link hovers. Change the chrome once, and it changes everywhere.

04Hosting

Served on Cloudflare via Workers static assets — files pushed to the edge, close to wherever you are. No origin server to babysit, no moving parts between you and the page.

05From prototype to page

Designs start life as in-house “Design Component” (.dc) prototypes — a way to settle the look before committing to code — then get rebuilt by hand into the static pages you're reading. The prototype is the sketch; the page is the real thing.

06Accessibility & security

Baked in, not bolted on. The site honors prefers-reduced-motion and keeps a consistent focus ring for keyboard users, and it ships with security headers — including a Content Security Policy — so the defaults are sane before anyone has to think about them.

Made by

Built by one person — that's me, Truman — with an AI pair at the keyboard alongside me. It's how a workshop of one covers the surface area of a small team without cutting corners on the craft.
