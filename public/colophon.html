How it's built.

A short note on the materials. KiteSink is small and hand-made on purpose — this is what it's made of and why.

01Static by hand

Every page is plain static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript — no framework, no bundler. All but one are written by hand. The Fitness page is the exception: a small script writes it from data files, because it holds a few hundred sourced entries. What you read is what's on disk. It keeps the whole thing legible, fast to ship, and easy to reason about a year from now.

02Type

Set in Geist for display and body, and Geist Mono for labels and small print, both served from Google Fonts. One family carries the voice of the workshop. The project pages keep their own type: Newsreader on Song of the Day, Fraunces on the Listening Club, Space Grotesk and JetBrains Mono on Vantix, Space Grotesk and Space Mono on Stage First, Bitter and Oswald on Campground Tycoon.

03Shared chrome

The header, footer, and stylesheet live in exactly one place. /ks.js injects the nav and footer into placeholders on the core pages and a slim KiteSink bar on several project pages, and /ks.css holds the design tokens, the accessibility defaults, and the shared components. One more file, /site-index.js, lists every page on the site; the Find palette and the Library both read it.

04Hosting

Served on Cloudflare via Workers static assets — files pushed to the edge, close to wherever you are. No origin server to babysit, no moving parts between you and the page.

05From prototype to page

The first pages started life as in-house “Design Component” (.dc) prototypes — a way to settle the look before committing to code — then were rebuilt by hand into static pages. The newer pages were designed directly in code. Either way the prototype is the sketch; the page is the real thing.

06Accessibility & security

Baked in, not bolted on. The site honors prefers-reduced-motion, keeps a consistent focus ring for keyboard users, and matches your system's light or dark theme out of the box — flip it yourself and the site remembers. It ships with security headers — including a Content Security Policy — so the defaults are sane before anyone has to think about them.

07Checked before it ships

A small audit reads every page before a change goes out: titles and descriptions, links, the site index, the sitemap, and whether the Fitness page is older than its data. The calculators have their own tests, with the maths checked against values worked by hand.

08Plain text for AI readers

The main pages have a plain-text twin, listed at /llms.txt, so an assistant can read the facts without the layout. A twin says the same things as its page.

Made by

Built by one person — that's me, Truman — with an AI pair at the keyboard alongside me. It's how a workshop of one covers the surface area of a small team without cutting corners on the craft.
