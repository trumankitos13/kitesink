    A starting estimate. Resting energy starts from KiteSink’s own working numbers: 10, 9 or 8 kcal per lb of body weight by body fat category, with day levels of × 1.1 to 1.5 and training added in the activity multiplier. The published equations are there to choose instead; the best of them lands within 10% of measured resting energy for most people, not all. Run it for two or three weeks, watch the trend on the scale, then adjust. {{c:mifflin1990,frankenfield2005,cunningham1991,fao2004}}

    “Effective reps” is a coaching shorthand: the idea that the reps closest to failure do most of the work. Drag the sliders to see how a set scores under it, then tally a session.

        For sets of up to ten reps the estimate is the average of two published equations, Epley’s and Brzycki’s, shown above; past ten it is Epley’s alone, and the two meet at ten. A set stopped short of failure counts its reps in reserve as reps. Every load on this card inverts that same estimate, so the numbers agree with each other. Both equations work best from sets of ten reps or fewer, and common equations have under-estimated the deadlift. {{c:brzycki1993,reynolds2006,lesuer1997}}

                  These are KiteSink’s own working numbers from coaching, not results from a study. Resting energy comes from My numbers, worked out the way Energy & macros is set to (by default 10, 9 or 8 kcal per lb of body weight, by body fat category). A usual day is × 1.1 to 1.5, and each hour of training adds 0.1 to 0.4. Use the answer as a starting point and correct it against your weight trend over two or three weeks.

                  What the plan has to cover

                    An estimate to start from. Loss is not linear: the first week or two moves more, mostly water and stored carbohydrate, and the 7,700 kcal per kg rule overstates loss over months. Watch the trend for two or three weeks and adjust maintenance to match. At the same weekly deficit, a few big diet days lost about as much as a small deficit every day in trials, though more people dropped out of alternate-day fasting. How protein climbs between its two ends is a KiteSink convention. {{c:mifflin1990,cunningham1991,fao2004,trepanowski-2017,harvie-2011,nice-2025,hall-2007,helms-2014-protein,jager-2017,nunes-2022,helms-2023,fao2010,who2003,lieberman-2018}}
